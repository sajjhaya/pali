## Answers: Word Formation

**Sotānugata — met by ear.**

1. Sāmaññañca vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi, sāmaññaphalāni ca. Taṁ suṇātha. Katamañca, bhikkhave, sāmaññaṁ? (SN 45.35 — S v 25)

    The base is *samaṇo* 'ascetic' and the suffix is the secondary *-ya*. Two changes happen at once, and item 18 asks for them again cold: the first vowel is strengthened, *sa-* to *sā-*, and the *y* assimilates to the preceding *ṇ* to give *-ñña*. So the word is filed under *sā-* and its base under *sa-*, and without the rule there is no way to get from the one to the other. DPD writes the construction as `samaṇa + *ya`, with the asterisk marking the suffix that strengthens, the same notation that separates *kāra* from *kara*.

    There are two bases, not one, and several numbered senses under each. Beside the *sāmañña* built on *samaṇo* (Sanskrit *śrāmaṇya*), DPD carries a *sāmañña* built on *samāno* 'same, equal', Sanskrit *sāmānya*, meaning 'similarity, accord, agreement', and it is canonical Pāli too: DPD illustrates it from the suttas, the Sandaka Sutta among them. The front vuddhi has taken *sama-* to *sāma-* and *samaṇa-* to *sāmañña-*, and the two arrive at the same spelling from different words.

    Here the sentence settles it, not the word. The Buddha is addressing monks; the second noun is *sāmaññaphalāni*, and the fruits he goes on to name at SN 45.35 — S v 25 are the four stages of awakening; and the definition at 1.5 is the noble eightfold path. The morphology cannot decide it and the context decides it at once: the chapter's whole position in one word.

2. Asevanā ca bālānaṁ, paṇḍitānañca sevanā; Pūjā ca pūjaneyyānaṁ, etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ. (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 46)

    Three formations in four lines. *Sevanā* and *asevanā* are one action noun in *-anā* used twice, the second time with the negative particle, and the verse's whole shape is that opposition. *Pūjā* is the same kind of noun made with a bare *-ā*. *Pūjaneyyānaṁ* is the future passive participle in *-aneyya*, 'to be honoured', declined as an ordinary *-a* stem in the genitive plural.

    What the verse gains by building its opposition out of one noun and a particle is that the two halves are the same act, and only the *a-* separates them. Two different nouns would have made two different subjects; one noun negated makes association itself the thing at issue, and leaves the choice of company as the only variable. The verse argues about one act, and it does so without a verb.

3. Patirūpadesavāso ca, pubbe ca katapuññatā; Attasammāpaṇidhi ca, etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ. (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 46)

    *-tā* has been added to *katapuñña*, a finished compound adjective, not a stem: 'who has made merit', *kata* + *puñña*, and DPD lists it as an adjective in its own right and gives the abstract as *katapuñña* + *tā*. That is the property of *-tā* the chapter singles out: it attaches to things much larger than a stem, to a compound as readily as to a noun, and English does the same with *-ness*.

    There is no vuddhi, for the reason item 27 gives about a different suffix. The front strengthening belongs to the *ṇ*-marked *taddhita* affixes (*ṇa*, *ṇeyya*, *ṇika*, *ṇiya*, *ṇaka*, *ṇya*), and *-tā* is not among them. *Kata-* stays *kata-*; contrast *bāhusaccaṁ* in item 4, where *-ya* is one of them and the base is unrecognisable afterwards. Two secondary suffixes on two compounds, two verses apart, and only one of them moves the front of the word. That teaches better than the rule stated abstractly.

    Two smaller things in the verse. *Paṇidhi* is an action noun in *-i* (DPD's *pa* + *ni* + √*dhā* + *i*), a type the chapter's four do not include, and a reader will meet it in *sandhi* and *vidhi* and *upadhi*. And the first word is spelled with a dental: *patirūpa-*, not *paṭirūpa-*. That is an orthographic question and was settled against the editions, not against a corpus, as it must be whenever the disagreement is about spelling; all three romanised editions print the dental in their running text, and only the PTS prints the retroflex, and once.

4. Bāhusaccañca sippañca, vinayo ca susikkhito; (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 47)

    *Bāhusaccaṁ* is the chapter in one word. The base is a compound, *bahussuto* 'one who has heard much'; the suffix is *-ya*; the first vowel is strengthened, *ba-* to *bā-*; and the *y* assimilates to the preceding dental to give *-cca*. Nothing of *suta* is visible in the result. *Susikkhito* shows *su-* used as an ordinary prefix on a past participle.

5. Mātāpitu upaṭṭhānaṁ, puttadārassa saṅgaho; (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 47)

    Two action nouns on prefixed roots, one with *-ana* and one with the bare *-a*, and both with the prefix in a form the reader has to undo: *upa-* is transparent, *saṁ-* has become *saṅ-* before the guttural.

**Vacasā paricitā — rehearsed aloud.**

6. svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opaneyyiko paccattaṁ veditabbo viññūhī'ti. (AN 6.10 — A iii 285)

    **Only *opaneyyiko* moves.** Its base is the prefix *upa-*, filed under *u-*; the word is filed under *o-*. The other three are looked up under the letter their base begins with, and from those three alone no one would discover that there was a rule.

    - ***Sandiṭṭhiko***: under *sa-*, and its base *sandiṭṭhi* is under *sa-*. The first vowel is long by position, a cluster following it, and a vowel long by position is not strengthened. That is the same exception that leaves *paṇḍiccaṁ* unstrengthened in item 25.
    - ***Akāliko***: under *a-*, and its base *akāla* is under *a-*. The first vowel is the negative particle, which the chapter says does not take the strengthening; the canon has *akāliko* and not *ākāliko*.
    - ***Ehipassiko***: under *e-*, and its base *ehi* is under *e-*. The first element is an imperative, not a stem, and in any case *e* is where the ladder for *i* ends: there is no step above it.
    - ***Opaneyyiko***: under *o-*, and its base *upa-* is under *u-*. This demonstrates the whole of the chapter's practical claim inside a formula most readers already know by heart.

    Item 28 takes the same word further, to the *-eyya-* that Warder's resolution leaves out.

7. 'Idaṁ dukkhaṁ ariyasaccan'ti me, bhikkhave, pubbe ananussutesu dhammesu cakkhuṁ udapādi, ñāṇaṁ udapādi, paññā udapādi, vijjā udapādi, āloko udapādi. (SN 56.11 — S v 422)

    Four of the five nouns are derivatives on prefixed or bare roots, and *ñāṇaṁ* and *paññā* are two suffixes on the **same** root, a small demonstration of what item 13 says. *Udapādi* shows where the augment goes in a prefixed verb: between the prefix and the stem, not at the front of the word.

8. "Cattārome, bhikkhave, iddhipādā bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā honti mahānisaṁsā". (SN 51.20 — S v 276)

    *Bahulīkatā* is the formation treated in the chapter's section on verbs made from nouns and adverbs: a noun or adjective takes a final *-ī* and is compounded with a form of √*kar* or √*bhū*. It is a fixed phrase behaving as one word, neither an ordinary compound nor a denominative verb. The verb *bahulīkaroti* occurs 134 times in the root texts, almost always in this pairing with *bhāvitā*.

**Manasānupekkhitā — gone over in the mind.**

**Recall.**

9. ***Kita***, the primary derivation, whose suffixes are added **directly to a root**; and ***taddhita***, the secondary derivation, whose suffixes are added to a stem that already exists, an ordinary noun or itself a primary derivative. So √*kar* + *-aka* → *kāraka* is *kita*, and *sīla* + *-vant* → *sīlavant* is *taddhita*. The two names have to be held because every grammar uses them, and the division is softer than it looks: Perniola says outright that it "is not of great importance since the same suffixes may be used both as primary and as secondary".

10. The first syllable of the word, not the syllable next to the suffix. *A* becomes *ā*, *i* becomes *e*, *u* becomes *o*. This is the chapter's whole practical point: the derivative is filed in the dictionary under a different letter from its base. *Nipuṇo* is under *ni-* and *nepuññaṁ* under *ne-*; *upa-* is under *u-* and *opaneyyiko* under *o-*. Without the rule you look in the wrong place and conclude the word is not in the dictionary.

11. It may **modify or intensify** the sense of the root (*dhātvatthavisesako*), it may **reverse** it (*dhātvatthabādhako*), or it may **add nothing at all** (*dhātvatthānuvattako*). The third has to be pointed out, because without a name for it a reader treats every prefix as meaningful and produces glosses the language does not support. Naming the category converts a recurring puzzle into a recognised fact.

12. It is an ***anubandha***, an "exponent": a fictitious letter attached to a suffix to distinguish it from another suffix of the same shape, *ṇ* being chosen because no Pāli word begins with it. It signals **vuddhi**: Kaccāyana calls the *ṇ*-marked affixes the *kārita* affixes because the root or the first syllable is strengthened before them. So a grammar's *ṇa* is a reader's *-a* with strengthening, against a plain *-a* without it, which is the whole difference between *kāra* 'maker' and *kara* 'making'.

13. Any two of three. **First**, words built on one root diverge in sense until nothing connects them: √*ñā* gives *saññā* 'perception' and *viññāṇa* 'consciousness' and *paññā* 'wisdom', and no arithmetic on the prefixes would produce those three senses. **Second**, a prefix often contributes nothing, so a prefixed verb has to be learnt as a word, not assembled; Bodhi's advice is to take the meaning from a dictionary and the nuance from the context. **Third**, a derivative may be identical in shape with its base, as *Gotamo* the clan name is with *Gotamo* the ancestor, so the absence of a visible suffix is not evidence that no derivation happened.

**Identify the formation.**

14. ***-anā***, primary, an action noun: 'feeling' as the *doing* of feeling. DPD's construction is √*vid* > *ved* + *anā*, literally 'causing to know': the suffix goes on the **root**, strengthened, and not on the finished verb *vedeti*. DPD's entry does relate the noun to *vedeti*, 'from *vedeti*', but its construction is built on the root. Deriving the noun from the verb itself is a common habit of teaching, and it claims a derivation nobody has checked.

15. ***-ti***, primary, an action noun, feminine: √*kham* + *ti*, 'patience, endurance'. The root is in its weak grade, so the vowel is short.

16. ***-aka***, primary, an agent noun: 'doer'. DPD writes it `√kar + *aka`, the asterisk marking the strengthening; the tradition writes the same suffix *ṇvu*, its *ṇ* saying the same. The strengthening separates *kāraka* from *kara*.

17. ***-mant***, secondary, possessive: 'having eyes'. This is the nominative singular; the stem is *cakkhumant* and a dictionary lists it that way, the usual mismatch between how a grammar cites a word and how a text prints it. The trap here is the direction of the derivation: a possessive in *-mant* or *-vant* is built on a *noun*, whereas a present participle in *-ant* is built on a present stem, and the *-m-* or *-v-* is the only visible difference.

18. ***-ya***, secondary, abstract: 'the ascetic life, the state of being a *samaṇa*'. Two changes happened at once: the first vowel was strengthened, *sa-* to *sā-*, and the *y* assimilated to the preceding *ṇ* to give *-ñña*. Neither change is optional and together they leave nothing of *samaṇo* visible on the surface.

19. **Nothing.** *Ācariyaka* is *ācariya* 'teacher' with a *-ka* that does not change the meaning at all. **This is the trap**, the commonest one in the language: the pleonastic *-ka*. There is no test for it, and the working rule is that when a *-ka* word will not read as a diminutive or an agent, it is probably the plain word. DPD analyses the whole thing back to *ā* + √*car* + *iya* + *ka*, which is accurate and no help at the point of reading.

20. ***-ka***, contemptuous: 'shaveling'. This is the same suffix as in item 19 and as in *puttaka* 'little son', in its third sense. The passage decides whether a given *-ka* is affectionate, dismissive or empty, not the word. The trap is thinking the suffix decides.

21. ***-ya***, secondary, abstract, with vuddhi: 'skill', from *nipuṇo* 'skilful'. DPD's construction is `ni > ne + √puṇ + ya + *a`, and the `ni > ne` is the front strengthening written out. This is the word the chapter opens with, because looking it up is the whole lesson.

22. ***-ika***, secondary, relational, with vuddhi: 'belonging to the ordinary person', from *puthujjano*. The *pu-* has become *po-*. Note that the base is itself a compound, *puthu* + *jana*, so the suffix operates on a finished compound. That is the *-ika* Chapter 13 treats, there and not here because it works on a compound, not on a stem.

23. ***-ū***, primary, an agent noun: 'gone to the far shore'. The trap is that it cannot stand alone. *Pāragū* is *pāra* + *gū*, where *gū* is a bound form of √*gam* that occurs only at the end of a compound and is in no dictionary as a word. The same is true of *-ga*, *-ja*, *-da*, *-ññū* and the rest of the family Chapter 13 listed and this chapter accounts for.

24. ***-ti***, primary, an action noun, feminine: 'attainment', DPD's *pa* + √*ap* + *ti*. The trap is that it looks like a third-person singular verb, and in the nominative singular nothing on the word distinguishes the two. Any oblique case declares itself (*pattiyā*, *pattiṁ*), so only the bare nominative has to be read from the sentence, a good reason to be suspicious of a "verb" that has no subject and sits in a list of nouns.

**Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — penetrated by view.**

**Build the form.**

25. ***Paṇḍiccaṁ***. Two changes, and they are independent of each other. The final vowel of the base is elided before the suffix, which is Duroiselle's general rule for *taddhita*; and the *y* of *-ya* assimilates to the preceding dental, giving *-cca*. There is no vuddhi here, because the first vowel of *paṇḍita* is already long by position: a cluster follows it. DPD gives the construction as simply *paṇḍita* + *ya*, with Sanskrit *pāṇḍitya* alongside, and the Sanskrit shows the vuddhi that Pāli has traded for the doubled consonant.

26. ***Gāravo***. The root is in its ***vuddhi*** grade: DPD gives `√gar + u + *a`, so the base is *garu* and the suffix is the strengthening *-a*. **Note that the base is *garu* and not *guru***: the *u*-vowel Sanskrit has in the first syllable is not what Pāli prints, and looking for a canonical *guru* returns the grammarians' texts and the later literature only.

27. ***Vicikicchī***, with **no** strengthening, not *vecikicchī*. Six root-text occurrences against one. **This is the trap**: a common teaching box on "vowel lengthening" gives *vicikicchā* + *in* → *vecikicchin* as an example of the rule. What the result shows is that the rule of item 10 belongs to the *ṇ*-marked *taddhita* affixes and not to every suffix. The possessive *-in* is not one of them, and neither are the primary suffixes, which strengthen the root instead if they strengthen anything.

28. The canon has ***opaneyyiko***, fifty-five times in the root texts; *opanayiko* occurs once, in the Visuddhimagga. A second suffix that Warder's resolution leaves out accounts for it: DPD gives `upa + √nī + *eyya + *ika`, so there is an *-eyya-* between the root and the *-ika*. His rule, front strengthening, *upa-* to *opa-*, is right and produces the right first syllable; the ending is a step short. The moral is the chapter's, drawn twice: a source's rules and a source's specimen forms are different things, and the corpus adjudicates the second.

29. ***Ponobhaviko***, or in the feminine plural *ponobhavikā* as the canon most often prints it. The **first** vowel changed, *pu-* to *po-*; the *-a-* of *-bhava-* did not, because the strengthening falls on the first syllable of the word and nowhere else. That is the same rule as item 10 and this is the item that shows what "first syllable" excludes.

**Read the sentence.**

30. - "Yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, santiṭṭhāmi tadāssu saṁsīdāmi; yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, āyūhāmi tadāssu nibbuyhāmi. (SN 1.1 — S i 1)
    - *yadā* when · *kho* indeed · *ahaṁ* I, the *o* of *kho* becoming the semivowel of *khvāhaṁ* · *āvuso* friend · *santiṭṭhāmi* < *saṁ-* + √*ṭhā* — I stand still · *tadā* then · *assu* indeed · *saṁsīdāmi* < *saṁ-* + √*sad* — I sink · *āyūhāmi* < *ā-* + √*yūh* — I strive, I swim · *nibbuyhāmi* < *nī-* + √*vah* + *-ya-* (passive) — I am swept away
    - When I stood still, friend, then I sank; when I struggled, then I was swept away.

    Four verbs and four prefixes, and the sentence rests on them: the roots are 'stand', 'sit', 'strive' and 'carry', and what makes the pairs into opposites is what sits in front of each. *Nibbuyhāmi* is also a passive (DPD gives *nī* + √*vah* + *ya* + *ti*), so the speaker is carried, not carrying, and that is the point of the simile.

**Put it back into Pāli.**

31. Bāhusaccañca sippañca, vinayo ca susikkhito (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 47). If you produced *bahussutañca* you have written good Pāli but not this line; the exercise is the abstract in *-ya*, and the verse has *bāhusaccaṁ*.

32. **Yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, santiṭṭhāmi tadāssu saṁsīdāmi; yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, āyūhāmi tadāssu nibbuyhāmi** (SN 1.1). The four prefixes are *saṁ-*, *saṁ-*, *ā-* and *nī-*. Getting the roots right and the prefixes wrong is the ordinary failure here, the failure the chapter predicts in the other direction.

**Read at length.**

33. **The reading.**

    - ***Abhi-***, both times, with a different sense in each. In *abhikkantāya rattiyā* the participle means 'far gone, advanced': the night was well on. In *abhikkantavaṇṇā* it means 'surpassing, excellent': the devatā's beauty was outstanding. DPD lists both senses under one headword and gives the literal sense as 'gone forward'. One prefix, one root, two senses that a reader cannot get from the parts, which is item 13 again in the first sentence of the sutta.
    - ***Upa-* then *saṁ-*, on √*kam***: DPD's construction is *upa* + *saṁ* + √*kam* + *a* + *ti*. The *saṁ-* has assimilated to the following guttural and is written *-saṅ-*. Two prefixes on one root is ordinary, and three is not rare.
    - *Santiṭṭhāmi* and *saṁsīdāmi*, *āyūhāmi* and *nibbuyhāmi*; then *appatiṭṭhaṁ* and *anāyūhaṁ*. The prefixes are *saṁ-*, *saṁ-*, *ā-*, *nī-*, and then the negative *a-*/*an-* on top of *paṭi-* and *ā-*. Note that the negated pair are **not** the same words negated: *appatiṭṭhaṁ* is built on *paṭi-* + √*ṭhā*, not on *saṁ-* + √*ṭhā*, and *anāyūhaṁ* is a present participle, not a first-person verb. The Buddha answers in a different formation from the one the question was posed in.
    - *Tattha* + *eva* + *antaradhāyi* + *ti*, 'right there she vanished'. No, *antara* is not a prefix; it is a noun, 'interval', standing unchanged in front of the verb. This is the last section of the chapter: an adverb or noun compounded with a verb to make a fixed expression, alongside *pātubhavati* and *purakkharoti*.

**Reaching back.**

34. Because a compound and a derivative are different operations and a word may undergo both. *Ehi* + *passa* is the compound, two second-person imperatives, and *-ika* is the suffix added to the finished compound to make an adjective of it; DPD's construction, *ehi* + *passa* + *ika*, contains both. The *-o* is neither chapter's: it is the masculine nominative singular ending of an *-a* stem, agreeing with *dhammo*, and it belongs to Chapter 3. Three layers of morphology in one familiar word.

35. *Paṇītataro* is built on the adjective *paṇīta* 'excellent'; *uttaritaro* is built on *uttara*, which is itself the comparative of the prefix *ud-* 'up'. So *uttaritaro* is a comparative of a comparative, and the difference shows in the extra syllable, *uttari-* instead of *uttara-*. Chapter 4 records three more of the same kind, *seyyataraṁ*, *pāpiṭṭhataraṁ* and *purimatarāni*, and draws the same moral: the suffix had stopped being felt as a suffix, so it could be added again.

36. Only the vowel of the suffix. *Diṭṭha* is √*dis* + *-ta*, a past participle, 'seen'; *diṭṭhi* is √*dis* + *-ti*, an action noun, 'view'. The root has behaved identically in both, with the same assimilation of *s* + *t* to *ṭṭh*, so the only signal is *-a* against *-i*. So a bare *-ti* form in a verse can be confused with a third-person singular verb, and the resolution is syntactic: look for a subject, and look at the functions of the other words in the line. A noun in a list of nouns is a noun.

37. The prefix is ***nī-***, which the grammars also write *nir-* and which has a consonant to contribute: the point of Warder's bracketed notation *u(d)*, *du(r)*. Before *v* it gives *bb* (*nī-* + √*vā* → *nibbāna*, *nī-* + √*vid* → *nibbindati*), and before *k* it gives *kkh*, *nī-* + √*kam* → *nikkhamati*, with the aspiration that the cluster produces. It is one process with three outputs, and a reader needs it because *nikkhamati* is not filed under *kh*.

**Say it aloud.**

38. **Three derivatives, three suffixes.** ***Aṭṭhaṅgiko*** is *-ika* on *aṭṭha* + *aṅga*, secondary, relational: 'having eight factors'. ***Virāgo*** is the strengthening *-a* on √*raj* with the prefix *vi-*, primary, an action noun: 'fading away'. And ***cakkhumā*** is *-mant*, secondary, possessive: 'the one with eyes', which is item 17 again, now standing on its own as a noun and meaning the Buddha. *Seṭṭho* deserves a glance too: it is an irregular superlative and belongs to Chapter 4, not here.

    Read the verse aloud and the geminates carry most of the metre: *-ṭṭh-* twice, *-cc-* in *saccānaṁ*, *-ṭṭh-* again. Holding them makes the line scan.

**One root, three formations.**

39. *nissajjitabbaṁ* is to be relinquished (nt. nom. sg., fpp.) · *saṅghassa* to the Sangha (m. dat. sg.) · *vā* or · *gaṇassa* to a group (m. dat. sg.) · *vā* or · *puggalassa* to an individual (m. dat. sg.) · *vā* or

    "It is to be relinquished to the Sangha, or to a group, or to an individual."

    All three words are built on *nissajjati* 'lets go, relinquishes', which is itself *nis-* 'out, away' prefixed to the root √*sajj*. The prefix's *s* has assimilated to the following consonant, so the words show *-ss-* and not *-s-s-*.

    - ***Nissajjitabbaṁ*** is the **gerundive**, the *-tabba* suffix on the stem with a connecting *-i-*. It is a verbal adjective and it declines like one; here it is neuter nominative singular, agreeing with the robe-cloth understood from the sentence before. Its sense is obligation: the thing is to be relinquished.
    - ***Nissaggiya*** is an adjective built on the action, and the whole class of offence is named after it: *nissaggiya pācittiya*, an offence entailing relinquishment. The formation is on the root's other grade, *sagga-*, not *sajj-*, with the *-iya* suffix.
    - ***Nissaṭṭha*** is the **past participle**, the *-ta* suffix straight onto the root with the cluster assimilating to *-ṭṭh-*. It reports the act as done: *nissaṭṭhaṁ*, 'has been relinquished'.

    The three between them cover obligation, class and completion, which a rule needs: what must happen, what kind of offence it is, and that it has happened. A root in Pāli is a supply of words more than a word, and the Vinaya draws on the supply harder than the suttas do because a procedure has to name each stage of itself.
