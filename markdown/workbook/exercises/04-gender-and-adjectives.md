## Exercises: Gender and Adjectives

An adjective has no endings of its own. Everything it does it borrows, and the whole skill this chapter asks for is reading that borrowing backwards: given an adjective, what does its ending tell you about the noun it belongs to? That is more useful than it sounds, because it is often the adjective, not the noun, that resolves an ambiguous phrase.

Agreement is easier to hear than to work out, so the first stage gives whole lines with nothing withheld. The last stage takes up comparison, which the chapter treats as the second defining property of an adjective. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — gender and adjectives

Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear one stem take different endings from the words around it.

1. Dīghā jāgarato ratti, dīghaṁ santassa yojanaṁ; Dīgho bālānaṁ saṁsāro, saddhammaṁ avijānataṁ. (Dhp 60 — Dhp p. 9)

    *dīghā* long (f. nom. sg.) · *jāgarato* for one lying awake (m. gen. sg., pres. part.) · *ratti* the night (f. nom. sg.) · *dīghaṁ* long (n. nom. sg.) · *santassa* for the weary (m. gen. sg., p.p.) · *yojanaṁ* a league (n. nom. sg.) · *dīgho* long (m. nom. sg.) · *bālānaṁ* for fools (m. gen. pl.) · *saṁsāro* wandering on (m. nom. sg.) · *saddhammaṁ* the true teaching (m. acc. sg.) · *avijānataṁ* of those not understanding (m. gen. pl., pres. part.)

    "Long is the night for one lying awake; long is the league for the weary; long is the wandering on for fools who do not understand the true teaching."

    Say it aloud, one *pāda* at a time. One adjective, three genders, three lines, and the stem never changes. Say the three forms of it in order without looking, and then say which noun gave each its ending.

2. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, bālo abyatto akusalo sūdo rājānaṁ vā rājamahāmattaṁ vā nānaccayehi sūpehi paccupaṭṭhito assa. (SN 47.8 — S v 149)

    Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, paṇḍito byatto kusalo sūdo rājānaṁ vā rājamahāmattaṁ vā nānaccayehi sūpehi paccupaṭṭhito assa. (SN 47.8 — S v 151)

    *seyyathāpi* suppose · *bhikkhave* mendicants (m. voc. pl.) · *bālo* foolish · *abyatto* incompetent · *akusalo* unskilful · *paṇḍito* wise · *byatto* competent · *kusalo* skilful · *sūdo* a cook (m. nom. sg.) · *rājānaṁ* a king (m. acc. sg.) · *vā* or · *rājamahāmattaṁ* a royal minister (m. acc. sg.) · *nānaccayehi* of various kinds (m. inst. pl.) · *sūpehi* with sauces (m. inst. pl.) · *paccupaṭṭhito* waiting on, attending (m. nom. sg., p.p.) · *assa* were to be (3 sg. opt.)

    "Suppose, mendicants, a foolish, incompetent, unskilful cook were to wait on a king or a royal minister with sauces of various kinds."

    "Suppose, mendicants, a wise, competent, skilful cook were to wait on a king or a royal minister with sauces of various kinds."

    The same sentence twice, four sections of one sutta apart, with three adjectives turned round. Say both aloud. Six adjectives and one noun, and every one of the six carries the same three letters at the end. Then say what the second and the fifth have in common that the others do not.

3. Tena kho pana samayena sukkā bhikkhunī mahatiyā parisāya parivutā dhammaṁ deseti. (SN 10.9 — S i 212)

    *tena kho pana samayena* now at that time · *sukkā* Sukkā (f. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhunī* the nun (f. nom. sg.) · *mahatiyā* large (f. inst. sg.) · *parisāya* by an assembly (f. inst. sg.) · *parivutā* surrounded (f. nom. sg., p.p.) · *dhammaṁ* the Dhamma (m. acc. sg.) · *deseti* she teaches

    "Now at that time the nun Sukkā was teaching the Dhamma surrounded by a large assembly."

    Five feminines in a row, and they are not all in one case. Say it aloud, then say which two of the five are not nominative, and how you know; and say why *parivutā*, which stands at the far end of the sentence from the word it describes, is nominative and not instrumental.

4. Yo pana bhikkhu atirekacīvaraṁ dhāreyya, nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan'ti. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 195)

    Yā pana bhikkhunī pattasannicayaṁ kareyya, nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan'ti. (Bi Np 1 — Vin iv 243)

    *yo* whoever (m. nom. sg.) · *yā* whoever (f. nom. sg.) · *pana* moreover · *bhikkhu* a monk (m. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhunī* a nun (f. nom. sg.) · *atirekacīvaraṁ* a surplus robe (n. acc. sg.) · *dhāreyya* should keep (3 sg. opt.) · *pattasannicayaṁ* a stash of bowls (m. acc. sg.) · *kareyya* should make (3 sg. opt.) · *nissaggiyaṁ* entailing relinquishment (n. nom. sg.) · *pācittiyaṁ* an offence entailing confession (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* (quotation)

    "Whatever monk should keep a surplus robe: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession."

    "Whatever nun should make a stash of bowls: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession."

    The monks' code and the nuns' code, built on one frame. Say the two openings one after the other. Two words change and the rest does not. Say which two, and then say why the four words that close both rules did not have to move.

### Vacasā paricitā — gender and adjectives

Recite each line until you can say it without looking. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing the endings is the work; recognising them is not.

5. Tāvadeva chāyā metabbā, utuppamāṇaṁ ācikkhitabbaṁ, divasabhāgo ācikkhitabbo, saṅgīti ācikkhitabbā, cattāro nissayā ācikkhitabbā. (Kd 1 — Vin i 95)

    *tāvadeva* straightaway · *chāyā* the shadow (f. nom. sg.) · *metabbā* is to be measured (f. nom. sg.) · *utuppamāṇaṁ* the date (n. nom. sg.) · *ācikkhitabbaṁ* is to be told (n. nom. sg.) · *divasabhāgo* the part of the day (m. nom. sg.) · *ācikkhitabbo* (m. nom. sg.) · *saṅgīti* the recitation (f. nom. sg.) · *ācikkhitabbā* (f. nom. sg.) · *cattāro nissayā* the four supports (m. nom. pl.) · *ācikkhitabbā* (m. nom. pl.)

    "Straightaway the shadow is to be measured, the date told, the part of the day told, the recitation told, and the four supports told."

    This is what a newly ordained monk is told in the first minutes after his ordination. One word takes four different endings in one sentence, and rebuilding it from the gloss means producing all four. Do that before reading on. Then say which two of the four are spelled alike, and what in the sentence (not in the word) decides between them.

6. Sahassamapi ce vācā, anatthapadasaṁhitā; Ekaṁ atthapadaṁ seyyo, yaṁ sutvā upasammati. (Dhp 100 — Dhp p. 15)

    *sahassaṁ* a thousand (n. nom. sg.) · *api* even · *ce* if · *vācā* words (f. nom. pl.) · *anatthapadasaṁhitā* not connected with a meaningful saying (f. nom. pl.) · *ekaṁ* one (n. nom. sg.) · *atthapadaṁ* a meaningful saying (n. nom. sg.) · *seyyo* better · *yaṁ* which (n. acc. sg.) · *sutvā* having heard (absol.) · *upasammati* one is calmed

    "Though there be a thousand words unconnected with meaning, better is one meaningful saying, hearing which one is calmed."

    Recite it, then rebuild it from the gloss. This is the chapter's comparison table in a verse, and *seyyo*, alone of its forms, cannot be derived from anything: it has to be known. When the line is fluent, say what its positive degree is according to the chapter's table of irregulars.
