## Exercises: The Past and the Future

The chapter's four aorist types are one habit to acquire, not four rules to memorise: look for the augment, then ask what is left. So the third stage gives you six aorists cold, and the fourth gives you three of them standing together in one paragraph of narrative, as they are actually met.

Between the two lies the point the chapter argues hardest: a tense in Pāli does not always do what its name suggests. A future can state a general law, an aorist can carry a prohibition, and the conditional exists chiefly to say what did not happen. The first two stages put that in front of you before any of it is named, because these are forms a reader has already heard hundreds of times without parsing one. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — the past and the future

Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear what the tense does.

1. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. (MN 10 — M i 63)

    *idam·avoca* < *idaṁ* this (n. acc. sg.) + *avoca* said (3 sg. aor.) · *bhagavā* the Blessed One (m. nom. sg.) · *attamanā* satisfied (m. nom. pl.) · *te* those (m. nom. pl.) · *bhikkhū* monks (m. nom. pl.) · *bhagavato* of the Blessed One (m. gen. sg.) · *bhāsitaṁ* what was said (n. acc. sg., p.p.) · *abhinanduṁ* they approved (3 pl. aor.) · *ti* (quotation)

    "This is what the Blessed One said. Satisfied, those monks approved what the Blessed One had said."

    The last sentence of most of the suttas in the canon, so a reader has heard it more often than any other sentence in the language and has probably never parsed a word of it. Say it aloud. Both its finite verbs are aorists and neither looks like the present of its own verb. Separate the first word into the two it is made of, and then say which two present-tense verbs the two aorists belong to.

2. Gahakāraka diṭṭhosi, puna gehaṁ na kāhasi; Sabbā te phāsukā bhaggā, gahakūṭaṁ visaṅkhataṁ; Visaṅkhāragataṁ cittaṁ, taṇhānaṁ khayamajjhagā. (Dhp 154 — Dhp p. 23)

    *gahakāraka* house-builder (m. voc. sg.) · *diṭṭho·si* < *diṭṭho* seen (m. nom. sg., p.p.) + *asi* you are (2 sg. pres.) · *puna* again · *gehaṁ* house (n. acc. sg.) · *na* not · *kāhasi* you will build (2 sg. fut.) · *sabbā* all (f. nom. pl.) · *te* your (2 sg. gen.) · *phāsukā* rafters (f. nom. pl.) · *bhaggā* broken (f. nom. pl., p.p.) · *gahakūṭaṁ* roof-peak (n. nom. sg.) · *visaṅkhataṁ* demolished (n. nom. sg., p.p.) · *visaṅkhāragataṁ* gone to the unconditioned (n. nom. sg.) · *cittaṁ* mind (n. nom. sg.) · *taṇhānaṁ* of cravings (f. gen. pl.) · *khayam·ajjhagā* < *khayaṁ* the ending (m. acc. sg.) + *ajjhagā* has reached (3 sg.)

    "House-builder, you are seen! You will not build a house again. All your rafters are broken, your roof-peak demolished. The mind has gone to the unconditioned; it has reached the ending of cravings."

    The verse tradition gives to the Buddha at the moment of awakening. Say it aloud. Three of this chapter's topics stand in six lines: a present tense hidden in a sandhi, a future that has no *-ss-* in it anywhere, and a past whose augment you can see with the naked eye. Find all three. The last of them is *ajjhagā*, and its prefix is *adhi-*: say where the augment went.

3. Māvamaññetha pāpassa, na mantaṁ āgamissati; Udabindunipātena, udakumbhopi pūrati; Bālo pūrati pāpassa, thokaṁ thokampi ācinaṁ. (Dhp 121 — Dhp p. 18)

    *mā* do not · *avamaññetha* let one think lightly (3 sg. opt. middle) · *pāpassa* of evil (n. gen. sg.) · *na* not · *man·taṁ* < *maṁ* to me (1 sg. acc.) + *taṁ* that (n. nom. sg.) · *āgamissati* will come (3 sg. fut.) · *uda·bindu·nipātena* by the falling of drops of water (m. inst. sg.) · *udakumbho* the water-pot (m. nom. sg.) · *api* even · *pūrati* is filled (3 sg. pres.) · *bālo* the fool (m. nom. sg.) · *thokaṁ thokaṁ* little by little · *api* even · *ācinaṁ* accumulating (m. nom. sg., pres. part.)

    "Do not think lightly of evil, saying 'it will not come to me'. By the falling of drops of water even a water-pot is filled; the fool is filled with evil, accumulating it little by little."

    Say it aloud. The verse has one prohibition and one future, and neither does what its name says. The prohibition is not on the aorist that the chapter's *mā* section leads you to expect; say what mood it is on instead, and what difference that makes to how hard it pushes. And the future is not the verse's own prediction. It is the content of the complacent thought being denied. Say who, in the verse, makes that prediction.

4. Etaṁ satiṁ adhiṭṭheyya, Brahmametaṁ vihāramidhamāhu. (Snp 1.8 — Sn p. 26)

    *etaṁ* this (f. acc. sg.) · *satiṁ* mindfulness (f. acc. sg.) · *adhiṭṭheyya* should undertake (3 sg. opt.) · *brahmam·etaṁ* < *brahmaṁ* divine (m. acc. sg.) + *etaṁ* this (m. acc. sg.) · *vihāram·idham·āhu* < *vihāraṁ* abiding (m. acc. sg.) + *idha* here + *āhu* they say (3 pl. perf.)

    "One should undertake this mindfulness. This, they say, is the divine abiding here."

    Two more lines of the Mettasutta, so this too is met by ear before it is met on a page. Say them aloud. One finite verb belongs to the last chapter and one to this one, and only the second is rare: *āhu* is one of a bare handful of forms surviving from a tense Pāli otherwise lost altogether. Name the tense. Then say what present-tense verb a reader would go looking for on the strength of it, and why they would not find one.

### Vacasā paricitā — the past and the future

Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing a past tense is the work; recognising one is not.

5. Anekajātisaṁsāraṁ, sandhāvissaṁ anibbisaṁ; Gahakāraṁ gavesanto, dukkhā jāti punappunaṁ. (Dhp 153 — Dhp p. 23)

    *aneka·jāti·saṁsāraṁ* through the wandering-on of many births (m. acc. sg.) · *sandhāvissaṁ* I ran on (1 sg. aor.) · *anibbisaṁ* not finding (m. nom. sg., pres. part.) · *gahakāraṁ* the house-builder (m. acc. sg.) · *gavesanto* seeking (m. nom. sg., pres. part.) · *dukkhā* painful (f. nom. sg.) · *jāti* birth (f. nom. sg.) · *punappunaṁ* again and again

    "Through the wandering-on of many births I have run, not finding, seeking the builder of this house — painful is birth again and again."

    The verse before item 2, and the two are one utterance. Rebuild this one from the gloss. Two of its words end in *-aṁ* and neither is what that ending would suggest, which is the whole difficulty of the line: one has the ending the chapter's conditional table gives for the first person singular and is an aorist, and the other looks like a first person singular and is not a finite verb at all. Say which is which before you look at the answer.

6. Tena kho pana samayena aññataro bhikkhu divā bhaṇḍaṁ passitvā nimittaṁ akāsi—"rattiṁ avaharissāmī"ti. So taṁ maññamāno attano bhaṇḍaṁ avahari. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 57)

    *tena kho pana samayena* now at that time · *aññataro* a certain (m. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhu* monk (m. nom. sg.) · *divā* by day · *bhaṇḍaṁ* goods (n. acc. sg.) · *passitvā* having seen (absol.) · *nimittaṁ* a mental note (n. acc. sg.) · *akāsi* made (3 sg. aor.) · *rattiṁ* at night (f. acc. sg.) · *avaharissāmi* I will steal (1 sg. fut.) · *ti* (quotation) · *so* he (m. nom. sg.) · *taṁ* that (n. acc. sg.) · *maññamāno* thinking it to be (m. nom. sg., pres. part. middle) · *attano* his own (m. gen. sg.) · *bhaṇḍaṁ* goods (n. acc. sg.) · *avahari* stole (3 sg. aor.)

    "Now at that time a certain monk saw some goods by day and made a mental note: 'I'll steal them at night.' Thinking it was that, he stole his own goods."

    A case from the rule against stealing, and one of the few in the Vinaya that is funny. Rebuild it from the gloss. The same verb stands here twice, once in each tense this chapter is about. Say which two forms they are, and say what the difference between them costs the monk. Then say what *akāsi* is, and why someone who knows only *karoti* will not recognise it.

### Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — the past and the future

**Decide what the form does.** Each of these sentences turns on a point this chapter makes. Say what the marked verb is, and what it does in the sentence, which is not always what its tense would suggest.

18. Mā bhāyi, vakkali. (SN 22.87 — S iii 122)

19. Na vatimāni manussabhūtassa padāni bhavissantī'ti. (AN 4.36 — A ii 37)

20. Atthi, kassapa, maggo atthi paṭipadā, yathāpaṭipanno sāmaṁyeva ñassati sāmaṁ dakkhati. (DN 8 — D i 165)

**Read the passage.** Gloss it word by word, then render it, then answer the question that follows.

21. No cedaṁ, bhikkhave, paṇḍito sucintitacintī ca abhavissa subhāsitabhāsī ca sukatakammakārī ca, kena naṁ paṇḍitā jāneyyuṁ. (AN 3.3 — A i 102) — Two different moods are at work here, one in each clause. Which is in which, and why that way round?

22. Atha kho te bhikkhū sāvatthiyaṁ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṁ piṇḍapātapaṭikkantā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdiṁsu. Ekamantaṁ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṁ etadavocuṁ. (AN 10.27 — A v 49) — There are three finite verbs here and they are not all the same type of aorist. Sort them, and say what that mix teaches about how to approach an unfamiliar past tense.

**Read the case law.** The Vibhaṅga records what happened, decides it, and moves on. Its narrative is the plainest prose in the canon and it is almost entirely aorist, which makes it the best place to practise one.

23. Tena kho pana samayena aññataro bhikkhu ajjhokāse cīvaraṁ pattharitvā vihāraṁ pāvisi. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 57) — There is one finite verb. Name it, say what it comes from, and find its augment. The chapter's rule about where an augment goes when a verb has a prefix will get you most of the way; the last step needs a dictionary.

24. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū gijjhakūṭā pabbatā orohantā sīhavighāsaṁ passitvā pacāpetvā paribhuñjiṁsu. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 58) — Gloss it, then render it. Four verb forms stand here and only one of them is finite: say what the other three are, and what fixes the time of the whole sentence. One of the three is a causative, and the monks' innocence depends on it.

**What happened next.** Item 23 leaves a robe on the ground and a monk indoors. A few cases later the Vibhaṅga runs the same situation again with a bowl, and this is how that one goes.

25. Aññataro bhikkhu—"māyaṁ patto nassī"ti paṭisāmesi. So nikkhamitvā taṁ bhikkhuṁ pucchi—"āvuso, mayhaṁ patto kena avahaṭo"ti? So evamāha—"mayā avahaṭo"ti. So taṁ ādiyi, "assamaṇosi tvan"ti. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 57)

    Gloss it and render it. Four finite verbs stand here, and between them they cover most of this chapter. Three are aorists of three different shapes; the fourth is not an aorist at all and belongs to the tense item 4 introduced. Sort them. Then find the *mā* construction in the first sentence, say what verb form it governs, and say why the sense here is a wish, not a prohibition. The chapter says the two shade into each other, and here they do.

**Put it back into Pāli.** Every item so far has run from a printed form to the tense behind it. Now run it the other way. Each phrase below occurs in a line already in this set. Give the Pāli from memory, naming the tense and the person and number of the verb, and only then look back at the line it came from.

### Coming back — the past and the future

The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

**Day 3.** Items 1–4 aloud, and 7–12. The four types are the chapter, and no amount of reading about them substitutes for having six in the mouth.

**Day 10.** Items 13–17 and 25. If the four verbs in 25 sort themselves without the chapter open, the aorist has stopped being a table.

**Day 30.** Items 5, 6 and 26. If (c) and (d) in 26 still come out as two different tenses of one verb, the future and the past have separated where they are hardest to keep apart.
