## Exercises: Indeclinables and Particles

Every other exercise set in this book can drill a form by asking you to build it. This one cannot, because an indeclinable has nothing to build: it falls into the language whole, which is what *nipāta* means. So the work here is recognition, not formation: knowing which job a small word has, and noticing when a word you have met as a case form has a different one. For the same reason the chapter had to check its forms against the canon directly, and why several items below turn on whether a form is printed at all.

Stage 1 is four short sentences in which the small words carry everything, which is the fastest way to stop skimming them. Every Pāli line is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — indeclinables

Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear which words join, separate, deny and fill.

1. Tesaṁ divā ca ratto ca, sadā puññaṁ pavaḍḍhati. (SN 1.47 — S i 33)

    *tesaṁ* for them (m. gen. pl.) · *divā* by day (adv.) · *ca* and · *ratto* by night (adv.) · *ca* and · *sadā* constantly · *puññaṁ* merit (n. nom. sg.) · *pavaḍḍhati* grows

    "For them, by day and by night, merit constantly grows."

    Say it aloud. Two of these words look like case forms and are not behaving as any case. Find them, name the case each was, and then say why the pair does not have to agree with each other or with anything else in the line.

2. Ruppatī'ti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā 'rūpa'nti vuccati. (SN 22.79 — S iii 86)

    *ruppati* it is deformed · *ti* (particle) · *kho* indeed · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *tasmā* therefore · *rūpaṁ* 'form' (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* (particle) · *vuccati* it is called

    "Because it is deformed, monks, it is therefore called 'form'."

    Set 09 took this sentence for its two passives. Say it aloud again and listen to the two *iti*s instead. They have different jobs, and only one of them is what a reader has been taught to call a quotation mark. Name both jobs, and say which word in the sentence answers to the first of them; if you read the first *iti* as a quotation, that word is left answering to nothing.

3. Kiṁ nu kho, āvuso gotama, taṁ jīvaṁ taṁ sarīraṁ, udāhu aññaṁ jīvaṁ aññaṁ sarīran'ti? (DN 7 — D i 159)

    *kiṁ* what · *nu* (question) · *kho* indeed · *āvuso gotama* friend Gotama (voc.) · *taṁ* that (n. nom. sg.) · *jīvaṁ* soul (n. nom. sg.) · *sarīraṁ* body (n. nom. sg.) · *udāhu* or · *aññaṁ* other (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* (quotation)

    "Is the soul the same as the body, friend Gotama, or is the soul one thing and the body another?"

    Say it aloud. Two particles between them make this a question with only two permitted answers. Name both. Then notice what the Buddha does with it. This is one of the undeclared questions, and his refusal to answer is a refusal of the frame these two small words have built.

4. Selo yathā ekaghano, vātena na samīrati; Evaṁ nindāpasaṁsāsu, na samiñjanti paṇḍitā. (Dhp 81 — Dhp p. 12)

    *selo* a rock (m. nom. sg.) · *yathā* just as · *ekaghano* of one solid mass (m. nom. sg.) · *vātena* by the wind (m. inst. sg.) · *na* not · *samīrati* is shaken · *evaṁ* so · *nindā·pasaṁsāsu* amid blame and praise (f. loc. pl.) · *na* not · *samiñjanti* are moved · *paṇḍitā* the wise (m. nom. pl.)

    "As a rock of one solid mass is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are not moved amid blame and praise."

    Say it aloud. Two indeclinables carry the whole simile and there is no verb of comparison anywhere in the verse. Name them, and say what English has to add that Pāli does not. Then say what the two *na*s contribute, and whether the second could have been left out.

### Vacasā paricitā — indeclinables

Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud.

5. Ayaṁ kho me, samma, sāṇabhāro dūrābhato ca susannaddho ca, alaṁ me tvaṁ pajānāhī'ti. (DN 23 — D ii 351)

    *ayaṁ* this (m. nom. sg.) · *kho* indeed · *me* my (1 sg. gen.) · *samma* friend (voc.) · *sāṇabhāro* load of hemp (m. nom. sg.) · *dūrābhato* brought from afar (m. nom. sg., p.p.) · *ca* and · *susannaddho* well tied up (m. nom. sg., p.p.) · *ca* and · *alaṁ* enough · *me* for me (1 sg. dat.) · *tvaṁ* you (nom. sg.) · *pajānāhi* know! (2 sg. imper.) · *ti* (quotation)

    "'This hemp load of mine, friend, was brought from far off and is well tied up; it is enough for me — you see to yourself.'"

    Rebuild it from the gloss. The second half is a refusal, and the refusing is done by one syllable. Say which, and say what it conveys that "enough" does not quite capture in English.

6. "Na sūpaṁ vā byañjanaṁ vā odanena paṭicchādessāmi bhiyyokamyataṁ upādāyā"ti sikkhā karaṇīyā. (Bu Sk 36 — Vin iv 192)

    *na* not · *sūpaṁ* curry (m. acc. sg.) · *vā* or · *byañjanaṁ* condiment (n. acc. sg.) · *vā* or · *odanena* with rice (m. inst. sg.) · *paṭicchādessāmi* I shall cover (1 sg. fut.) · *bhiyyokamyataṁ* wanting more (f. acc. sg.) · *upādāya* on account of (absol. as postposition) · *ti* (quotation) · *sikkhā* the training (f. nom. sg.) · *karaṇīyā* is to be done (f. nom. sg., gerundive)

    "'I shall not cover curry or condiment with rice out of wanting more' — this is a training to be done."

    One of the seventy-five *sekhiya* rules, all built to this pattern and all recited fortnightly. Rebuild it from the gloss. Three of the Saddanīti's four jobs for a particle are done here. Name each particle and the job it does, and say which job is missing and why a rule of this shape has no use for it. Then account for the *ā* at the end of *upādāyā*, which is not part of the word.

7. The tradition sorts the *nipātā* by the job each does, not by part of speech. Name the four jobs, in Pāli or in English, and give one specimen of each.

8. Two of the reference grammars this book relies on most have no section on indeclinables at all. Say why. The reason is about how those grammars are organised, not about the words.

### Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — indeclinables

**Read the sentence.** Gloss it, render it, and answer the question.

27. Nanu tvaṁ sālavatikaṁ ajjhāvasasī'ti? (DN 12 — D i 227) — what answer does the questioner expect, and what in the sentence shows it?

28. Kudāssu nāma imassa dukkhassa nissaraṇaṁ paññāyissati jarāmaraṇassā'ti? (SN 12.10 — S ii 10) — one word here contains a particle that never stands alone in the root texts. Find it, separate it out, and say what the whole word would look like if the two were printed apart.

**Put it back into Pāli.** The answer is a particular line of canon, and getting close to it is the exercise.

29. "Do not speak so, friend Sāti; do not misrepresent the Blessed One." (Watch the mood.)

30. "Come now, monks, I address you." (Four words in the Pāli, three of them indeclinable.)

**Read at length.**

31. AN 3.65 — A i 189, the Kālāmas' instruction, in one passage:

    Etha tumhe, kālāmā, mā anussavena, mā paramparāya, mā itikirāya, mā piṭakasampadānena, mā takkahetu, mā nayahetu, mā ākāraparivitakkena, mā diṭṭhinijjhānakkhantiyā, mā bhabbarūpatāya, mā samaṇo no garūti.

    Read it through twice without stopping to parse. Then:
    - Count the *mā*s. This chapter taught *mā* with the aorist; how many aorists are in this passage, and what does *mā* govern here instead?
    - Give the case of *anussavena*, *paramparāya* and *ākāraparivitakkena*, and say what that case is contributing.
    - *Takkahetu* and *nayahetu* break the pattern of the others. How?
    - The last item, *mā samaṇo no garū'ti*, breaks it completely: it is a whole clause with a finite verb understood and an *iti* closing it. Translate it, and say what the *iti* marks.
    - What is gained by repeating *mā* ten times instead of writing it once and listing the rest?

**Reaching back.**

32. *Chapter 8.* The aorist is the past tense. In item 29 above it is not. State the rule that governs which of the two readings applies, and say what would happen to the sentence if the particle were removed.

33. *Chapter 5.* The pronoun chapter taught *tasmā*, *tato* and *tahiṁ* as oblique forms of *ta(d)* hardened into adverbs. This chapter taught *tato* as a *-to* adverb and *tasmā* as a frozen ablative. These are not two competing accounts. Say what each is describing, and why both can be true of the same word.

34. *Chapter 4.* The adjectives chapter taught that Pāli makes an adverb from the accusative singular. What does this chapter add to that, and which single sentence quoted in this chapter shows both an adverb-by-suffix and an adverb-by-accusative side by side?

35. *Chapter 11.* Both chapters teach *paṭicca*, *nissāya* and *uddissa*. What does each chapter contribute that the other does not?

**Say it aloud.**

36. Dhp 1 — Dhp p. 1, the opening verse of the Dhammapada:

    Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;\
    Manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;\
    Tato naṁ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṁva vahato padaṁ.

    Read it five times slowly, holding the long vowels and sounding the geminates in *paduṭṭhena* and *cakkaṁva*. Four indeclinables carry the whole logic of the verse: *ce* sets the condition, *vā … vā* offers the two ways it may be met, *tato* draws the consequence, and *va* makes the simile. Notice that not one of them inflects, and that without them the six lines are a list of nouns. Set 07 read this verse for its present tenses; this is what was left when the verbs were accounted for.

### Coming back — indeclinables

The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

**Day 3.** Items 7–11 and 12–21. The four jobs and the three traps go first, because recognition is the whole skill in this chapter and there is no form to fall back on.

**Day 10.** Items 22–26 and 31. If the ten *mā*s in 31 still resolve without the chapter open, the particle's job has separated from the construction it is usually taught in.

**Day 30.** Items 1–4 aloud, and 29–30. If item 30 comes out in four words, the difference between a word that inflects and a word that does not has stopped being theoretical.
