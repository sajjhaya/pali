## Exercises: Numerals

The pronoun chapter's exercises began with isolated forms because its thesis was that a pronoun must be identified, not merely recognised. This chapter's thesis is different: the forms are the easy half, and what has to be worked out is how a numeral attaches to what it counts. So the third stage here matters most, and the first two exist to make it possible.

The first stage is arranged around the four constructions, not around the numbers, because a reader has to be able to hear them. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — numerals

Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear what the numeral is attached to.

1. Ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. (SN 10.9 — S i 212)

    *ekaṁ* one, a certain (m. acc. sg.) · *samayaṁ* occasion, time (m. acc. sg.) · *bhagavā* the Blessed One (m. nom. sg.) · *rājagahe* at Rājagaha (n. loc. sg.) · *viharati* dwells · *veḷuvane* in the Bamboo Grove (n. loc. sg.) · *kalandakanivāpe* at the squirrels' feeding ground (m. loc. sg.)

    "At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha, in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels' feeding ground."

    This is the numeral you have heard most often and have probably never counted with. Say it aloud. *Eka* here agrees with *samayaṁ* as an adjective does, and it is not really counting anything; it does what English does with "a". Then say which of the four constructions this is, and whether you would have called it a numeral at all had it not been printed under this heading.

2. Tena kho pana samayena aññatitthiyā paribbājakā cātuddase pannarase aṭṭhamiyā ca pakkhassa sannipatitvā dhammaṁ bhāsanti. (Kd 2 — Vin i 101)

    *tena kho pana samayena* now at that time · *aññatitthiyā* of other sects (m. nom. pl.) · *paribbājakā* wanderers (m. nom. pl.) · *cātuddase* on the fourteenth (m. loc. sg.) · *pannarase* on the fifteenth (loc. sg., used as an adverb) · *aṭṭhamiyā* on the eighth (f. loc. sg.) · *ca* and · *pakkhassa* of the fortnight (m. gen. sg.) · *sannipatitvā* having assembled (absol.) · *dhammaṁ* the teaching (m. acc. sg.) · *bhāsanti* they speak

    "Now at that time wanderers of other sects would assemble on the fourteenth, the fifteenth and the eighth of the fortnight and speak on the teaching."

    The Buddha's fortnightly observance was instituted in answer to this passage, so here the uposatha days enter the canon. Say it aloud. Three ordinals in a row, and they are not all of one gender. Say which one is the odd one out, and hold the answer until item 15 asks you for it again.

3. Abhivādanasīlissa, niccaṁ vuḍḍhāpacāyino; Cattāro dhammā vaḍḍhanti, āyu vaṇṇo sukhaṁ balaṁ. (Dhp 109 — Dhp p. 16)

    *abhivādanasīlissa* for one whose habit is to bow (m. gen. sg.) · *niccaṁ* constantly · *vuḍḍhāpacāyino* honouring elders (m. gen. sg.) · *cattāro* four (m. nom. pl.) · *dhammā* things (m. nom. pl.) · *vaḍḍhanti* increase · *āyu* long life · *vaṇṇo* beauty · *sukhaṁ* happiness · *balaṁ* strength

    "For one in the habit of bowing, always honouring the elders, four things increase: long life, beauty, happiness and strength."

    Say it aloud, and then say the four things without looking. The numeral and its noun share case, number and gender, which is the construction most readers assume is the only one. Item 4 is what happens when it is not.

4. Tasmiṁ sahassadhā loke sahassaṁ candānaṁ sahassaṁ sūriyānaṁ … cattāri mahāsamuddasahassāni … sahassaṁ brahmalokānaṁ. (AN 10.29 — A v 59)

    *tasmiṁ* in that (m. loc. sg.) · *sahassadhā* thousandfold · *loke* world (m. loc. sg.) · *sahassaṁ* a thousand (n. nom. sg.) · *candānaṁ* of moons (m. gen. pl.) · *sūriyānaṁ* of suns (m. gen. pl.) · *cattāri* four (n. nom. pl.) · *mahāsamuddasahassāni* thousands of great oceans (n. nom. pl.) · *brahmalokānaṁ* of realms of divinity (m. gen. pl.)

    "In that thousandfold world there are a thousand moons, a thousand suns … four thousand great oceans … a thousand realms of divinity."

    The full passage says *sahassaṁ* fourteen times, and it is a list built to be recited. Say it. Nothing here agrees with anything: *sahassaṁ* is a neuter noun and what it counts stands in the genitive plural beside it, so the phrase is literally "a thousand of moons". Then find the place in the line where the construction changes, and say what changed and why it had to.

### Vacasā paricitā — numerals

Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing a numeral compound is the work; recognising one is not.

5. Niṭṭhitacīvarasmiṁ bhikkhunā ubbhatasmiṁ kathine dasāhaparamaṁ atirekacīvaraṁ dhāretabbaṁ. Taṁ atikkāmayato nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan'ti. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196)

    *niṭṭhitacīvarasmiṁ* when the robe is finished (n. loc. sg.) · *bhikkhunā* by a monk (m. inst. sg.) · *ubbhatasmiṁ* when it has been withdrawn (n. loc. sg., p.p.) · *kathine* the kathina (n. loc. sg.) · *dasāhaparamaṁ* for ten days at most (adv., acc. sg.) · *atirekacīvaraṁ* a surplus robe (n. acc. sg.) · *dhāretabbaṁ* may be kept (n. nom. sg., gerundive) · *taṁ* that (n. acc. sg.) · *atikkāmayato* for one exceeding it (m. gen. sg., pres. part.) · *nissaggiyaṁ* entailing relinquishment · *pācittiyaṁ* an offence entailing confession · *ti* (quotation)

    "When a monk's robe is finished and the kathina has been withdrawn, a surplus robe may be kept for ten days at most. For one exceeding that: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession."

    A rule recited fortnightly by every community of monks, and the whole of it turns on one word. Rebuild it from the gloss, and expect *dasāhaparamaṁ* to be the word that will not come. Then say how many parts that word has, and what case it is in. Having answered that, say why a word in that case is being translated as "for ten days at most" and not as the object of anything.

6. Māse māse sahassena, yo yajetha sataṁ samaṁ; Ekañca bhāvitattānaṁ, muhuttamapi pūjaye; Sāyeva pūjanā seyyo, yañce vassasataṁ hutaṁ. (Dhp 106 — Dhp p. 16)

    *māse māse* month by month (m. loc. sg., repeated) · *sahassena* with a thousand (n. inst. sg.) · *yo* whoever · *yajetha* should sacrifice (opt.) · *sataṁ* a hundred (n. acc. sg.) · *samaṁ* year (f. acc. sg.) · *ekaṁ* one (m. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *bhāvitattānaṁ* one who has developed himself (m. acc. sg.) · *muhuttaṁ* for a moment (adv., acc. sg.) · *api* even · *pūjaye* should honour (opt.) · *sā* that · *eva* just · *pūjanā* honouring (f. nom. sg.) · *seyyo* better · *yaṁ* than which · *ce* if · *vassasataṁ* a hundred years (n. acc. sg.) · *hutaṁ* offering (n. nom. sg., p.p.)

    "Though month after month for a hundred years one should sacrifice with a thousand, and another honour for a single moment one who has developed himself — that honouring is better than a hundred years of offerings."

    Six numeral expressions in six lines, and no two of them work the same way. Rebuild it from the gloss and then set them out: which is a distributive, which a plain instrumental, which an accusative of duration, which an ordinary agreeing adjective, and which two are compounds. Item 16 takes the first two lines apart in detail; this stage asks only that the whole verse be in your mouth first.

The same three days, twice, in two genders. The chapter quotes the Bodhisatta naming the nights he chose to sit out in the forest.

19. Cātuddasī pañcadasī aṭṭhamī ca pakkhassa. (MN 4 — M i 20) — Set this beside item 2 and give the gender and case of all six day-names across the two passages. Then say why the third day behaves differently from the other two.
