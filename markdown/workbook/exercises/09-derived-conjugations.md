## Exercises: Passive, Causative and Denominative

This set is longer than the ones before it, because five formations have to be told apart and two of the five are traps. The four stages do what they have done since the first set, but the weight has moved: stage 3 is the heart of this one, since a formation you cannot name from memory is one you will not spot in a sentence, and stage 4 is unusually long because reading at length is what all the rest is for.

Stage 1 is four lines a reader has almost certainly already heard, two of them carrying the plainest passives in the canon and two of them carrying causatives nobody thinks of as causatives. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — derived conjugations

Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear which stem carries the meaning.

1. ayaṁ vuccati jarā. (SN 12.2 — S ii 2)

    *ayaṁ* this (f. nom. sg.) · *vuccati* is called (3 sg. pres. pass.) · *jarā* old age (f. nom. sg.)

    "This is called old age."

    Three words, and the middle one is a passive with an active ending. Say it aloud. This is the formula that closes every term in the analysis of dependent origination, so anyone who has been through that analysis once has heard it twelve times in a row. Say what in the form shows it is passive and, having failed to find anything, say where the information actually is.

2. Ruppatī'ti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā 'rūpa'nti vuccati. (SN 22.79 — S iii 86)

    *ruppati* is afflicted (3 sg. pres. pass.) · *ti* (quotation) · *kho* indeed · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *tasmā* therefore · *rūpaṁ* 'form' (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *vuccati* it is called (3 sg. pres. pass.)

    "'It is afflicted', monks — that is why it is called 'form'."

    Say it aloud. Both finite verbs here are passives, and one of them is the word you were listening for while the other is not. Find the second. Then say why the doctrine in this sentence would not work if the verb were active.

3. Mettañca sabbalokasmi, Mānasaṁ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṁ; Uddhaṁ adho ca tiriyañca, Asambādhaṁ averamasapattaṁ. (Snp 1.8 — Sn p. 26)

    *mettaṁ* loving-kindness (f. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *sabbalokasmi* in the whole world (m. loc. sg.) · *mānasaṁ* heart, mind (n. acc. sg.) · *bhāvaye* should develop (3 sg. opt., causative) · *aparimāṇaṁ* boundless (n. acc. sg.) · *uddhaṁ* above · *adho* below · *tiriyaṁ* across · *asambādhaṁ* unobstructed (n. acc. sg.) · *avera·masapattaṁ* < *averaṁ* without hatred (n. acc. sg.) + *asapattaṁ* without enmity (n. acc. sg.)

    "And towards the whole world one should develop a boundless heart of loving-kindness — above, below, across, unobstructed, without hatred, without enmity."

    A third helping of the Mettasutta, which by now should be coming without the page. Say it aloud. The verb is a causative and almost nobody notices, because the word it has become is one of the commonest in the vocabulary of practice. Say what verb it is built on, and then say what the causative literally does to that verb's meaning, and whether any of that literal sense survives in the line.

4. Sabbadā ve sukhaṁ seti, brāhmaṇo parinibbuto; Yo na limpati kāmesu, sītibhūto nirūpadhi. (SN 10.8 — S i 212)

    *sabbadā* always · *ve* indeed · *sukhaṁ* at ease (adv.) · *seti* sleeps (3 sg. pres.) · *brāhmaṇo* the brahmin (m. nom. sg.) · *parinibbuto* fully quenched (m. nom. sg., p.p.) · *yo* who (m. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *limpati* clings, is smeared (3 sg. pres.) · *kāmesu* in sensual pleasures (m. loc. pl.) · *sītibhūto* become cool (m. nom. sg.) · *nirūpadhi* free of attachment (m. nom. sg.)

    "A brahmin fully quenched always sleeps at ease — one who does not cling to sensual pleasures, cooled, without attachment."

    The Buddha's answer to the question that closes item 30. Read it aloud five times, slowly, holding the long vowels for two beats and sounding both halves of every doubled consonant. Then say what you notice about where the long syllables fall. Then answer: not one word in this verse is a derived stem of any of the five kinds. But one of its two finite verbs raises the chapter's question anyway. Which, and what is the question?

### Vacasā paricitā — derived conjugations

Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Both are stock formulas, which is the point: the canon teaches vocabulary by repetition, and a formula in the mouth is several words learnt at once.

5. Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṁ pāpakānaṁ akusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ anuppādāya chandaṁ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati. (AN 4.13 — A ii 15)

    *idha* here · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *bhikkhu* a monk (m. nom. sg.) · *anuppannānaṁ* unarisen (m. gen. pl.) · *pāpakānaṁ* bad (m. gen. pl.) · *akusalānaṁ* unwholesome (m. gen. pl.) · *dhammānaṁ* of states (m. gen. pl.) · *anuppādāya* for the non-arising (m. dat. sg.) · *chandaṁ* desire (m. acc. sg.) · *janeti* generates (3 sg. pres. caus.) · *vāyamati* strives · *vīriyaṁ* energy (n. acc. sg.) · *ārabhati* arouses · *cittaṁ* the mind (n. acc. sg.) · *paggaṇhāti* exerts · *padahati* endeavours

    "Here, monks, a monk generates desire for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states; he strives, arouses energy, exerts the mind and endeavours."

    The first of the four right efforts. Five finite verbs in a row and only one of them is derived. Rebuild the sentence from the gloss, then say which one, and what it is built on.

6. Idhānanda, bhikkhu sabbasaṅkhāresu aṭṭīyati harāyati jigucchati. (AN 10.60 — A v 111)

    *idha* here · *ānanda* Ānanda (m. voc. sg.) · *bhikkhu* a monk (m. nom. sg.) · *sabbasaṅkhāresu* in all conditioned things (m. loc. pl.) · *aṭṭīyati* is troubled (3 sg. pres.) · *harāyati* is ashamed (3 sg. pres. denom.) · *jigucchati* is disgusted (3 sg. pres. desid.)

    "Here, Ānanda, a monk is troubled, ashamed and disgusted by all conditioned things."

    Rebuild it. Three verbs in a row and three different answers, and so it is here and not in stage 1: one is a denominative, one is one of the five surviving desideratives, and the third is the word the sources cannot agree how to spell. Say which is which.

### Manasānupekkhitā — derived conjugations

Cover the chapter. These are answered from memory or not at all, and this stage is the long one because this is the chapter where naming the formation *is* the skill.

**Recall.** Answer these in a phrase, before opening anything. If one will not come, that is the item to go back to.

7. Which three of the five derived stems are productive, and what does "productive" mean about a category?
8. In Pāli, what carries the difference between an active verb and a passive one: the personal endings, or the stem?
9. Name the three routes by which the passive suffix reaches a stem.
10. How many objects does a causative verb govern, compared with the verb it is built on?
11. The desiderative and the intensive are unproductive. What does that mean you should *do* on meeting one?

**Identify the formation.** For each form: which of the five formations is it, what is it built on, and what does it mean? Two of these are traps; say what the trap is.

### Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — derived conjugations

**Read the sentence.** Gloss word by word, render it, and name the derived stems at work.

25. … Satisambojjhaṅgaṁ tasmiṁ samaye bhikkhu bhāveti, satisambojjhaṅgo tasmiṁ samaye bhikkhuno bhāvanāpāripūriṁ gacchati. (MN 118 — M iii 85)

26. Yo pana bhikkhu pathaviṁ khaṇeyya vā khaṇāpeyya vā, pācittiyan'ti. (Bu Pc 10 — Vin iv 33) — you have met this one in the chapter. Do it again without looking, and say what the rule turns on.

**Put it back into Pāli.** Each of these is the rendering of a real sentence. Reconstruct the Pāli. The words you need are given; what you have to supply is the derived stem and the ending. Do not settle for a sentence that merely works; the answer is a particular line of canon, and getting close to it is the exercise.

27. "This is called death.": *idaṁ*, the verb 'is called', *maraṇa*.

28. "Let the Blessed One teach the Dhamma; let the Holy One teach the Dhamma.": *bhagavā*, *sugata*, *dhamma*, *bhante*, and the causative of *dis-*, in the imperative.

29. "And one should not cherish anything in the world.": *na*, *ca*, *kiñci*, *lokasmiṁ*, and the denominative of *mama*, in the optative middle.

**Read at length.** This is not a drill. Read the following passage through, twice, without stopping to parse: the first time for the build of the sentences, the second for the sense. Then go back and answer the questions under it.

30. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā rattiyā paccūsasamayaṁ paccuṭṭhāya abbhokāse caṅkamati. Addasā kho bhagavā anāthapiṇḍikaṁ gahapatiṁ dūratova āgacchantaṁ. Disvāna caṅkamā orohitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Nisajja kho bhagavā anāthapiṇḍikaṁ gahapatiṁ etadavoca: "ehi, sudattā"ti. Atha kho anāthapiṇḍiko gahapati, nāmena maṁ bhagavā ālapatīti, haṭṭho udaggo tattheva bhagavato pādesu sirasā nipatitvā bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: "kacci, bhante, bhagavā sukhamasayitthā"ti? (SN 10.8 — S i 212)

    - *Caṅkamati* and *caṅkamā* stand in consecutive sentences. What is the difference between them, and what does the second one being a noun tell you about the first?
    - *Orohitvā* is built on the verb that also gives the chapter's causative *voropeti*. Having read this passage, say what *voropeti* literally does to somebody.
    - *Sukhamasayittha* is a verb form Chapter 7 warned you about and Chapter 8 gave you the tense of. What is it, and why is it in this sentence instead of an ordinary *-i* aorist?
    - Nothing here is a passive. Say why not: what is it about this kind of narrative that keeps the passive out of it?

**Reaching back.** Four items reaching over earlier chapters. Each is a place where what you learnt there and what you learnt here have to be held together.

31. Chapter 7 gives *deseti* as its specimen verb of the eighth conjugation class. Chapter 9 gives *deseti* as a causative. Are both true, and if so, what is the relation between the two statements?

32. Chapter 8 taught *bhavissati* 'it will be'. Chapter 9 taught *bhāveti* 'develops'. Both are built on *bhū-*, and neither is the other. Set out how each is formed, and name the single feature that tells them apart on sight.

33. Chapter 6. The verse at SN 10.8 — S i 211, two pages before the passage in item 30, reads Kalaṁ nāgghanti soḷasiṁ. What kind of numeral is *soḷasiṁ*, what is it agreeing with, and what is the idiom saying?

34. Chapter 5. In item 30, Anāthapiṇḍika thinks nāmena maṁ bhagavā ālapati. Why *maṁ* and not the enclitic *me*?

**A causative in the case law.** One sentence from the Vinaya's account of how the rule against theft came to be laid down.

35. Atha kho bhagavā etasmiṁ nidāne etasmiṁ pakaraṇe bhikkhusaṅghaṁ sannipātāpetvā chabbaggiye bhikkhū paṭipucchi. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 46) — Gloss it and render it. One of the two verb forms is a causative: name it, say what it is the causative of, and say what difference it makes to who did what.

### Coming back — derived conjugations

The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.
