## Exercises: Pronouns

The chapter's one recurring claim is that a Pāli pronoun has to be identified, not merely recognised, and this set is built to make that claim expensive to ignore. The first stage gives whole sentences with the pronouns already parsed, because a form met in a sentence is easier to identify later than a form met in a list. The third stage takes the parsing away.

Every Pāli sentence below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

### Sotānugata — pronouns

Nothing is withheld. Each sentence comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear how much of the sentence is pronoun.

1. Tvaṁ, samma medakathālike, mamaṁ rakkha, ahaṁ taṁ rakkhissāmi. (SN 47.19 — S v 168)

    *tvaṁ* you (2nd nom. sg.) · *samma* dear · *medakathālike* Medakathālikā (voc.) · *mamaṁ* me (1st acc. sg.) · *rakkha* protect! · *ahaṁ* I (1st nom. sg.) · *taṁ* you (2nd acc. sg.) · *rakkhissāmi* I shall protect

    "You protect me, dear Medakathālikā, and I shall protect you."

    An acrobat speaking to his assistant on the pole. Say it aloud until the four pronouns come out in the right order without thinking (*tvaṁ … mamaṁ … ahaṁ … taṁ*), because the sentence is a chiasmus and half the work of holding it is rhythmic. Then say which two of the four are first person and which two second, and notice that the two accusatives are the two that look least like their nominatives.

2. Evaṁ me sutaṁ. (DN 1 — D i 1)

    *evaṁ* thus · *me* by me (1st instr. sg.) · *sutaṁ* heard

    "Thus have I heard."

    Three words, and they open almost every sutta in the canon. Say it. Then say what case *me* is in here and, before looking anything up, say whether you had ever thought about that before.

3. Ayaṁ te patto, ayaṁ saṅghāṭi, ayaṁ uttarāsaṅgo, ayaṁ antaravāsako. (Kd 1 — Vin i 94)

    *ayaṁ* this (nom. sg.) · *te* your (2nd gen./dat. sg.) · *patto* bowl (m. nom. sg.) · *saṅghāṭi* outer robe (f. nom. sg.) · *uttarāsaṅgo* upper robe (m. nom. sg.) · *antaravāsako* inner robe (m. nom. sg.)

    "This is your bowl, this your outer robe, this your upper robe, this your inner robe."

    A newly ordained monk being handed his requisites, one at a time. Say it aloud. One word, four times, and it is not agreeing with four words of one gender. Say which of the four nouns is the odd one out, and then say what that shows about the form *ayaṁ*.

4. Ko āpanno, kiṁ āpanno, kismiṁ āpanno, kathaṁ āpanno, kaṁ bhaṇatha, kiṁ bhaṇathā'ti? (Bu Pc 12 — Vin iv 35)

    *ko* who (nom. sg. m.) · *āpanno* has fallen into (an offence) · *kiṁ* what (acc. sg. n.) · *kismiṁ* in what (loc. sg.) · *kathaṁ* how (interrog. adverb) · *kaṁ* whom (acc. sg. m.) · *bhaṇatha* do you speak of · *ti* (quotation)

    "Who has committed an offence? What has he committed? In what has he committed it? How has he committed it? Whom are you speaking of? What are you speaking of?"

    A monk being cross-examined, and one of the plainest displays of the interrogative paradigm anywhere in the canon. Say it aloud as a series: *ko, kiṁ, kismiṁ, kathaṁ, kaṁ, kiṁ*. Then say which one of the six is not a pronoun at all, and which one is built on the chapter's second stem.

### Vacasā paricitā — pronouns

Recite each line until you can say it without looking. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing the enclitic in the right place is the work; recognising it is not.

5. Nagaraṁ yathā paccantaṁ, guttaṁ santarabāhiraṁ; Evaṁ gopetha attānaṁ, khaṇo vo mā upaccagā. (Dhp 315 — Dhp p. 45)

    *nagaraṁ* city · *yathā* as · *paccantaṁ* frontier · *guttaṁ* guarded · *santarabāhiraṁ* within and without · *evaṁ* so · *gopetha* guard! (2nd pl. imv.) · *attānaṁ* yourselves (acc. sg.) · *khaṇo* the moment · *vo* for you (2nd enclitic) · *mā* do not · *upaccagā* let it pass by

    "As a frontier city is guarded within and without, so guard yourselves. Do not let the moment pass you by."

    Two pronouns and neither behaves as English would. When the verse is fluent, say what is odd about the number of *attānaṁ* against the number of *gopetha*, and why that is not a mistake. Then say where in the line *vo* could not have stood, and why.

6. Yo vo, ānanda, mayā dhammo ca vinayo ca desito paññatto, so vo mamaccayena satthā. (DN 16 — D ii 154)

    *yo* which (nom. sg. m.) · *vo* to you (2nd enclitic) · *ānanda* Ānanda · *mayā* by me (1st instr. sg.) · *dhammo* teaching · *ca* and · *vinayo* training · *desito* taught · *paññatto* laid down · *so* that (nom. sg. m.) · *mamaccayena* < *mama* of me (1st gen. sg.) + *accayena* with the passing · *satthā* teacher

    "Ānanda, the teaching and training I have taught and laid down will be your Teacher after I am gone."

    The Buddha's instruction shortly before his death, and there is no sentence in the canon it is more useful to have by heart. Five pronouns in one sentence, in four cases. Rebuild it from the gloss, and expect the two *vo* to be the hard part. Then say what an English translation of *vo* has to throw away.

15. Sabbe tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbesaṁ jīvitaṁ piyaṁ. (Dhp 130 — Dhp p. 19)
16. Ko imaṁ pathaviṁ vicessati, yamalokañca imaṁ sadevakaṁ. (Dhp 44 — Dhp p. 7)
17. Tumhehi kiccamātappaṁ, akkhātāro tathāgatā. (Dhp 276 — Dhp p. 40)
18. Tato naṁ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṁva vahato padaṁ. (Dhp 1 — Dhp p. 1)

A demonstrative welded to a personal pronoun. What the monk says as he hands the robe over.

19. Imāhaṁ saṅghassa nissajjāmi. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196) — Resolve the first word into the two pronouns that made it, give the case and number of each, and say what each one's role is. Then say why looking the verb up in this shape will fail.

Four ways of saying "self", in four lines. This is the hardest reading in the set, and it is here because the verse is about the very thing it is difficult for: a person who talks about himself.

20. Yo attano sīlavatāni jantu, anānupuṭṭhova paresa pāva; Anariyadhammaṁ kusalā tamāhu, yo ātumānaṁ sayameva pāva. (Snp 4.3 — Sn p. 153) — Gloss it and render it. Then identify the three reflexive expressions it holds, say which of the three does not decline, and say why the fourth pronoun in the verse, the one in the second line, has an ending no noun would give it.

**Put it back into Pāli.** Every item so far has run from a printed form to the pronoun behind it. Now run it the other way, which is where an enclitic will catch you out, because producing one means knowing where in the sentence it is allowed to stand. ### Coming back — pronouns

The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

**Day 3.** Items 1–4 aloud, and 12–14. The ambiguities are the first thing to go, and they go unnoticed.

**Day 10.** Items 7–11 with the tables covered, and item 19.

**Day 30.** Items 6, 20 and 21. If 6 still comes out whole, with both *vo* in place, the enclitics have stopped being a list.
