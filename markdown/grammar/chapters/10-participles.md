## Participles

A participle has aspects of both a verb and an adjective: it "participates in" each. Like a verb it carries tense; like an adjective it inflects for gender, case and number to agree with the noun it describes, and for translation it can usually be treated as one. Pāli has three participles where English has two: **past** (*done*), **present** (*doing*), and a third English calls the *gerundive*, *to be done*, which the tradition, not quite accurately, also calls the "future" participle. The names describe a form more than a time. A present participle can describe the past, as in "I was *walking*"; a past participle can describe the present, as in "she is *gone*"; and the gerundive is not future at all, as the next section explains.

Pāli usually does without the helping verbs English needs ('was' in "I *was* walking", 'is' in "she *is* gone"), so a participle standing alone in a Pāli sentence often has to be given one in translation. The last section of this chapter returns to that, and to a further use no English participle has: standing on its own as a noun, with nothing for it to agree with. There is a fourth use, in which a participle and a subject of its own make a whole clause with no finite verb anywhere in it, and it is large enough to have Chapter 16 to itself. This chapter supplies the participles for it.

### The past participle, and its two voices

The most common past-participle ending is *-ta*, added to the root or, for many verbs, to the present stem.

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *kata* | *karoti* 'does, makes' | done, made |
| *gata* | *gacchati* 'goes' | gone |
| *desita* | *deseti* 'teaches' | taught |
| *bhūta* | *bhavati* 'is' | been |
| *mato* | *marati* 'dies' | died |

They inflect as *-a*/*-ā* stem adjectives do (Appendix A.1). Two of the words in that table have different functions, and the difference is the most useful thing this chapter can add.

A transitive verb's past participle is passive; an intransitive verb's is active.[^oberlies-56a] *Desita* 'taught' and *kata* 'done' describe what was done *to* something: a transitive sense, and a passive one, since the thing described is the patient, not the agent. *Gata* 'gone' and *bhūta* 'been' describe what the subject itself did: an intransitive sense, and an active one, because there is no patient for a passive to have. English blurs this by using the same *-en/-ed* form for both ("a *broken* pot", passive; "a *fallen* leaf", active, not "a leaf that fell upon"), so a reader coming from English needs the rule stated, not left to be noticed.

[^oberlies-56a]: Oberlies §56(a) (p. 262), stating it as the general case with named exceptions on both sides: a handful of transitive verbal adjectives keep an active sense (*diṭṭho ahaṁ dhammavaraṁ* 'I have seen the excellent teaching', where *diṭṭha* takes an object instead of being taken as one) and a handful of intransitive ones are used passively. A reader needs the direction of the rule first, not its exceptionlessness.

- **Desito**, ānanda, mayā dhammo anantaraṁ abāhiraṁ karitvā. (SN 47.9 — S v 153)
- *desito* taught (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *ānanda* Ānanda (m. voc. sg.) · *mayā* by me (m. inst. sg.) · *dhammo* the teaching (m. nom. sg.) · *anantaraṁ abāhiraṁ karitvā* holding nothing back (absol. phrase)
- "Ānanda, the teaching **has been taught** by me, holding nothing back."

- Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu **gacchanto** vā 'gacchāmī'ti pajānāti, ṭhito vā 'ṭhitomhī'ti pajānāti, nisinno vā 'nisinnomhī'ti pajānāti, sayāno vā 'sayānomhī'ti pajānāti. (MN 10 — M i 56)
- *puna caparaṁ* furthermore · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *bhikkhu* a monk (m. nom. sg.) · *gacchanto* walking (m. nom. sg. prp.) · *vā* or · *gacchāmi* I walk (1 sg. pres.) · *ti* (quotation) · *pajānāti* understands (3 sg. pres.) · *ṭhito* standing (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *ṭhitomhi* < *ṭhito* + *amhi* I am (1 sg.) · *nisinno* seated (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *sayāno* lying down (m. nom. sg. prp.)
- "Furthermore, monks, a monk, when **walking**, understands 'I am walking'; when **standing**, understands 'I am standing'; when **seated**, understands 'I am seated'; when **lying down**, understands 'I am lying down'."

That second passage carries the whole point in one sentence. *Ṭhito* and *nisinno* are past participles of intransitive verbs ('having come to stand', 'having come to sit'), and both are read actively, as ordinary descriptions of the monk's own posture; *gacchanto* and *sayāno* beside them are present participles of the same verbs, active in the ordinary way present participles are. None of these is passive, because there is no object for a passive to act on.

Just as with adjectives, the "thing" a participle describes is sometimes an unstated "it": the neuter singular carrying the whole clause:

- Evaṁ me **sutaṁ**. (MN 26 — M i 160)
- *evaṁ* thus · *me* by me (gen./inst. sg.) · *sutaṁ* heard (n. nom. sg. pp.)
- "Thus (it) was **heard** by me."

**Less common than the *-ta* type are past participles in *-na/-ṇa***, which inflect the same way.

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *ucchinno* | *ucchindati* 'cuts off' | cut off |
| *upapanna* | *upapajjati* 'takes rebirth' | took rebirth |
| *khīṇa* | *khīyati* 'ends, is exhausted' | ended, exhausted |
| *dinna* | *dadāti* 'gives' | given |

- '**Khīṇā** jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā'ti pajānātī"ti. (SN 35.28 — S iv 20)
- *khīṇā* ended (f. nom. sg. pp.) · *jāti* birth (f. nom. sg.) · *vusitaṁ* lived (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *brahmacariyaṁ* the spiritual life (n. nom. sg.) · *kataṁ* done (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *karaṇīyaṁ* what was to be done (n. nom. sg. grd.) · *na aparaṁ* no further · *itthattāya* for this state of being (n. dat. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *pajānāti* understands (3 sg. pres.)
- "He understands: 'Birth **is ended**, the spiritual life **has been lived**, what had to be done **has been done**, there is no more of this state of being.'"

This is the canon's formula for full awakening, and it is four participles serving as finite verbs in one breath, three of them past (*khīṇā*, *vusitaṁ*, *kataṁ*) and the last a gerundive (*karaṇīyaṁ*, met properly below).

A third group is assimilated: *-ṭha*, *-dha* and *-ḷha* are all sound changes on *-ta*, produced when the root ends in a consonant that the *t* cannot follow directly.

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *diṭṭha* | *dassati* 'sees' | seen |
| *buddha* | *bujjhati* 'wakes up (to), understands' | woken up (to), understood |
| *laddha* | *labhati* 'gets, obtains' | got, obtained |
| *virūḷha* | *virūhati* 'grows up'[^rulha-fix] | grown up |

[^rulha-fix]: The plain, unprefixed *rūḷha* is a grammarians' abstraction: it does not occur bare anywhere in the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana. The canon has it only compounded (*virūḷha* 'grown up', *samārūḷha* 'mounted, climbed', *ārūḷha* 'mounted', *abhirūḷha* 'mounted', the last of these Oberlies's own example at §56, p. 262), and a reader will meet those forms.

The most famous verse in the language is built almost entirely from this group, and it is given whole here, not as isolated headwords:

- Gahakāraka **diṭṭhosi**, puna gehaṁ na kāhasi; sabbā te phāsukā **bhaggā**, gahakūṭaṁ **visaṅkhataṁ**; visaṅkhāragataṁ cittaṁ, taṇhānaṁ khayamajjhagā. (Dhp 154 — Dhp p. 23)
- *gahakāraka* house-builder (m. voc. sg.) · *diṭṭho* seen (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *asi* you are (2 sg. pres.) · *puna* again · *gehaṁ* a house (n. acc. sg.) · *na* not · *kāhasi* you will make (2 sg. fut.) · *sabbā*<sub>a</sub> all (f. nom. pl.) · *te* your (gen. sg.) · *phāsukā*<sub>a</sub> rafters (f. nom. pl.) · *bhaggā*<sub>a</sub> broken (f. nom. pl. pp.) · *gahakūṭaṁ*<sub>b</sub> the roof-peak (n. nom. sg.) · *visaṅkhataṁ*<sub>b</sub> demolished (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *visaṅkhāragataṁ*<sub>c</sub> gone to the unconditioned (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *cittaṁ*<sub>c</sub> the mind (n. nom. sg.) · *taṇhānaṁ* of craving (f. gen. pl.) · *khayaṁ* the ending (m. acc. sg.) · *ajjhagā* has reached (3 sg. aor.)
- "House-builder, **you are seen**! You will not build a house again. All your rafters **are broken**, the roof-peak **demolished**; the mind **has gone** to the unconditioned, it has reached the ending of craving."

*Diṭṭhosi* is one printed word standing for two: *diṭṭho* + *asi*, 'seen' + 'you are', the *-o* and *a-* fusing as Chapter 2 predicts. The de-sandhied pair belongs in the gloss line, as here, never in the line quoted as the verse; the tradition holds that the joined form was said, and the manuscripts have only the joined form.

### The past active participle

A participle can also be built *from* a past participle, by adding *-vant-* or *-in-*, and the result is active regardless of transitivity.[^tavant-oberlies] *Vusitavā* 'one who has lived (the spiritual life)', from *vusita* 'lived' plus *-vant-*, means simply 'accomplished': an epithet, not a passive report of having-been-lived. It inflects like the *(m/v)ant*-stems of Appendix A.1, the very paradigm this chapter's present participles use, since *bhagavant-* 'the Blessed One' is built the same way; the *-in* type (*bhuttāvī* 'one who has eaten', from *bhutta* + *-in-*) inflects like the *ī*/*in*-stems of the same appendix.

[^tavant-oberlies]: Oberlies §56, its closing paragraph (p. 263): "By adding the suffix *°va(nt)*- to a verbal adjective an active participle is formed." Thānuttamo (pp. 113–114) gives it its own section, "Past Active Participle", and reports that the absolutive has mostly displaced it in ordinary use, so no coursebook consulted for this chapter teaches it, although it is common enough in the canon to be met on purpose, not by accident.

This is no marginal formation. *Vusitavā* has sixty occurrences in the root texts, *bhuttāvī* thirty-six, *katāvī* fifteen, *vijitāvī* forty-three: more common, cell for cell, than most of the paradigm this book has taught so far. It shows up chained together with other participles in the standard description of an arahant:

- … yo hoti bhikkhu arahaṁ khīṇāsavo **vusitavā** katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṁyojano sammadaññāvimutto, so nesaṁ aggamakkhāyati dhammeneva, no adhammena. (DN 27 — D iii 83)
- *yo hoti bhikkhu* whichever monk (m. nom. sg.) · *arahaṁ* an arahant (m. nom. sg.) · *khīṇāsavo* one whose defilements are ended (m. nom. sg.) · *vusitavā* who has lived (the spiritual life) (m. nom. sg. p. act. p.) · *katakaraṇīyo* who has done what was to be done (m. nom. sg.) · *ohitabhāro* who has laid down the burden (m. nom. sg.) · *anuppattasadattho* who has attained the goal (m. nom. sg.) · *parikkhīṇabhavasaṁyojano* whose fetters of existence are exhausted (m. nom. sg.) · *sammadaññāvimutto* who is rightly freed through knowledge (m. nom. sg.) · *so* he (m. nom. sg.) · *nesaṁ* among them (m. gen. pl.) · *aggam* foremost · *akkhāyati* is said to be (3 sg. pres. pass.) · *dhammena* by principle (m. inst. sg.) · *eva* just · *no* not · *adhammena* by what is against principle (m. inst. sg.)
- "… whichever monk is an arahant, one whose defilements are ended, **who has lived the spiritual life**, who has done what was to be done, who has laid down the burden, who has attained the goal, whose fetters of existence are exhausted, who is rightly freed through knowledge — he is said to be foremost among them by principle, not by what is against principle."

Every element of that chain except *vusitavā* is itself a past participle used adjectivally: five ordinary ones and a compound (*katakaraṇīya*, past participle plus gerundive, 'done-what-was-to-be-done'). Only *vusitavā* is built as this section describes, and without the formation you will parse it as a stray irregular, not as a member of a real, if minor, class.

### The present participle

The present participle stem is formed with *-māna* or with *-(n)t*. *-māna* is the commoner of the two in a beginner's early reading; *-(n)t* is the ending this book's own inherited chapters lean on more heavily, and it deserves the fuller treatment.

#### Present participle in *-māna*

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *gacchamāno* | *gacchati* 'goes' | going |
| *jānamāno* | *jānāti* 'knows, understands' | knowing, understanding |
| *vedayamāno* | *vedeti* = *vedayati* 'feels' | feeling |
| *samāno* | *atthi* 'is, exists' | being, existing |

They inflect like *-a* stem adjectives (*gacchamāno*, *gacchamānaṁ*, *gacchamānena* …). A handful of verbs (including, oddly, *atthi* and *karoti*) keep an older, historical form of the same ending, bare *-āna*, not *-māna*, with no *m*.[^ana-not-mana] *Sayāno* 'lying down', already met above, is built this way straight on the root, *saya-* + *-āna*, not by dropping an *m* from a longer form that never existed for this verb.

[^ana-not-mana]: Oberlies §54 (p. 257): as the middle voice stopped being a living category and the passive took over active endings, the two suffixes *-māna* and *-āna* (one originally the middle-voice ending, the other originally the passive's) became "true alternatives", the *-āna* type often hidden behind the commoner *-māna* one. *Karoti*'s own present middle participle is a further irregularity: *kurumāno* or *kubbamāno*, not the expected *kārayamāna*, built on the old stem *kubba-*. Oberlies names *kubba(nt)-/kubba(m)āna-* explicitly as "one of the very few exceptions" to the regular present-stem-plus-*-māna* rule. *Kārayamāna* is a real word all the same, and it means what its shape says: the middle participle of the *causative* *kāreti*, 'having something made'. DPD gives it twice, both "prp of *kāreti*", √*kar*, *kāraya* + *māna*, glossed 'making; causing to make' and 'building; causing to do', and cites it from the *Kuṭikārasikkhāpada* and the *Dvebhāgasikkhāpada*, the Vinaya rules on having a hut and a sitting-mat made. Geiger §191 (p. 182) had it as well, filed under the causative's uncontracted stems. In the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana it has twenty-two occurrences, ten of them in the root-text Vinaya, and every one is oblique: *bhikkhunā kuṭiṁ kārayamānena*, 'by a monk having a hut built'. There is no nominative *kārayamāno* in the canon at all. This chapter's first draft therefore called the form unattested, having looked it up in the one shape it never takes.

- Evaṁ **viharamānassa**, suddhikāmassa bhikkhuno; khīyanti āsavā sabbe, nibbutiñcādhigacchati. (Thag 10.6 — Th p. 61)
- *evaṁ* thus · *viharamānassa* dwelling (m. gen. sg. prp.) · *suddhikāmassa* longing for purity (m. gen. sg.) · *bhikkhuno* for a monk (m. gen. sg.) · *khīyanti* are exhausted (3 pl. pres.) · *āsavā* the taints (m. nom. pl.) · *sabbe* all (m. nom. pl.) · *nibbutiṁ* extinguishment (f. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *adhigacchati* reaches (3 sg. pres.)
- "For a monk **dwelling** thus, longing for purity, all defilements are ended, and he reaches extinguishment."

#### Present participle in *-(n)t*

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *gaccha(n)t* | *gacchati* 'goes' | going |
| *jāna(n)t* | *jānāti* 'knows, understands' | knowing, understanding |
| *sa(n)t* | *atthi* 'is, exists' | being, existing |

The *-(n)t* type inflects like the *(m/v)ant*-stems of Appendix A.1, and four things about it need care. The nominative singular has a special short form, *-aṁ*, standing alongside the regular *-anto*. The weak stem appears through the oblique singular and the genitive plural (*-atā*, *-ato*, *-ati*, *-ataṁ*) beside the thematised endings, so the locative singular of *sa(n)t* is *sati*, spelt like the noun *sati* 'mindfulness'; the locative absolute of *atthi* takes that form, and Chapter 16 has the construction. The feminine strengthens to *-antī*, not the plain *-atī* an *ī*-stem would predict, and only *sa(n)t* among the participles also builds a feminine on the weak stem.[^weak-stem-sant] The pronominal endings *-asmā*, *-amhā*, *-ato*, *-asmiṁ* and *-amhi* attach only to the thematised stem in *-anta*, and even there the root texts print them seldom.[^ant-pronominal] The whole paradigm is in Appendix A.1.

[^weak-stem-sant]: Oberlies §41 (pp. 178–179): the feminines of this stem type "are usually derived from the strong stem (*kubbantī-*); only adjectives have a feminine in *°atī-* (*sīlavatī-*, *mahatī-*) as has *sa(nt)-* (*satī-*)". Perniola §38(f), pp. 46–47, draws the same line between adjectives and participles without naming the exception (an *-ant* adjective takes *-ī* on the weak stem, *sīlavatī*, *mahatī*; a present participle takes it on the strong, *gacchantī*, *carantī*), and Ānandajoti's *Practical Guide*, "Verbs", p. 10, puts the strong stem in the nominative and accusative (*gacchantī*, *gacchantiṁ*) but the weak stem in the oblique singular (*gacchatiyā*), a form the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana does not print; the canon has *gacchantiyā*. The canon has both feminines. *Satī* stands in Jātaka verse, of a hope that succeeds however far off it has gone: *tāva dūragatā satī* (Ja 380 — J iii 251), agreeing with *āsā*, feminine. The prose uses *santī*, built regularly on the strong stem: *indasālaguhā visamā santī samā samapādi* (DN 21 — D ii 269), of the Indasāla cave that was uneven and became even. So the weak-stem feminine is real, and it is not the commoner of the two.

[^ant-pronominal]: Oberlies prints the locative *arahantamhi* in his own paradigm (§41, p. 178), and Perniola §34, p. 40, sets out the full *-a*-stem declension of *sīlavant* beside the consonantal one, *sīlavantasmā* and *sīlavantasmiṁ* among it. In the root texts of the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana these endings are rare and mostly in verse: *himavantamhi* sixteen times, *arahantamhi* twice, *mahantasmiṁ* and *mahantasmā* once each, and five present participles standing in locative absolutes: *sampādayantamhi*, *gaḷantamhi*, *sarantamhi*, *uppajjantamhi*, *nidhāyantamhi*. None of them occurs on the weak stem; *gacchatasmiṁ* and *gacchatamhi* do not occur anywhere in the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana.

Present participles of verbs whose stem ends in *-e* or *-o* inflect the same way but keep their stem vowel, and have no special short *-aṁ* form: *dento* 'giving', *desento* 'teaching', *karonto* 'doing, making, working'.

- So taṁ itthiṁ passeyya aññena purisena saddhiṁ **santiṭṭhantiṁ** **sallapantiṁ** **sañjagghantiṁ** **saṁhasantiṁ**. (MN 101 — M ii 223)
- *so* he (m. nom. sg.) · *taṁ*<sub>a</sub> that (f. acc. sg.) · *itthiṁ*<sub>a</sub> woman (f. acc. sg.) · *passeyya* might see (3 sg. opt.) · *aññena*<sub>b</sub> with another (m. inst. sg.) · *purisena*<sub>b</sub> man (m. inst. sg.) · *saddhiṁ* together with · *santiṭṭhantiṁ*<sub>a</sub> standing (f. acc. sg. prp.) · *sallapantiṁ*<sub>a</sub> chatting (f. acc. sg. prp.) · *sañjagghantiṁ*<sub>a</sub> joking (f. acc. sg. prp.) · *saṁhasantiṁ*<sub>a</sub> laughing (f. acc. sg. prp.)
- "He might see that woman **standing**, **chatting**, **joking** and **laughing** together with another man."

- Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā udāyī mahatiyā gihiparisāya parivuto dhammaṁ **desento** nisinno hoti. (AN 5.159 — A iii 184)
- *tena samayena* at that time · *kho pana* now · *āyasmā udāyī*<sub>a</sub> the venerable Udāyī (m. nom. sg.) · *mahatiyā gihiparisāya* by a large lay assembly (f. inst. sg.) · *parivuto*<sub>a</sub> surrounded (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *dhammaṁ* the teaching (m. acc. sg.) · *desento*<sub>a</sub> teaching (m. nom. sg. prp.) · *nisinno*<sub>a</sub> seated (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *hoti* is (3 sg. pres.)
- "Now at that time the venerable Udāyī, surrounded by a large lay assembly, was seated **teaching** the Dhamma."

One use of the present participle has no equivalent in the *-māna* type and is easy to miss: it can carry a conditional sense, marking what would happen, not what is happening.[^prp-irrealis] The reading is rare enough that it should be suspected only where the ordinary progressive sense makes no sense of the sentence, but where it applies, the participle functions as Chapter 8's conditional mood does, without any of that mood's own morphology.

[^prp-irrealis]: Oberlies's *rem.* to §54 (p. 257): "Pāli shows the incipient use of the present participle as conditional (i.e. to denote the *irrealis*)", with the example *adhammaṁ sārathi kayirā, maṁ ce tvaṁ nikhaṇaṁ vane* 'you **would do** wrong, charioteer, if you were to bury me in the forest', a present participle, *kayirā* being read as if it carried the counterfactual sense a conditional would.

### The gerundive

The third participle means *to be done*, *should be done*, *must be done*. It carries an obligation and not a time, and everything awkward about its names follows from that one fact.

Earlier grammars call it the "future participle", which is wrong twice over: it is not built on the future stem, and it does not mean 'about to'.[^future-participle-misnomer] Its more exact name is the **gerundive**, or *participium necessitatis*, 'the participle of necessity', and that is the name this book uses and the sense the gloss `grd.` carries throughout. Thānuttamo's chapter heads it "Future **Passive** Participle (aka gerundive, participle of necessity)", which at least restores the passivity the common label drops.

[^future-participle-misnomer]: A real, if vanishingly rare, active participle built on the *future* stem does exist: Oberlies §54, p. 256, gives *(fut.) marissaṁ* 'who is dying, about to die' as his only example, the *-nt-* suffix added to the future instead of the present stem. It is not what "future participle" means in ordinary use, and a reader is unlikely to meet it outside a grammar's own footnotes.

**The commonest ending is *-tabba*.**

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *gantabbaṁ* | *gacchati* 'goes' | to be gone |
| *kātabbaṁ* | *karoti* 'does' | to be done |
| *pahātabbaṁ* | *pajahati* 'abandons' | to be abandoned |
| *veditabbaṁ* | *vedeti* 'feels, experiences' | to be felt, experienced |

They inflect like *-a* stem adjectives, and the two DN 17 examples below show the whole moral vocabulary of a precept built from nothing but this one suffix:

- Pāṇo na hantabbo, adinnaṁ na **ādātabbaṁ**, kāmesu micchā na caritabbā, musā na bhaṇitabbā, majjaṁ na pātabbaṁ, yathābhuttañca bhuñjathā'ti. (DN 17 — D ii 173)
- *pāṇo* a living being (m. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *hantabbo* to be killed (m. nom. sg. grd.) · *adinnaṁ* what is not given (n. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *ādātabbaṁ* to be taken (n. nom. sg. grd.) · *kāmesu* in sensual pleasures (m. loc. pl.) · *micchā* wrongly · *na* not · *caritabbā* to be indulged in (grd.) · *musā* falsely · *na* not · *bhaṇitabbā* to be spoken (grd.) · *majjaṁ* intoxicant (n. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *pātabbaṁ* to be drunk (n. nom. sg. grd.) · *yathābhuttaṁ* what is properly eaten (n. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *bhuñjatha* eat! (2 pl. impv.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "A living being **is not to be killed**, what is not given **is not to be taken**, one **should not** wrongly **indulge** in sensual pleasures, one **should not speak** falsely, intoxicants **are not to be drunk**, and you should eat only what is properly given."

- Dveme, bhikkhave, antā pabbajitena na **sevitabbā**. (SN 56.11 — S v 421)
- *dve*<sub>a</sub> two (m. nom. pl.) · *ime*<sub>a</sub> these (m. nom. pl.) · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *antā*<sub>a</sub> extremes (m. nom. pl.) · *pabbajitena* by one gone forth (m. inst. sg.) · *na* not · *sevitabbā*<sub>a</sub> to be practised (m. nom. pl. grd.)
- "Monks, these two extremes **are not to be practised** by one who has gone forth."

*Pabbajitena* there is the agent, in the instrumental, where a gerundive puts an agent when it names one. The Khandhaka's account of what a student owes his preceptor runs to 158 gerundives in 1,364 words, and names the student, in that same instrumental, fourteen times. One agent for every eleven obligations.[^upajjhayavatta] The rest read like this:

- Sace upajjhāyo gāmaṁ pavisitukāmo hoti, nivāsanaṁ **dātabbaṁ**, paṭinivāsanaṁ **paṭiggahetabbaṁ**, kāyabandhanaṁ **dātabbaṁ**, saguṇaṁ katvā saṅghāṭiyo **dātabbā**, dhovitvā patto sodako **dātabbo**. (Kd 1 — Vin i 46)
- *sace* if · *upajjhāyo* the preceptor (m. nom. sg.) · *gāmaṁ* the village (m. acc. sg.) · *pavisitukāmo* wanting to enter (m. nom. sg.) · *hoti* is · *nivāsanaṁ* a sarong (n. nom. sg.) · *dātabbaṁ* is to be given (n. nom. sg. grd.) · *paṭinivāsanaṁ* the one being worn (n. nom. sg.) · *paṭiggahetabbaṁ* is to be received (n. nom. sg. grd.) · *kāyabandhanaṁ* a belt (n. nom. sg.) · *saguṇaṁ* the two as one (adverbial acc.) · *katvā* having made (absol.) · *saṅghāṭiyo* the outer robes (f. nom. pl.) · *dātabbā* are to be given (f. nom. pl. grd.)[^saguna] · *dhovitvā* having rinsed (absol.) · *patto* the bowl (m. nom. sg.) · *sodako* still wet (m. nom. sg.) · *dātabbo* is to be given (m. nom. sg. grd.)
- "If the preceptor wants to enter the village, a sarong **is to be given** and the one he is wearing **received**, a belt **is to be given**, the outer robes folded together and **given**, the bowl rinsed and **given** to him still wet."

Five gerundives, and not one of them names who must do any of it. *Dātabba* alone stands three times in three forms (neuter singular beside *nivāsanaṁ*, feminine plural beside *saṅghāṭiyo*, masculine singular beside *patto*), and each time it agrees with the thing to be given and never with the person who must give it. That is the whole grammar of the form. A gerundive is passive, so its subject is what the action is done to; the agent is an extra, and Pāli supplies it only when it is not obvious.[^grd-agent]

[^grd-agent]: The translator has no such option, and the gap shows how the Pāli works. Brahmāli renders this sentence "the student should give him a sarong and receive the one he's wearing in return … He should give him a belt", naming the student and then carrying him through four more clauses as *he*: five agents in the English where this sentence's Pāli has none. Nothing has been added to the sense: the section is headed with the student's duties, and the Pāli can leave him unsaid for a page together where an English finite verb must have a subject.

[^upajjhayavatta]: Counted over Kd 1 — Vin i 44, the *upajjhāyavatta* or 'duties towards a preceptor': 158 forms in *-tabb-*, and *saddhivihārikena* 'by the student' fourteen times. That is 116 gerundives per thousand words, against the 14.7 of the Vinaya taken as a whole, so whoever can follow the whole section can follow the Khandhaka's procedural chapters generally.

[^saguna]: DPD's headword *saguṇa* is 'with good qualities', *sa* + *guṇa*, and it is a different word: a collision of a kind to watch for, where the form is right and the sense belongs to something else. The Vinaya commentary glosses the phrase where it stands, in its own *Upajjhāyavattakathā* (Sp V 978): *Saguṇaṁ katvāti dve cīvarāni ekato katvā*, "*saguṇaṁ katvā* means having made the two robes into one". It then explains the plural this example depends on: folded together they are still *saṅghāṭiyo*, plural, *sabbañhi cīvaraṁ saṅghaṭitattā 'saṅghāṭī'ti vuccati*, "for every robe, being stitched together, is called a *saṅghāṭī*".

A second ending is *-anīya*, with a family of near-relations: *-aniya*, *-aneyya*, *-eyya*.

| Form | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *gamanīyo* | *gacchati* 'goes' | to be gone |
| *karaṇīyaṁ* | *karoti* 'does' | to be done |
| *pariññeyyo* | *parijānāti* 'fully understands' | to be fully understood |
| *vedanīyaṁ* | *vedeti* 'feels, experiences' | to be felt, experienced |

- Rūpaṁ, bhikkhave, **pariññeyyo** dhammo, vedanā pariññeyyo dhammo, saññā pariññeyyo dhammo, saṅkhārā pariññeyyo dhammo, viññāṇaṁ pariññeyyo dhammo. (SN 22.23 — S iii 26)
- *rūpaṁ* form (n. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *pariññeyyo*<sub>a</sub> to be fully understood (m. nom. sg. grd.) · *dhammo*<sub>a</sub> a thing (m. nom. sg.) · *vedanā* feeling (f. nom. sg.) · *saññā* perception (f. nom. sg.) · *saṅkhārā* volitional formations (m. nom. pl.) · *viññāṇaṁ* consciousness (n. nom. sg.)
- "Monks, form is a thing **to be fully understood**, feeling is a thing to be fully understood, perception is a thing to be fully understood, volitional formations are a thing to be fully understood, consciousness is a thing to be fully understood."

That is the five aggregates, each in turn declared *pariññeyyo*: the whole of a practitioner's task, stated as a single repeated gerundive.

The Vinaya's use of the same ending is a good deal less exalted, and it is interesting for the repetition, not the sense. Every training rule governing a monastic's deportment closes on the same two words:

- “Sakkaccaṁ piṇḍapātaṁ paṭiggahessāmīti **sikkhā karaṇīyā**”ti. (Bu Sk 27 — Vin iv 190)
- *sakkaccaṁ* respectfully · *piṇḍapātaṁ* almsfood (m. acc. sg.) · *paṭiggahessāmi* I shall receive (1 sg. fut.) · *ti* (quotative) · *sikkhā*<sub>a</sub> the training (f. nom. sg.) · *karaṇīyā*<sub>a</sub> is to be done (f. nom. sg. grd.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "'I shall receive almsfood respectfully' — **this is the training to be done**."

*Karaṇīyā* is feminine because *sikkhā* is, and it is the gerundive proper of the same verb whose substantivised neuter, *karaṇīyaṁ* 'duty, work', appears in the drift table at the end of this chapter. The pair stands unaltered at the close of seventy-nine sentences in the root texts.[^sekhiya-count] Everything before the quotative changes and everything after it does not, the property Appendix B measures as a low type/token ratio and recommends a reader seek out, arrived at here by nothing cleverer than repetition.

[^sekhiya-count]: Counted in the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana root texts, where *sikkhā karaṇīyā* occurs 79 times, all of them in the Vibhaṅga. Seventy-six are in the monks' analysis, one more than the seventy-five rules, because the last rule is given twice: once as first laid down and once as amended to exempt the sick, *na udake agilāno uccāraṁ … karissāmi*. The remaining three are where the nuns' analysis restates a rule in its own words.

A third, less obvious type is formed with a bare *-a*, no *-tabba* or *-(a)nīya* affix at all: a direct survival of the Sanskrit pattern *sukara-* 'easy to do', *duṣkara-* 'hard to do'.[^a-gerundive] *Dukkara* 'hard to do' and *sulabha* 'easy to get' are built this way, the *su-* and *du-* prefixes in the role a suffix fills everywhere else.

[^a-gerundive]: Oberlies §55 (p. 259). The type is unproductive (new examples were made by analogy to the old ones, not freely), so it survives only with *su-* or *du-* attached and not as a general-purpose gerundive suffix.

[^grd-density]: Counted over the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana root texts by shelf, as forms in *-tabb-* against the shelf's own word count: 6,170 in the five Vinaya books (420,685 words, 14.7 per thousand), 4,578 in the forty-one files of the Sutta Piṭaka (1,549,966 words, 2.95), and 4,829 in the Abhidhamma (830,590 words, 5.8). The two lists in the text are illustrative, so the ranking itself is given here, each gerundive counted across all its inflected forms. The Vinaya's ten commonest are *dātabba* 488, *kātabba* 486, *gantabba* 321, *jānitabba* 194, *vattabba* 192, *ñāpetabba* 166, *nikkhipitabba* 163, *upasampādetabba* 160, *dhāretabba* 144, *kāretabba* 126; and *jānitabba* is procedural too, the word for ascertaining whether a building site is fit before it is approved. The Sutta Piṭaka's are *pahātabba* 537, *veditabba* 484, *bhāvetabba* 402, *sikkhitabba* 295, *sevitabba* 240, *sacchikātabba* 157, *vitthāretabba* 95, *kātabba* 91, *niddisitabba* 86, *paccavekkhitabba* 68. One form, *kātabba*, is in both tens, which states the disjunction as starkly as possible. Widening to eighteen a side adds only *dātabba*, *gantabba* and *vattabba*, the three other verbs no Pāli of any kind can do without.

[^cca-yha]: The two are not quite the same formation, and DPD separates them. *Gārayha* it derives as √*garah* > *gārah* + *ya*, Sanskrit *garhya*: the *y* and the *h* changing places, the *-ya* suffix clearly. *Kicca* it derives as √*kar* > *kir* + *tya*, Sanskrit *kṛtya*, so the *cc* is an assimilated *ty*, not an assimilated *y*. The endings converge; the suffixes behind them do not.

A last, small group has the endings *-cca* or *-yha*: assimilations of *-(i)ya*, the same sound changes that produced *-ṭha/dha/ḷha* on the *-ta* past participle. *Kicca* 'to be done', from *karoti*, and *gārayha* 'to be reproached', from *garahati*, both inflect like *-a* stem adjectives, and neither needs a table of its own.[^cca-yha]

The gerundive is not spread evenly through the canon, and where it collects says what it is for. In the root texts *-tabba* runs at about fifteen occurrences per thousand words in the Vinaya against three in the Sutta Piṭaka, and the verbs it attaches to in the two are all but disjoint. The Vinaya's commonest are *dātabba* 'to be given', *kātabba* 'to be done', *gantabba* 'to be gone to', *upasampādetabba* 'to be given the full ordination', *nissajjitabba* 'to be relinquished'. The suttas' are *pahātabba* 'to be abandoned', *bhāvetabba* 'to be developed', *veditabba* 'to be known', *sevitabba* 'to be cultivated', *sacchikātabba* 'to be realised'.[^grd-density] One list is a procedure and the other is a practice. The suffix is the same; the obligations are not.

### Participles on the page

Three participles, four formations and a paradigm make up the system. What follows is the rest a reader meets: a participle negated, a participle leaning on a helping verb, a participle that has stopped needing a noun, and a participle whose sense has walked away from its derivation.

**Negated with *a-*.** Participles are occasionally negated with the adverb *na*, but usually with the prefix *a-*, which becomes *an-* before a vowel.

- … **asantaṁ** vā ajjhattaṁ kāmacchandaṁ: 'natthi me ajjhattaṁ kāmacchando'ti pajānāti … (AN 3.121 — A i 272)
- *asantaṁ* not being present (n. acc. sg. prp.) · *vā* or · *ajjhattaṁ* internal (n. acc. sg.) · *kāmacchandaṁ* sensual desire (n. acc. sg.) · *na atthi* there is not · *me* for me (dat. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *pajānāti* he understands (3 sg. pres.)
- "Or, sensual desire **not being present** internally, he understands: 'There is no sensual desire in me.'"

*Asantaṁ* is itself a present participle, the negated form of *santaṁ*, 'being', from *atthi*: the *-nt* type this chapter has just given a full paradigm, used here in a way that paradigm alone would not predict.

**With helping verbs.** Pāli can, though it rarely must, add a helping verb (*atthi*, *bhavati*, *hoti*) to a participle, much as English adds 'is' or 'has been'. *Diṭṭhosi*, already met above, is the fused case: *asi* 'you are' welded onto *diṭṭho* 'seen', with no space between them on the page. Translation of the helping verb is often not literal: *hoti* can as easily give 'has' or 'has been' as 'is':

- Idha, bhikkhave, ekaccassa kāyaduccaritaṁ **kataṁ** hoti, akataṁ hoti kāyasucaritaṁ. (AN 2.3 — A i 49)
- *idha* here · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *ekaccassa* for someone (m. gen. sg.) · *kāyaduccaritaṁ* bodily misconduct (n. nom. sg.) · *kataṁ* done (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *hoti* is (3 sg. pres.) · *akataṁ* not done (n. nom. sg. pp.) · *kāyasucaritaṁ* bodily good conduct (n. nom. sg.)
- "Here, monks, for someone bodily bad conduct **has been done**, and bodily good conduct has not been done."

A participle can even serve as the helping verb of another participle:

- So sabyābajjhehi phassehi **phuṭṭho** **samāno** sabyābajjhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayati ekantadukkhaṁ, seyyathāpi sattā nerayikā. (AN 3.23 — A i 122)
- *so*<sub>a</sub> he (m. nom. sg.) · *sabyābajjhehi*<sub>b</sub> afflictive (m. inst. pl.) · *phassehi*<sub>b</sub> by contacts (m. inst. pl.) · *phuṭṭho*<sub>a</sub> touched (m. nom. sg. pp.) · *samāno*<sub>a</sub> being (m. nom. sg. prp.) · *sabyābajjhaṁ*<sub>c</sub> afflictive (f. acc. sg.) · *vedanaṁ*<sub>c</sub> feeling (f. acc. sg.) · *vedayati* feels (3 sg. pres.) · *ekantadukkhaṁ*<sub>c</sub> entirely painful (f. acc. sg.) · *seyyathāpi* just as · *sattā nerayikā* beings in hell (m. nom. pl.)
- "He, **being** **touched** by afflictive contacts, feels afflictive feeling, entirely painful, just as beings in hell do."

And a periphrasis carries the tenses English's own perfect system covers, one step further than 'is' and 'has been' reach. A past participle plus *bhavissati* (future in form, but not necessarily future in sense here) gives a pluperfect or a future-perfect: *patto abhavissaṁ* 'I would have attained', *gato bhavissati* 'he will have gone' (or, depending on context, 'he must have gone').[^periphrastic-perfect]

[^periphrastic-perfect]: Oberlies §56(d) (p. 262), calling the construction a *futurum exactum* when read as future-perfect and noting it can equally carry the pluperfect sense, past participle plus a past-tense auxiliary supplying 'had'.

**As nouns.** Like adjectives generally, a participle can stand alone as a noun when no noun is named for it to agree with:

- **Buddho** dhammamadesesi, jātiyā samatikkamaṁ. (SN 5.6 — S i 132)
- *buddho* the Awakened One (m. nom. sg. pp., substantivised) · *dhammaṁ* the teaching (n. acc. sg.) · *adesesi* taught (3 sg. aor.) · *jātiyā* of birth (f. gen. sg.) · *samatikkamaṁ* the going-beyond (m. acc. sg.)
- "**The Awakened One** taught the teaching, the going-beyond of birth."

- Pāṇo na hantabbo, **adinnaṁ** na ādātabbaṁ … (DN 17 — D ii 173, opening of the same precept quoted above)
- *pāṇo* a living being (m. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *hantabbo* to be killed (m. nom. sg. grd.) · *adinnaṁ* what is not given (n. nom. sg. pp., substantivised) · *na* not · *ādātabbaṁ* to be taken (n. nom. sg. grd.)
- "A living being is not to be killed, **what is not given** should not be taken …"

*Buddho* is the plainest possible case: a past participle, 'awakened', that has become a proper name so completely that its participial origin is easy to forget, though it is the reason the word means what it means.

**With non-literal meanings.** A participle's ordinary sense sometimes drifts a long way from its literal derivation, and the drift is better known than re-derived each time.

| Form | Sense | Literally | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|
| *arahaṁ* (prp.) | worthy, an arahant | "(being) worthy" | *arahati* 'is worthy' |
| *kamanīyo* (grd.) | lovely, desirable | "to be desired" | *kāmeti* 'desires' |
| *karaṇīyaṁ* (grd., n.) | duty, work | "to be done" | *karoti* 'does, makes' |
| *kuddho* (pp.) | angry | "angered" | *kujjhati* 'is angry' |
| *dassanīyo* (grd.) | beautiful | "to be seen" | *dassati* 'sees' |
| *paṭipanno* (pp.) | practising | "entered upon" | *paṭipajjati* 'enters upon' |
| *phoṭṭhabbaṁ* (grd., n.) | a tangible object | "to be touched" | *phusati* 'touches' |
| *bhojanīyaṁ* (grd., n.) | food | "to be eaten" | *bhuñjati* 'eats' |
| *saṅkhittaṁ* (pp.) | in brief | "shortened" | *saṅkhipati* 'shortens' |
| *santo* (prp.) | true, good | "existing" | *atthi* 'exists' |

### What this chapter is for

The largest thing a participle does was promised at the start and belongs to Chapter 16: put a participle beside a subject of its own, in an oblique case, and the pair becomes a clause with no finite verb anywhere in it: the locative and genitive absolutes. That is syntax and it needs the room. Here it is useful only to say which of this chapter's participles are eligible for it, because not all of them are. The present participle and the past passive participle head absolutes freely and account for nearly all of them. The past active participle does so too, rarely, one reason it has a section above, not a footnote. The gerundive never does.

That is the chapter's practical outcome. Tell a past participle from a present one, and which voice a past participle carries, and you have most of what the rest of the book assumes, because participles are a large part of how canonical Pāli makes statements at all, not an ornament of it. The awakening formula quoted above says *khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ* and has no finite verb in it until *pajānāti* arrives to report the whole thing. Four participles carry the most important sentence in the canon. This chapter exists mainly to teach you to hear them as verbs, instead of parsing them as adjectives and then wondering where the verb went.
