## Indeclinables and Particles

Every chapter so far has been about endings. A noun takes a case, a verb takes a person, an adjective agrees, and even the participle, which has no person, declines like an adjective. This chapter is about the words that do none of that.

The tradition calls them ***nipātā***, 'those that fall in'. Collins gives the image its full force: a *nipāta* is a word which falls into the language whole and complete, needing no grammatical transformation to fit.[^ind-nipata-etym] They are also called ***avyaya***, 'unchanging', the word behind *avyayībhāva*, the adverbial compound type in the chapter on compounds. Duroiselle counts about two hundred of them.

[^ind-nipata-etym]: Collins p. 121: "The word *nipāta* means falling, and a *nipāta* is a word which falls into language whole and complete, not needing grammatical transformation. All of them, especially the sub-category which western grammar calls adverbs, are sometimes called *avyayībhāva*, not subject to change."

One curious thing about the class needs saying at the outset. The two grammars this book leans on hardest for deciding whether a form is real have no section on indeclinables at all. Oberlies's grammar ends at §58, the absolutive; his index has one entry under *indeclinables*, and it points at a footnote. Geiger's ends at §214, the gerunds in *-yāna*. Neither omission is careless. A historical grammar is organised by inflection, tracking a sound change through a set of endings, and an indeclinable has no endings for the method to work on. So a word class supplying some of the commonest words in the canon falls straight through the middle of both.

The consequence for this chapter is that the sources which do treat it are coursebooks and reference grammars, not philological ones, and that every form below has had to be checked against the canon directly, with no grammarian having done it first. Seventeen forms offered by those sources turned out not to be canonical Pāli. They are not taught here.

### What the tradition does with them

Pāli's own grammarians recognise four kinds of word: ***nāma*** (nouns, adjectives, pronouns, numerals), ***ākhyāta*** (verbs), ***upasagga*** (prefixes) and ***nipāta*** (particles).[^ind-four-classes] The first two inflect; the last two do not. Prefixes are treated in the chapter on word formation, because a prefix cannot stand alone as a word, and standing alone is the property this chapter is about. Everything else that does not inflect is here.

[^ind-four-classes]: Collins pp. vii–viii: "Pali and Sanskrit grammar recognize four kinds of word: *nāma* (nouns, adjectives, pronouns, numerals), *ākhyāta* (verbs), *upasagga* (prepositions, prefixes), and *nipāta* (particles, conjunctions, adverbs), and the book is organized in those terms."

Where a Western grammar sorts these words by part of speech (adverb, conjunction, interjection), the Saddanīti sorts them by the job they do in a sentence. The traditional scheme is the better one to learn first, and the reason is that it has a fourth category. Calling *kho* an "emphatic" says what it feels like; calling it a **filler** says what it is for.[^ind-sadd-886]

[^ind-sadd-886]: Saddanīti 886 f., translated in Collins §4.1: "*samuccaya-vikappana-paṭisedha-pūraṇatthaṁ asatva-vācaka nepātika*, *nipāta* words are used for conjunction, disjunction, negation/prohibition, or as fillers, and do not denote any substantial existent."

| The job | Traditional name | Specimens |
|---|---|---|
| Joining | *samuccaya* | *ca*, *pi*, *atha* |
| Separating | *vikappana* | *vā*, *udāhu* |
| Denying and forbidding | *paṭisedha* | *na*, *no*, *mā*, *alaṁ* |
| Filling | *pūraṇattha* | *kho*, *hi*, *vata*, *sudaṁ*, *khalu* |

Three of those four are what any reader would expect. The fourth is treated in its own section below, because it changes how a page is read.

### Joining and separating

***Ca*** 'and' and ***vā*** 'or' are enclitic, which in practice means they follow the word they attach instead of standing between the two things joined. The commonest arrangement repeats the particle after each member:

- **Santussako ca subharo ca**, appakicco ca sallahukavutti. (Snp 1.8 — Sn p. 25)
- *santussako* content (m. nom. sg.) · *ca* and · *subharo* easy to support (m. nom. sg.) · *ca* and · *appakicco* of few duties · *ca* and · *sallahukavutti* living lightly
- "**Content and easy to support**, of few duties and living lightly."

That is 'A *ca* B *ca*'. Two other arrangements are ordinary, 'A *ca* B' and 'A B *ca*', and the second of these, with a single *ca* at the end, catches a new reader out, because nothing in the first half of the phrase signals that a conjunction is coming.[^ind-ca-three] Bare 'A B', with no *ca* at all and 'and' understood, is also common. The same three arrangements hold for *vā*.

[^ind-ca-three]: Collins §4.1(i): "Horses and elephants can be (i) *assā ca hatthino ca*, (ii) *assā ca hatthino*, or (iii) *assā hatthino ca*." He gives the same three for *vā* at (ii), and adds that *ca* can in context mean 'but' and *vā* can in context mean 'and'.

Because these words are enclitic they can fall inside a phrase that English would keep together, splitting an adjective from its noun:

- … iti **purāṇañca** vedanaṁ paṭihaṅkhāmi **navañca** vedanaṁ na uppādessāmi … (MN 2 — M i 10)
- *iti* thus · *purāṇaṁ*<sub>a</sub> old (f. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *vedanaṁ*<sub>a</sub> feeling (f. acc. sg.) · *paṭihaṅkhāmi* I shall ward off · *navaṁ*<sub>a</sub> new (f. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *na* not · *uppādessāmi* I shall produce
- "… thinking, 'I shall ward off **old** feeling **and** shall not produce **new**.'"

Read straight through, *purāṇañca vedanaṁ* looks like 'old, and feeling'. It is not: *ca* has landed after the first word of the phrase because it is enclitic and could land nowhere else, and it joins the whole clause to the one after it. The rule to carry is that a *ca* is not a boundary. Look for what it joins; do not assume it joins whatever stands immediately either side.

*Ca* also combines with the emphatic *eva*, usually assimilating to ***c'eva***, and the pair marks the first member of a list without adding much of its own:

- Ayoniso, bhikkhave, manasikaroto anuppannā **ceva** āsavā uppajjanti, uppannā **ca** āsavā pavaḍḍhanti. (MN 2 — M i 7)
- *ayoniso* unwisely · *bhikkhave* monks (voc. pl.) · *manasikaroto* for one attending (m. gen. sg. pres. part.) · *anuppannā* unarisen (m. nom. pl.) · *ca* and · *eva* just · *āsavā* defilements (m. nom. pl.) · *uppajjanti* arise · *uppannā* arisen · *ca* and · *pavaḍḍhanti* increase
- "For one who attends unwisely, monks, unarisen defilements arise **and** arisen defilements increase."

***Udāhu*** 'or' does a job *vā* does not: it introduces the second limb of a question offering two alternatives.

- Kiṁ **nu** kho, āvuso gotama, taṁ jīvaṁ taṁ sarīraṁ, **udāhu** aññaṁ jīvaṁ aññaṁ sarīran'ti? (DN 7 — D i 159)
- *kiṁ* what · *nu* (question) · *kho* indeed · *āvuso* friend (voc.) · *gotama* Gotama (voc.) · *taṁ* that (n. nom. sg.) · *jīvaṁ* soul · *taṁ* that · *sarīraṁ* body · *udāhu* or · *aññaṁ* other · *jīvaṁ* soul · *aññaṁ* other · *sarīraṁ* body · *ti* (quotation)
- "Is the soul the same as the body, friend Gotama, **or** is the soul one thing and the body another?"

### Enclitics, and how the editions write them

A word is **enclitic** when it cannot begin a sentence and leans instead on the word in front. *Ca*, *vā*, *pi*, *ce*, *pana*, *kho*, *hi* and *eva* are all enclitic, and the grammars state this as a rule about word order.[^ind-enclitic-rule]

[^ind-enclitic-rule]: Thānuttamo, *Māgadhabhāsā*, p. 120, citing Duroiselle's 1997 re-edition: "Never used as sentence initials are *ca*, *vā*, *yadi*, *sace*, *ce* …, *atha*, *atho*." In the 1921 edition, §538, pp. 243–244, Duroiselle says this of *ca*, *vā* and *ce* only, and lists *yadi*, *sace*, *atha* and *atho* without it; p. 330 repeats it of *ca* and *vā*. Thānuttamo's Warder citation, pp. 117–118, belongs to the clause that follows, not to this one, and it is as well that it does: Warder's synopsis there marks each of these words *enclitic* or *initial* one by one, and he makes *sace* and *atha* **initial**. Seven of the eight words called enclitic here are marked enclitic by Warder; the eighth, *eva*, is not a connective and does not appear in his synopsis at all.

The texts state it more strongly, and in a way that changes what a reader has to do about it. Counted as free-standing words in the root texts, *pi* occurs seven times and *eva* ninety-seven, figures so far below what anyone who has read a page of the canon would expect that the first response should be to doubt the count. The count is right. These words are not usually printed as words at all: they are written joined to whatever precedes them, and the joined forms are everywhere. *Sopi*, *ahampi*, *tvampi*, *mayampi*; *tatheva*, *sova*, and the string *-yeva* inside six hundred and ninety-three distinct root-text words.

One sentence of the Sāmaññaphala's account of the psychic powers makes the point seven times over:

- … **ekopi** hutvā bahudhā hoti, **bahudhāpi** hutvā eko hoti … **udakepi** abhijjamāne gacchati seyyathāpi pathaviyā; **ākāsepi** pallaṅkena kamati seyyathāpi pakkhī sakuṇo … (DN 2 — D i 78)
- *eko* one (m. nom. sg.) · *pi* even · *hutvā* having been (absol.) · *bahudhā* manifold · *hoti* becomes · *bahudhā* manifold · *pi* even · *eko* one · *hoti* becomes · *udake* on water (n. loc. sg.) · *pi* even · *abhijjamāne* not breaking apart (loc. sg. pres. part.) · *gacchati* goes · *seyyathā* just as · *pi* even · *pathaviyā* on earth (f. loc. sg.) · *ākāse* in space (m. loc. sg.) · *pi* even · *pallaṅkena* cross-legged (m. instr. sg.) · *kamati* moves · *pakkhī* winged · *sakuṇo* bird
- "… having been **one** he becomes many, having been **many** he becomes one … he goes **on water** without sinking as though on earth; he travels **through space** cross-legged like a bird on the wing."

Every one of those bolded words is a noun or numeral with *pi* fused onto it, and the fusion is the ordinary spelling, not a poetic licence. So the practical skill is seeing *pi* when it is not printed separately. A word ending in *-pi* that will not parse should be tried without it. The same holds for *-eva*, *-yeva* and *-ñeva*, for *-ti* at the end of a quotation, and for the *-ssu* below.

### Denying and forbidding

***Na*** is the ordinary negative, placed before the word it denies, and it negates a whole clause as readily as a single word. ***No*** is a stronger or more emphatic negative and also serves as the negative answer, 'no'. Both attach freely to a series:

- '**Neva** davāya, **na** madāya, **na** maṇḍanāya, **na** vibhūsanāya …' (MN 2 — M i 10)
- *na* not · *eva* just · *davāya* for amusement (m. dat. sg.) · *na* not · *madāya* for intoxication · *na* not · *maṇḍanāya* for beautifying · *na* not · *vibhūsanāya* for adorning
- "'**Not** for amusement, **not** for intoxication, **not** for beautifying, **not** for adorning …'"

The first member takes *n'eva*, as the first member of a positive list takes *c'eva*.

***Mā*** forbids; it does not deny, and it does not take an imperative. **It takes the aorist**:

- '**Mā** evaṁ, āvuso sāti, **avaca**, **mā** bhagavantaṁ **abbhācikkhi**.' (MN 38 — M i 256)
- *mā* do not · *evaṁ* thus · *āvuso* friend (voc.) · *sāti* Sāti (voc.) · *avaca* you said (2 sg. aor.) · *mā* do not · *bhagavantaṁ* the Blessed One (m. acc. sg.) · *abbhācikkhi* you misrepresented (2 sg. aor.)
- "'**Do not** speak so, friend Sāti, **do not** misrepresent the Blessed One.'"

*Avaca* and *abbhācikkhi* are aorists, and the sentence means neither 'you did not say' nor 'you were not saying'. It means 'do not say'. The aorist here has lost its past reference entirely, and what it has gained it has gained from the particle. Chapter 8 reaches the same rule from the tense's side, where it stands as the one exception to the aorist being a past tense at all. If you have learnt the aorist as the past tense, *mā* is the signal to stop reading it that way.

***Alaṁ*** means 'enough', and by extension 'enough of', a refusal. With a dative it means 'fit for, sufficient for', which is the sense in the Saṁyutta's definition of what makes someone a speaker on the Dhamma:

- 'Dhammakathiko bhikkhū'ti **alaṁvacanāya**. (SN 12.16 — S ii 18)
- *dhammakathiko* speaker on the Dhamma (m. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhu* monk (m. nom. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *alaṁ* enough, sufficient · *vacanāya* for saying (n. dat. sg.)
- "**Enough** to call him 'a monk who speaks on the Dhamma'."

Negation is also done without a particle at all, by the prefix *a-* (*an-* before a vowel), which the chapter on word formation treats and which every reader meets first in *anicca*, *anattā*, *asaṅkhata*. A negated participle almost always uses the prefix, not *na*.

### Asking

Pāli marks a question with a particle, not with word order, and the particle carries information English puts into intonation.

***Nu***, usually *nu kho*, marks a plain question and expects nothing in particular. ***Nanu***, which is *na* + *nu*, a negative folded into a question, expects the answer yes, as English 'surely you …?' or 'don't you …?' does:[^ind-nanu]

[^ind-nanu]: Duroiselle p. 242: "*Nanu*, used in asking questions to which an affirmative answer is expected. *Nu*, used in asking simple questions." DPD, s.v. *nanu*: "surely?; but wouldn't?; is it true?; wasn't?", from *na* + *nu*.

- **Nanu** tvaṁ sālavatikaṁ ajjhāvasasī'ti? (DN 12 — D i 227)
- *nanu* surely (interrog.) · *tvaṁ* you (nom. sg.) · *sālavatikaṁ* Sālavatikā (f. acc. sg.) · *ajjhāvasasi* you dwell in · *ti* (quotation)
- "**Surely** you live in Sālavatikā?"

The Buddha is asking Lohicca something he already knows, and Lohicca duly answers *Evaṁ, bho Gotama*, 'Just so, master Gotama'. The particle predicted the answer.

***Kacci*** asks a question with concern in it: a hope that all is well, or a fear that it is not:

- '**Kacci** te, tāta, aggi na nibbuto'ti? (DN 23 — D ii 341)
- *kacci* I hope, is it that (interrog.) · *te* your (gen. sg.) · *tāta* dear one (voc.) · *aggi* fire (m. nom. sg.) · *na* not · *nibbuto* gone out (m. nom. sg. p.p.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "'**I trust**, my dear, that the fire has not gone out?'"

***Su***, and its doubled form ***ssu***, never stand as free words in the canon. The particle survives fused to the word in front, and a reader meets it constantly in *kudāssu nāma*, 'when, oh when', the Bodhisatta's question at the head of dependent origination:

- **Kudāssu** nāma imassa dukkhassa nissaraṇaṁ paññāyissati jarāmaraṇassā'ti? (SN 12.10 — S ii 10)
- *kudā* when · *ssu* (emphatic) · *nāma* indeed · *imassa* of this (n. gen. sg.) · *dukkhassa* of suffering (n. gen. sg.) · *nissaraṇaṁ* escape (n. nom. sg.) · *paññāyissati* will be discerned (3 sg. fut. pass.) · *jarāmaraṇassa* of ageing-and-death (n. gen. sg.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "**When, oh when** will an escape be discerned from this suffering, from ageing and death?"

*Kudā* is not otherwise printed on its own in the root texts. It exists there in this compound and one or two like it, which fairly illustrates what "enclitic" costs anyone who searches for the bare word and concludes it does not exist.

### The fillers

The Saddanīti's fourth class is ***pūraṇattha***, 'for the purpose of filling', and it divides in two. ***Padapūraṇa*** particles fill out the **line**; ***atthapūraṇa*** particles fill out the **meaning**.[^ind-puranattha] It is the first kind that a reader needs to be told about, because they include several of the commonest words in the canon and because the natural instinct on meeting them is the wrong one.

[^ind-puranattha]: Collins §4.1(iv), translating Saddanīti: "*pūraṇatthaṁ duvidhaṁ: padapūraṇañca atthapūraṇañca*, (a *nipāta* used) as a filler can be of two kinds: filling up a verse or sentence or filling out the meaning." His list of *padapūraṇa* particles runs *atha*, *atho*, *assu*, *ā*, *enaṁ*, *kahaṁ*, *kīva*, *khalu*, *kho*, *carahi*, *tato*, *naṁ*, *pana*, *yagghe*, *yathā*, *vata*, *ve*, *sudaṁ*, *seyyathidaṁ*, *have*, *hi*.

***Kho*** is the clearest case: thirty-one thousand occurrences in the root texts, and no translation. It is faintly emphatic, it is enclitic, and in the overwhelming majority of its appearances (*atha kho*, *evaṁ kho*, *yo kho*, *tena kho pana samayena*) the right rendering is nothing at all. The same holds, in varying degrees, for ***hi***, ***pana***, ***vata***, ***ve*** and ***have***, ***khalu***, ***assu*** and ***sudaṁ***.

The tradition's account of why they exist is not the account a reader would guess: they are there for the sound. Collins ends the section by saying so: the particles join words together "without expressing a specific meaning", and although their semantic value may seem small, "they contribute to the sound and rhythm of the sentence, especially in verse."[^ind-rhythm] In a literature composed to be recited and memorised, a syllable that fills out a line has a use. Its use is not to mean something.

[^ind-rhythm]: Collins pp. 121, 123. The Pāli phrase he is translating at (iv.a) is *asati atthavisesābhidhāne vacasiliṭṭhatāya*, roughly 'for smoothness of speech, there being no special sense expressed'.

- **Tena sudaṁ**, bhante, devā tāvatiṁsā attamanā honti pamuditā pītisomanassajātā. (DN 19 — D ii 221)
- *tena* by that (n. instr. sg.) · *sudaṁ* (emphatic) · *bhante* sir (voc.) · *devā* gods (m. nom. pl.) · *tāvatiṁsā* of the Thirty-Three · *attamanā* pleased (m. nom. pl.) · *honti* become · *pamuditā* delighted · *pītisomanassajātā* filled with rapture and joy
- "**At that**, sir, the gods of the Thirty-Three became pleased, delighted, filled with rapture and joy."

*Sudaṁ* contributes emphasis and two syllables, but nothing that can be put into English.[^ind-sudam] The instruction that follows from all this is a negative one, and it saves a great deal of wasted effort: when a short word will not yield a meaning, consider that it may not have one. Look instead at what it does to the rhythm of the sentence.

[^ind-sudam]: DPD gives two entries: *sudaṁ* the emphatic indeclinable, Sanskrit *svid*; and *sudaṁ* the sandhi form of *su* + *idaṁ*, 'indeed this'. The second is why the word sometimes appears to be doing more than emphasis, as at MN 12's *tapassī sudaṁ homi*.

A few of the fillers do carry meaning, and two have to be learnt as items. ***Nāma*** does two quite separate jobs: after a proper name it marks the name as a name (*assapuraṁ nāma aṅgānaṁ nigamo*, 'a town of the Aṅgas called Assapura'), and elsewhere it is simply emphatic, 'certainly, for sure'.[^ind-nama] ***Kira*** reports at second hand ('it is said', 'apparently', 'so they say'), and a sentence carrying it is not the speaker's own assertion.[^ind-kira] Missing *kira* is a real misreading, not a lost nuance.

[^ind-nama]: DPD, s.v. *nāma*, separates the emphatic indeclinable ('certainly, truly, for sure') from the adverbial 'called, means, by the name of, namely', giving the Cūḷa-Assapura's opening formula for the second. *Nāma* is also the ordinary noun 'name', and in the grammarians' vocabulary it is the word for the whole noun class, the *nāma* of *nāma/ākhyāta/upasagga/nipāta* above.

[^ind-kira]: DPD gives *kira* two senses, 'really, truly' and 'it is said, allegedly, apparently, people say'; it is the Pāli of Sanskrit *kila*, and so some grammars print *kila* beside it. *Kila* itself occurs nowhere in the canon or the commentaries. Note also that *kira* is a homograph of an inflected form of *kirati* 'scatters'.

The naming *nāma* has a second face, and a reader of the Vinaya meets it constantly. Placed after an ordinary term instead of a proper name, it heads a definition: *gāmo nāma* is '*gāmo* **means**', not 'a village called'. It is how the Vibhaṅga's word-analysis states a definition. Chapter 2 showed how such an entry opens, with the lemma welded to the quotative: *yo panāti*. But the quotative is only one of the two markers the analysis uses, and the choice between them is not free.

The rule against theft opens *Yo pana bhikkhu gāmā vā araññā vā adinnaṁ theyyasaṅkhātaṁ ādiyeyya* (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 46), 'if a monk should take from a village or a forest, by way of theft, what is not given', and continues *yathārūpe adinnādāne rājāno coraṁ gahetvā …* (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 46). Its analysis works through that sentence in order, twenty lemmas of it. Set beside the words being analysed, the first nine look like this:

| The rule's word | The lemma |
|---|---|
| *yo pana* | *yo panāti* |
| *bhikkhu* (nom.) | *bhikkhūti* |
| *gāmā* (abl.) | *gāmo nāma* |
| *araññā* (abl.) | *araññaṁ nāma* |
| *adinnaṁ* (acc.) | *adinnaṁ nāma* |
| *theyyasaṅkhātaṁ* (acc.) | *theyyasaṅkhātanti* |
| *ādiyeyya* (3 sg. opt.) | *ādiyeyyāti* |
| *yathārūpe* (loc.) | *yathārūpaṁ nāma* |
| *coraṁ* (acc.) | *coro nāma* |

A lemma marked with the quotative stands in the form the rule gave it: an accusative stays accusative, an optative stays an optative. A lemma marked with *nāma* stands in the nominative, and where the rule had another case the analysis has changed it without saying so: ablative *gāmā* cited as *gāmo*, locative *yathārūpe* as *yathārūpaṁ*, accusative *coraṁ* as *coro*. Eighty-eight lemmas of this kind stand in the Vinaya texts read for this book, and every one of them is a citation form: no oblique case anywhere, and the single verb among them put into the third person present, so that the rule's optative *paṭiseveyya* is cited as *paṭisevati nāma*.[^ind-nama-citation] The quotative reproduces the wording. *Nāma* names the word.

Where the rule's word was already nominative the two markers overlap, and which one an entry takes is then not something the text explains: *bhikkhu* is quoted, *rājāno* is named.

An entry closes as it opened, with a demonstrative added:

- **Coro nāma** yo pañcamāsakaṁ vā atirekapañcamāsakaṁ vā agghanakaṁ adinnaṁ theyyasaṅkhātaṁ ādiyati. **Eso coro nāma**. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 47)
- *coro* thief (m. nom. sg.) · *nāma* means · *yo* who (m. nom. sg.) · *pañcamāsakaṁ* five small coins (m. acc. sg.) · *vā* or · *atirekapañcamāsakaṁ* more than five small coins · *vā* or · *agghanakaṁ* being worth (m. acc. sg.) · *adinnaṁ* what is not given (n. acc. sg.) · *theyyasaṅkhātaṁ* reckoned as theft (n. acc. sg.) · *ādiyati* takes (3 sg. pres.) · *eso* this (m. nom. sg.) · *coro* thief · *nāma* means
- "'**Thief**' **means**: one who takes, by way of theft, what is not given and is worth five small coins or more. **That is what 'thief' means.**"

The term stands at the head of its own definition and again at the foot, so an entry is bounded at both ends by the word it defines.

One idiom has to be kept out of all this, because the *nāma* in it has stopped being a separate word. ***Appeva nāma*** 'perhaps, if only' is *api* + *eva* + *nāma*, three indeclinables fused into one, and DPD lemmatises the whole of it.[^ind-appeva] It introduces a hope or an intention, and is regularly followed by an optative:

- … amūlakena pārājikena dhammena anuddhaṁseyya — '**appeva nāma** naṁ imamhā brahmacariyā **cāveyyan**'ti … (Bu Ss 8 — Vin iii 163)
- *amūlakena* groundless (m. instr. sg.) · *pārājikena* entailing expulsion (m. instr. sg.) · *dhammena* with a charge (m. instr. sg.) · *anuddhaṁseyya* he should charge (3 sg. opt.) · *appeva nāma* perhaps · *naṁ* him (m. acc. sg., enclitic) · *imamhā* from this (n. abl. sg.) · *brahmacariyā* spiritual life (n. abl. sg.) · *cāveyyaṁ* I might make fall away (1 sg. opt. caus.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "… should charge him groundlessly with an offence entailing expulsion, thinking '**perhaps** I can **make him fall away** from this spiritual life' …"

The *nāma* there names nothing and the *eva* emphasises nothing; the sense belongs to the three of them together. Look an idiom of this kind up whole, or not at all.

[^ind-nama-citation]: Counted across the Vinaya texts cached for this book, eighty-eight lemma lines of the form *X nāma* head a word-analysis entry. Every one stands in a citation form, the nominative singular or plural for a nominal, across *-o*, *-aṁ*, *-ā*, *-i*, *-ī* and *-u* stems alike, and none is in an oblique case. The single verb among them is *paṭisevati nāma* (Bu Pj 1 — Vin iii 28), which analyses the *paṭiseveyya* of the rule at Bu Pj 1 — Vin iii 23.

[^ind-appeva]: DPD lemmatises *appevanāma*, 'it would be good if; perhaps; hopefully; if only', as a sandhi compound of *api* + *eva* + *nāma*, and *appeva* on its own, from *api* + *eva*, with much the same sense. The doubled *pp* is a join of the kind Chapter 2 describes.

### The adverb, which Pāli does not have

English has an adverb because English marks one: *kind* becomes *kindly*. Pāli has no such class and no such marker, and the tradition says so: the category does not exist in Pāli or Sanskrit grammar.[^ind-no-adverb] What it has instead is ***kiriyāvisesana***, 'specification of the action', and within that a subclass called ***bhāvanapuṁsaka***, 'the neuter [specifying] a state', which is to say the neuter accusative used adverbially.

[^ind-no-adverb]: Collins §4.2: "Many of the words cited by Aggavaṁsa as examples of *nipāta* would be classified as adverbs by western grammar. There is no such category in Pali or Sanskrit. The category of *kiriyāvisesana* (specification of the action of the verb) contains a sub-category corresponding to that of adverb; words used adverbially in the neuter accusative are called *bhāvanapuṁsaka* (neuter [specifying a] state)."

That is more than a difference in terminology. It says that a Pāli adverb is a **use** of a word, not a kind of word, and it predicts what the reader will find: three quite different things all serving as adverbs. Duroiselle's division of them is the clearest.[^ind-three-groups]

[^ind-three-groups]: Duroiselle §530: "Adverbs may be divided into three groups: (i) Derivative Adverbs formed by means of suffixes, (ii) Case-form Adverbs, (iii) Pure Adverbs."

#### Derived by suffix

A closed set of suffixes attaches to pronominal stems, and to nouns, adjectives and numerals, to make words of place, time and manner. These are best learnt as a system, not as vocabulary, because the same suffix on a different stem is predictable.

| Suffix | Sense | On pronominal stems |
|---|---|---|
| *-tra*, *-ttha* | place, 'where' | *tatra*, *yatra*, *atra*, *aññatra*; *tattha*, *yattha*, *kattha*, *ettha*, *aññattha*, *sabbattha* |
| *-to* | place, 'whence' (ablative sense) | *ito*, *tato*, *yato*, *kuto*, *sabbato* |
| *-thā*, *-thaṁ* | manner, 'how' | *yathā*, *tathā*, *aññathā*, *sabbathā*; *kathaṁ*, *itthaṁ* |
| *-dā* | time, 'when' | *tadā*, *yadā*, *kadā*, *sabbadā* |
| *-dhi* | place | *sabbadhi* |
| *-rahi* | time | *etarahi*, *carahi* |
| *-hiṁ*, *-haṁ* | place | *kuhiṁ*, *tahiṁ*, *tahaṁ*, *kahaṁ* |
| *-dha*, *-ha* | place, 'here' | *idha*; *iha* (rare) |

The interrogatives in that table are the ones to watch. *Kattha* and *kuhiṁ* are the canon's ordinary words for 'where?'; the forms *kutra* and *kuttha* that some grammars print beside them belong to the grammarians' own texts and not to the canon.[^ind-kutra]

[^ind-kutra]: *Kutra* and *kuttha* do not occur in the canonical or commentarial texts of the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana, only in the grammatical literature; against them, *kattha* has 549 root-text occurrences and *kuhiṁ* 79. Perniola §104(a), pp. 130–131, prints both without marking the difference, as he prints *yathaṁ* beside *kathaṁ* and *itthaṁ*; and *yathaṁ* occurs nowhere in the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana at all.

On nouns, adjectives and numerals the productive suffixes are ***-to*** again (*dakkhiṇato* 'to the south', *pācīnato* 'to the east', *piṭṭhito* 'from behind', *parato* 'beyond'), ***-so*** distributively or manner-wise (*yoniso* 'from the root, wisely', *bhāgaso* 'by portions', *sahasā* 'suddenly'), ***-dhā*** 'in so many ways' (*pañcadhā* 'fivefold', *bahudhā* 'in many ways'), and ***-khattuṁ*** 'so many times':

- … añjaliṁ paṇāmetvā **tikkhattuṁ** citakaṁ padakkhiṇaṁ katvā bhagavato pāde sirasā vandi. (DN 16 — D ii 163)
- *añjaliṁ* joined palms (m. acc. sg.) · *paṇāmetvā* having raised (absol.) · *ti-* three · *khattuṁ* times · *citakaṁ* the pyre (m. acc. sg.) · *padakkhiṇaṁ* keeping to the right (n. acc. sg., adverbial) · *katvā* having done (absol.) · *bhagavato* of the Blessed One (m. gen. sg.) · *pāde* the feet (m. acc. pl.) · *sirasā* with his head (n. instr. sg.) · *vandi* he paid homage (3 sg. aor.)
- "… and raising his joined palms, having circled the pyre **three times** keeping it to his right, he paid homage with his head at the Blessed One's feet."

That sentence carries two of the three groups at once: *tikkhattuṁ* is derived by suffix, and *padakkhiṇaṁ* is an ordinary accusative doing adverbial work.

#### Frozen case forms

Any oblique case can be used adverbially, and the chapter on gender and adjectives has already taught the commonest instance, the accusative singular of an adjective: *sādhukaṁ* 'well', *niccaṁ* 'constantly', *ciraṁ* 'for a long time'. The generalisation is that the other five oblique cases do the same thing, each with a characteristic sense.[^ind-case-adverbs]

[^ind-case-adverbs]: Duroiselle §532 and Perniola §104(c), pp. 131–132. Duroiselle notes at §532(e) that "some adverbs of obscure origin may be classed as the accusative case of nouns or adjectives long obsolete" (*tuṇhī* 'silently', *sāyaṁ* 'in the evening', *jātu* 'surely'), which is as far as one can go with forms whose noun no longer exists.

| Case | Sense | Specimens |
|---|---|---|
| Accusative | manner, extent, duration | *sukhaṁ*, *dukkhaṁ*, *khippaṁ*, *ciraṁ*, *saccaṁ*, *rattiṁ*, *divasaṁ* |
| Instrumental | manner, means | *dhammena* 'rightly', *anupubbena* 'gradually', *kicchena* 'with difficulty', *vegena* 'in haste', *divā* 'by day' |
| Dative | purpose | *atthāya* 'for the sake of', *hitāya* 'for the benefit of' |
| Ablative | cause, position | *tasmā* 'therefore', *kasmā* 'why', *pacchā* 'afterwards', *heṭṭhā* 'below' |
| Genitive | time elapsed | *cirassaṁ* 'at long last' |
| Locative | place, time | *dūre* 'far off', *santike* 'near', *pubbe* 'formerly', *bāhire* 'outside' |

Several of these are so thoroughly frozen that a reader has no reason to think of them as case forms at all. *Divā* 'by day' and *ratto* 'by night' are an old instrumental and an old locative respectively. They are two different cases, and that makes the pairing look odd until one knows that neither is functioning as a case any longer:[^ind-diva-ratto]

[^ind-diva-ratto]: Perniola p. 132: "The adverbs *divā*, *ratto* are derived directly from Sanskrit and are an instrumental and a locative case respectively." He adds that from the genitive *cirassa* a new adverb has been formed with the accusative neuter ending, *cirassaṁ*, the second form being much the commoner of the two in the canon.

- Tesaṁ **divā** ca **ratto** ca, sadā puññaṁ pavaḍḍhati. (SN 1.47 — S i 33)
- *tesaṁ* for them (m. gen. pl.) · *divā* by day · *ca* and · *ratto* by night · *ca* and · *sadā* always · *puññaṁ* merit (n. nom. sg.) · *pavaḍḍhati* grows
- "For them, **by day and by night**, merit constantly grows."

***Yathābhūtaṁ*** 'in accordance with what has come to be' stands in the root texts nearly two thousand times, and the word after it is almost always a verb of knowing or seeing.[^ind-yathabhutam] It is a frozen accusative of the kind this section describes, and the *-ṁ* on the end of it stopped being a case ending long ago. Take it for the object of the verb and you will go looking for a noun that is not there.

[^ind-yathabhutam]: Counted across the root texts of the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana, 1,938 occurrences, of which 1,855 have a following word. *Pajānāti* and its negative *nappajānāti* account for 912 between them; adding the plurals, *jānāti*, *abbhaññāsiṁ*, *ñāṇaṁ* and *sammappaññāya* (which itself stands before a verb of seeing), brings the total to 1,626, or 88 per cent.

- Taṁ sutavā ariyasāvako **yathābhūtaṁ** pajānāti. (AN 1.52 — A i 10)
- *taṁ* that (n. acc. sg.) · *sutavā* learned (m. nom. sg.) · *ariyasāvako* noble disciple (m. nom. sg.) · *yathābhūtaṁ* in accordance with what has come to be · *pajānāti* understands (3 sg. pres.)
- "A learned noble disciple understands that **as it really is**."

*Pajānāti* has its object already, in *taṁ*; *yathābhūtaṁ* is saying how the understanding is done. It is frozen from a whole compound, not a noun, and so the chapter on compounds returns to it and gives the class a name. Here it is enough that it never changes shape, never agrees with anything, and modifies the verb. It is an adverb, and it will never be anything else.

#### Neither

Duroiselle's third group is the residue, and naming it is more useful than it looks: these are the adverbs "which are not obtained by derivation and which are not case forms".[^ind-pure-adverbs] *Kira*, *hi*, *khalu*, *tu*, *atha* and *atho*, *na* and *mā*, *nanu*, *nu*, *no*, *nūna*, *nānā*, *kva*: most of the fillers, that is, and most of the question particles. They have no analysis. They are simply words, and the reason a reader cannot break them down is that there is nothing to break down.

[^ind-pure-adverbs]: Duroiselle §531(iii), p. 241. Of the forms he lists there, *tu* needs a caveat: it has 29 root-text occurrences against 2,714 outside the canon, so it is a particle of the later literature that a reader will meet chiefly in the commentaries and the chronicles.

### The preposition, and what replaced it

Perniola opens his section on prepositions with a sentence that is more useful than a list would be: "In Pāli the prepositions have practically disappeared."[^ind-prep-disappeared] The old prepositions have become **prefixes**, joined onto verbs and nouns, and a handful (*anu*, *pati*, *adhi*) survive as free words in a few rare constructions and no more.

[^ind-prep-disappeared]: Perniola §105, p. 132. Duroiselle reaches the same conclusion from the other direction at §533: "It has been seen that Verbal Prefixes are properly prepositions and are used with nouns as well as verbs."

The work does not disappear with them. It is redistributed four ways, and all four need recognising because they look like different things on the page.

**Adverbs used prepositionally.** An adverb governs a noun in a fixed case, and which case it governs is a property of the word that has to be learnt with it:

| Governing | Postpositions |
|---|---|
| Accusative | *antarā*, *pacchā*, *yāva*, *vinā*, *samantā* |
| Instrumental | *aññatra*, *vinā*, *saddhiṁ*, *saha* |
| Genitive | *heṭṭhā*, *antarena*, *upari*, *pure*, *samantato*, *avidūre* |
| Ablative | *ārā*, *ārakā*, *uddhaṁ*, *oraṁ*, *paraṁ*, *pacchā*, *yāva*, *adho* |

That *vinā* 'without' and *pacchā* 'after' and *yāva* 'as far as' appear twice is not an error in the table. Several of them govern more than one case, which is the ordinary state of affairs for a word that is an adverb first and a preposition only by use.

- … **yāva** brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṁ vatteti. (DN 2 — D i 78)
- *yāva* as far as · *brahmalokā* the Brahmā world (m. abl. sg.) · *pi* even · *kāyena* with the body (m. instr. sg.) · *vasaṁ* mastery (m. acc. sg.) · *vatteti* he exercises
- "… he exercises bodily mastery **as far as** the Brahmā world."

**Nouns used prepositionally.** A noun of time, place, cause or manner takes a genitive, or forms a *tappurisa* compound with the word it governs, and the pair then reads as a prepositional phrase: *santike* 'in the presence of', *hetu* and *nidānaṁ* 'by reason of', *vasena* 'by way of', *atthāya* 'for the sake of', *accayena* 'after the lapse of'.

**Absolutives used as postpositions.** *Paṭicca* 'dependent on', *ārabbha* 'concerning', *uddissa* 'with reference to', *nissāya* 'depending on', *ādāya* 'taking', *ṭhapetvā* 'excepting': all are treated in the chapter on absolutives and infinitives, where they are formed. What belongs here is the government, and it is simple: **they take the accusative.**[^ind-absol-government]

[^ind-absol-government]: Perniola p. 133 states the rule with one exception: "Of these past gerunds, *paṭṭhāya* governs the ablative case, all the others govern the accusative case." *Paṭṭhāya* is left out of the list above for the same reason the chapter on absolutives left it out of its own: of its eighty root-text occurrences, seventy-nine are the Visuddhimagga's and the remaining one is the Paṭṭhāna naming its own title. It is a postposition of the commentarial language, not of the canon.

**Other verbal forms.** A participle or an adjective sometimes carries the sense a preposition would: *sahitaṁ* 'together with', *sampanno* 'endowed with', *gato* 'gone to, regarding'.

### Calling out

Interjections are words indicating feelings of joy, sadness or anger, or words used to catch someone's attention, and Pāli has a good stock of them, most of which have no analysis and simply have to be recognised.[^ind-interjections] ***Aho*** expresses surprise or longing, usually paired with *vata*; ***re***, ***je*** and ***ambho*** address someone abruptly; ***yagghe*** and ***taggha*** are respectful, the first calling for attention and the second conceding a point. ***Bho*** 'sir' is not strictly one of them at all. It is the vocative of *bhavant*, and so a declined form, not an indeclinable; but it functions as an address particle and a reader meets it constantly in the mouths of brahmins.[^ind-bho]

[^ind-bho]: DPD, s.v. *bho*: "nom & voc sg & pl of *bhavant*, irreg", glossed "respectful address; especially between Brahmans". Duroiselle §§333–335 goes further and treats *bhavaṁ*, *ayya* and *āvuso* as a small class of pronominals, noting that *bhavaṁ* takes a third-person verb.

[^ind-interjections]: Perniola §107, p. 134: "Interjections are words indicating feelings of joy, sadness, anger, or words used to call the attention of someone: *aho*, *je*, *re*, *dhi*, *iṅgha*, *he*, *aho nūna*, *aho vata*, *yagghe*, *taggha*, *ambho*. As interjections are also used *maññe*, *bhaṇe* 'I think' and 'I say' (first person *attanopada* of the present indicative)."

- '**Aho vata** mayaṁ na jātidhammā assāma, na ca **vata** no jāti āgaccheyyā'ti. (DN 22 — D ii 307)
- *aho* oh! · *vata* indeed · *mayaṁ* we (nom. pl.) · *na* not · *jātidhammā* liable to birth (m. nom. pl.) · *assāma* we might be (1 pl. opt.) · *na* not · *ca* and · *vata* indeed · *no* to us (dat. pl.) · *jāti* birth (f. nom. sg.) · *āgaccheyya* might come (3 sg. opt.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "'**Oh, if only** we were not liable to birth, and **would that** birth might not come to us!'"

Three of them turn out on inspection to be verbs that have stopped conjugating. ***Bhaṇe*** 'I say!' and ***maññe*** 'I think' are first-person middle presents used as asides, and ***iṅgha*** 'come on!' is a second-person imperative of *iñjati* 'stirs, moves', frozen in that form.[^ind-ingha] ***Handa*** 'come now!' has no such parse but behaves like them, and it commonly stands in front of a first-person plural, which is how a Pāli speaker says 'let us'.

[^ind-ingha]: DPD, s.v. *iṅgha*: "*ind*, *excl*, imp 2nd sg of *iñjati*", from √*iñj*.

- **Iṅgha** tvaṁ, bhaggava, yo ca te ayaṁ mayi pasādo, tameva tvaṁ sādhukamanurakkhā'ti. (DN 24 — D iii 35)
- *iṅgha* come on! · *tvaṁ* you (nom. sg.) · *bhaggava* Bhaggava (voc.) · *yo* which (m. nom. sg.) · *te* your (gen. sg.) · *ayaṁ* this · *mayi* in me (loc. sg.) · *pasādo* confidence (m. nom. sg.) · *tameva* < *taṁ* that (m. acc. sg.) · *eva* just · *sādhukamanurakkhāti* < *sādhukaṁ* well (adv.) · *anurakkha* guard! (2 sg. imper.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "**Come now**, Bhaggava, guard well that confidence of yours in me."

Two of this chapter's own rules are visible in the printing of that last word. *Sādhukaṁ* + *anurakkha* joins across the *ṁ*, and the imperative's final *-a* then lengthens to *-ā* before the quotative *ti*. So *sādhukamanurakkhā'ti* is three words, and none of the joins is optional.

- **Handa** mayaṁ, āvuso, tathā vihāraṁ kappema yathā no viharataṁ bhagavā attamano assā'ti. (Ud 3.3 — Ud p. 25)
- *handa* come now! · *mayaṁ* we (nom. pl.) · *āvuso* friends (voc. pl.) · *tathā* in such a way · *vihāraṁ* dwelling (m. acc. sg.) · *kappema* let us arrange (1 pl. imper.) · *yathā* so that · *no* our (gen. pl.) · *viharataṁ* of those dwelling (m. gen. pl. pres. part.) · *bhagavā* the Blessed One (m. nom. sg.) · *attamano* pleased (m. nom. sg.) · *assa* might be (3 sg. opt.) · *ti* (quotation)
- "**Come now**, friends, let us so arrange our living that the Blessed One will be pleased with us as we live."

### Quotation: *iti*

The indeclinable *iti* 'thus' marks the end of a quotation. It stands *after* what is quoted, where English puts a closing quotation mark.

- Appahāya pañca verāni, dussīlo **iti** vuccati. (AN 5.174 — A iii 205)
- *appahāya* not having abandoned (absol.) · *pañca* five · *verāni* enmities (n. acc. pl.) · *dussīlo* of poor virtue (m. nom. sg.) · *iti* thus (quotation) · *vuccati* one is called (3 sg. pres. pass.)
- "Not having abandoned the five enmities, one is called '**of poor virtue**'."

Pāli manuscripts carry no quotation marks; the ones printed in modern editions are the editors' addition, so the example above is usually printed *'Dussīlo' iti vuccati*. The word almost always assimilates to what precedes it, and editors place the closing mark inside the assimilation:

- *dussīlo* + *iti* → *dussīloti* — printed '*dussīlo*'*ti*
- *gacchati* + *iti* → *gacchatīti* — printed '… *gacchatī*'*ti*
- *sukhaṁ* + *iti* → *sukhanti* — printed '… *sukhan*'*ti*

Before *ti* a final vowel is lengthened and a final *ṁ* becomes *n*. Going the other way, before a vowel *iti* itself becomes *icc-*: *iti* + *evaṁ* → *iccevaṁ*. And the sequence *ti* + *eva* is printed *tveva* throughout the editions, for reasons nobody has satisfactorily explained.[^ind-iti-sandhi]

[^ind-iti-sandhi]: Collins p. 141: "When followed by a vowel *iti* → *icc*, *iti eva* → *icceva*, and also (for reasons not known) *ti eva* appears often in manuscripts as *tveva*." *Tveva* has 331 root-text occurrences, so it is not a scribal rarity.

- '*Parimuccati dukkhasmā*'**ti** vadāmī'**ti**. (SN 22.147 — S iii 179)
- *parimuccati* he is freed (3 sg. pres.) · *dukkhasmā* from suffering (n. abl. sg.) · *ti* thus (quotation) · *vadāmi* I say (1 sg. pres.) · *ti* thus (quotation)
- "'I say, '**He is freed from suffering**.''"

That line carries two of them, one closing the Buddha's words and one closing the reported speech that contains them, and so *vadāmi* has its final vowel lengthened to *vadāmī*. Nested quotation is ordinary in the canon and the only mark of it is the stacking of *ti*.

Pāli has no indirect speech, so a quotation marked by *iti* covers both what English reports directly and what it reports indirectly. The sentence above can equally be rendered "I say *that* he is freed from suffering", and the choice is the translator's, not the grammar's.

- '*Dīghaṁ assasāmī*'**ti** pajānāti. (MN 118 — M iii 82)
- *dīghaṁ* long (adv.) · *assasāmi* I breathe in · *ti* thus (quotation) · *pajānāti* he understands
- "He knows, '**I breathe in long**.'" — or: he knows *that* he is breathing in long.

*Iti* marks more than speech. It marks thoughts, intentions, namings and acts of recognition, and the verb governing it is often left to be supplied in translation.

- … susumāro āviñcheyya '*udakaṁ pavekkhāmī*'**ti** … (SN 35.247 — S iv 199)
- *susumāro* the crocodile (m. nom. sg.) · *āviñcheyya* would pull (3 sg. opt.) · *udakaṁ* the water (n. acc. sg.) · *pavekkhāmi* I shall enter (1 sg. fut.) · *ti* thus (quotation)
- "The crocodile would pull, **thinking**, 'I will enter the water.'"

- … so taṁ udapānaṁ olokeyya. Tassa '*udaka*'**n**ti hi kho ñāṇaṁ assa, na ca kāyena phusitvā vihareyya. (SN 12.68 — S ii 118)
- *so* he (m. nom. sg.) · *taṁ* that (m. acc. sg.) · *udapānaṁ* well (m. acc. sg.) · *olokeyya* would look into (3 sg. opt.) · *tassa* for him (gen. sg.) · *udakaṁ* water (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* thus (quotation) · *hi kho* indeed · *ñāṇaṁ* knowledge (n. nom. sg.) · *assa* would be (3 sg. opt.) · *na* not · *ca* and · *kāyena* with the body (m. instr. sg.) · *phusitvā* having touched (absol.) · *vihareyya* would dwell (3 sg. opt.)
- "… he would look down into that well. He would have the knowledge, '**water**', and yet would not dwell touching it with his body."

- Vadhakañca pana santaṁ na aññāsi: '*vadhako me*'**ti**. (SN 22.85 — S iii 113)
- *vadhakaṁ* murderer (m. acc. sg.) · *ca* and · *pana* moreover · *santaṁ* being (m. acc. sg. pres. part.) · *na* not · *aññāsi* he knew (3 sg. aor.) · *vadhako* murderer (m. nom. sg.) · *me* my (gen. sg.) · *ti* thus (quotation)
- "And though he was a murderer, he did not recognise him **as** 'my murderer'."

A quoted phrase can be treated as a single grammatical unit inside the sentence that contains it, and the last two depend on that: '*udaka*'*ti* stands as the content of *ñāṇaṁ*, and '*vadhako me*'*ti* as the content of *aññāsi*.

The tradition goes considerably further than this. Aggavaṁsa notes that *iti* is ***anekatthappabheda***, 'of many different senses', and distinguishes seven.[^ind-iti-seven] Two of them a reader meets constantly and cannot reach from the quotation account.

[^ind-iti-seven]: Saddanīti 317, translated with its illustrative passages in Collins pp. 141–142. His seven are *hetu* (cause, reason), *parisamāpana* (bringing to completion), *ādi* (beginning with, 'such as'), *padatthavipariyāya* (variation in the meanings of near-synonymous words), *pakāra* (manner), *avadhāraṇa* (affirmation) and *nidassana* (illustration).

The first is ***hetu***, cause: *iti* attaches to a clause and makes it the reason for what follows, and the Khandhasaṁyutta's definition of form is the standing example.

- Ruppatī'**ti** kho, bhikkhave, tasmā '*rūpa*'**n**ti vuccati. (SN 22.79 — S iii 86)
- *ruppati* it is deformed (3 sg. pres.) · *ti* (causal) · *kho* indeed · *bhikkhave* monks (voc. pl.) · *tasmā* therefore (abl.) · *rūpaṁ* form (n. nom. sg.) · *ti* thus (quotation) · *vuccati* it is called (3 sg. pres. pass.)
- "**Because** it is deformed, monks, it is therefore called '**form**'."

Two *iti*s in one short sentence, with two different jobs: the first supplies the reason and answers to *tasmā*, the second simply names. The first is not reporting anyone's words.

The second sense is ***ādi***, 'and so on'. A list closed with *iti* or *iti vā* means the list and others like it, and the phrase *iti vā iti evarūpā* 'or anything of such a kind' recurs through the whole of the Sīlakkhandha's catalogue of what the Buddha abstains from: *… iti vā iti evarūpā visūkadassanā paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo'ti* (DN 1 — D i 6), "the ascetic Gotama abstains from unseemly shows or anything of the sort." Reading that *iti* as a quotation mark produces a sentence that will not resolve.

### Where the indeclinables are treated elsewhere

**Prefixes** (*upasagga*, the twenty or so particles that attach to verbs and nouns and cannot stand alone) are in the chapter on word formation, with the full inventory in Appendix A.4. Absolutives that have frozen into postpositions are in the chapter on absolutives and infinitives, where they are formed; this chapter has added only which case each of them governs. **The correlative pairs** *yadā…tadā*, *yattha…tattha*, *yathā…tathā* are in the chapter on relative clauses, where they are used; this chapter has shown only how the forms are made. And **the adverbial accusative** was introduced in the chapter on gender and adjectives, which promised that this one would return to it; it has. Adverbs frozen out of a whole compound, *yathābhūtaṁ* above among them, are analysed in the chapter on compounds, which names the class they belong to; this chapter has taken them only as words a reader meets.

What is left over is the vocabulary, and there is no way around it. About two hundred of these words exist, and a dictionary is a better guide to any single one than a grammar can be. What a grammar can give is an outline of the class: that they do not inflect, that most of them fall into one of the tradition's four jobs, that the ones which mean nothing are the ones a reader should stop trying to translate, and that a good half of them are not printed as separate words at all.

The Buddha's last recorded sentence has five of them in it.

- **Handa dāni**, bhikkhave, āmantayāmi vo, vayadhammā saṅkhārā appamādena sampādethā'ti. (DN 16 — D ii 156)
- *handa* come now! · *dāni* now · *bhikkhave* monks (voc. pl.) · *āmantayāmi* I address (1 sg. pres.) · *vo* you (acc. pl., enclitic) · *vayadhammā* subject to decay (m. nom. pl.) · *saṅkhārā* conditions (m. nom. pl.) · *appamādena* with diligence (m. instr. sg.) · *sampādetha* accomplish! (2 pl. imper.) · *ti* thus (quotation)
- "**Come now**, monks, I address you: conditions are subject to decay; accomplish it with diligence."

*Handa*, *dāni*, the enclitic *vo*, the frozen instrumental *appamādena*, and the *ti* that closes the whole of it.

### Reading a whole sutta

That is the end of Part IV, and it is the point at which the front matter says to start reading, alongside the chapters still to come, not after them. Appendix B's pathway begins with three short suttas of the Khandhasaṁyutta, and the first of them is short enough to read here entire. It is the *Yadanicca*, and almost everything in it has now been taught.

- Sāvatthinidānaṁ. Rūpaṁ, bhikkhave, aniccaṁ. Yadaniccaṁ taṁ dukkhaṁ; yaṁ dukkhaṁ tadanattā; yadanattā taṁ 'netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā'ti evametaṁ yathābhūtaṁ sammappaññāya daṭṭhabbaṁ. (SN 22.15 — S iii 22)
- *sāvatthinidānaṁ* at Sāvatthī (an editors' abbreviation, below) · *rūpaṁ*<sub>a</sub> form (n. nom. sg.) · *bhikkhave* monks (m. voc. pl.) · *aniccaṁ*<sub>a</sub> impermanent (n. nom. sg.) · *yadaniccaṁ* < *yad* what (n. nom. sg., relative) + *aniccaṁ* impermanent · *taṁ* that (n. nom. sg.) · *dukkhaṁ* suffering (n. nom. sg.) · *yaṁ* what (n. nom. sg., relative) · *tadanattā* < *tad* that + *anattā* not-self (m. nom. sg.) · *yadanattā* < *yad* what + *anattā* not-self · *taṁ* that (n. nom. sg.) · *netaṁ* < *na* not + *etaṁ* this · *mama* mine (gen. sg.) · *nesohamasmi* < *na* not + *eso* this + *ahaṁ* I + *asmi* am · *na meso attā* < *na* not + *me* my (gen. sg., enclitic) + *eso* this + *attā* self (m. nom. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *evametaṁ* < *evaṁ* thus + *etaṁ* this · *yathābhūtaṁ* as it really is (adv.) · *sammappaññāya* with right wisdom (f. instr. sg.) · *daṭṭhabbaṁ* is to be seen (n. nom. sg., gerundive)
- At Sāvatthī. "Form, monks, is impermanent. What is impermanent is suffering; what is suffering is not-self; and what is not-self should be seen as it really is, with right wisdom: '**This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.**'"

The sutta says the same of feeling, *vedanā aniccā*, in full. Then the page changes:

- Saññā aniccā…pe… saṅkhārā aniccā… viññāṇaṁ aniccaṁ. (SN 22.15 — S iii 22)
- *saññā*<sub>a</sub> perception (f. nom. sg.) · *aniccā*<sub>a</sub> impermanent (f. nom. sg.) · *pe* (the editors' mark of a cut) · *saṅkhārā*<sub>b</sub> volitional formations (m. nom. pl.) · *aniccā*<sub>b</sub> impermanent (m. nom. pl.) · *viññāṇaṁ*<sub>c</sub> consciousness (n. nom. sg.) · *aniccaṁ*<sub>c</sub> impermanent (n. nom. sg.)
- "Perception is impermanent … volitional formations are impermanent … consciousness is impermanent."

Appendix B's first text asks this of a reader before it asks anything grammatical, and no rule of grammar covers it. *Pe* is short for *peyyāla*, which the Digital Pali Dictionary glosses "an elided repetition within a text". The editions write a passage out once and then, where it recurs, print *…pe…* in its place and leave it to the reader to put it back. Here, it stands for the whole of *yadaniccaṁ taṁ dukkhaṁ … daṭṭhabbaṁ* said of perception. The bare ellipsis after *saṅkhārā aniccā* does the same without the label. Read aloud, the cut is restored and the passage recited in full.[^ind-peyyala]

[^ind-peyyala]: Gair and Karunatillake, *A New Course in Reading Pāli*, Lesson II §8, p. 24, where *peyyāla*, shortened to *pe*, is described as a substitute for a stretch of text identical with part of what precedes, to be restored in full when the text is read aloud. Their wording is paraphrased here, not quoted. DPD files *peyyāla* as both a neuter and a masculine noun.

The first word of the sutta is an abbreviation of the same kind. DPD glosses *sāvatthinidānaṁ* '(this) occurred at Sāvatthī', literally 'Sāvatthī is the source', and it stands for the setting, which the editions print in full in some suttas and reduce to this one word in others. Ten suttas earlier in the same *saṁyutta*, that setting stands in full:

- Evaṁ me sutaṁ – ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. (SN 22.5 — S iii 13)
- *evaṁ* thus · *me* by me (1 sg., enclitic) · *sutaṁ* heard (n. nom. sg., p.p.) · *ekaṁ samayaṁ* on one occasion (m. acc. sg., adverbial) · *bhagavā* the Blessed One (m. nom. sg.) · *sāvatthiyaṁ* at Sāvatthī (f. loc. sg.) · *viharati* is staying (3 sg. pres.) · *jetavane* in Jeta's Grove (n. loc. sg.) · *anāthapiṇḍikassa* of Anāthapiṇḍika (m. gen. sg.) · *ārāme* in the monastery (m. loc. sg.)
- "Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery."

Every word of that is now readable. *Me* is the enclitic of Chapter 5, *viharati* the historic present of Chapter 7, *sutaṁ* the past participle of Chapter 10, and *ekaṁ samayaṁ* the frozen accusative of time.

The *Yadanicca* closes with a second cut:

- Evaṁ passaṁ…pe… nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānātī'ti. (SN 22.15 — S iii 22)

and the sutta immediately before it, SN 22.14, prints what was cut:

- Evaṁ passaṁ, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako rūpasmimpi nibbindati, vedanāyapi nibbindati, saññāyapi nibbindati, saṅkhāresupi nibbindati, viññāṇasmimpi nibbindati. Nibbindaṁ virajjati; virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṁ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṁ hoti. 'Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā'ti pajānātī'ti. (SN 22.14 — S iii 21)
- *evaṁ* thus · *passaṁ* seeing (m. nom. sg., pres. part.) · *bhikkhave* monks · *sutavā* learned (m. nom. sg.) · *ariyasāvako* noble disciple (m. nom. sg.) · *rūpasmimpi* < *rūpasmiṁ* with form (n. loc. sg.) + *pi* also · *nibbindati* becomes disenchanted (3 sg. pres.) · *vedanāyapi* < *vedanāya* with feeling (f. loc. sg.) + *pi* · *saññāyapi* with perception · *saṅkhāresupi* with volitional formations (m. loc. pl.) · *viññāṇasmimpi* with consciousness (n. loc. sg.) · *nibbindaṁ* being disenchanted (m. nom. sg., pres. part.) · *virajjati* becomes dispassionate (3 sg. pres.) · *virāgā* through dispassion (m. abl. sg.) · *vimuccati* is freed (3 sg. pres.) · *vimuttasmiṁ* when it is freed (loc. sg., p.p.) · *vimuttamiti* < *vimuttaṁ* freed (n. nom. sg., p.p.) + *iti* thus · *ñāṇaṁ* knowledge (n. nom. sg.) · *hoti* there is · *khīṇā* destroyed (f. nom. sg., p.p.) · *jāti* birth (f. nom. sg.) · *vusitaṁ* lived (n. nom. sg., p.p.) · *brahmacariyaṁ* the spiritual life (n. nom. sg.) · *kataṁ* done (n. nom. sg., p.p.) · *karaṇīyaṁ* what had to be done (n. nom. sg., gerundive) · *na* not · *aparaṁ* further · *itthattāya* for this state of being (n. dat. sg.) · *ti* (quotation) · *pajānāti* he understands · *ti* (quotation)
- "Seeing thus, monks, the learned noble disciple becomes disenchanted with form, with feeling, with perception, with volitional formations, with consciousness. Being disenchanted, he becomes dispassionate; through dispassion he is freed. When it is freed, there is the knowledge: 'It is freed.' He understands: '**Birth is destroyed, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this state of being.**'"

So a sutta of seventy-seven printed words is, restored, more than twice that length.

Look back over what the reading needed. The junctions of Chapter 2 opened *yadaniccaṁ*, *tadanattā*, *nesohamasmi* and *evametaṁ*. Chapter 4's agreement made *aniccaṁ* neuter beside *rūpaṁ*, feminine beside *saññā*, and masculine plural beside *saṅkhārā*, and the last two are spelled alike, so it is the noun that decides.[^ind-anatta] The enclitic *me* and the demonstrative *eso* are Chapter 5's. The *asmi* hidden in *nesohamasmi* is Chapter 7's. *Daṭṭhabbaṁ* 'to be seen' and *passaṁ* 'seeing' are Chapter 10's, and the *ti* that closes each thought is this chapter's.

One thing is still ahead. *Yad … taṁ*, 'what … that', is a relative and its correlative, and the construction belongs to Chapter 17. It has been glossed here as a unit, and it will be easier to learn there for having been read here first. The two suttas that follow this one in the *saṁyutta*, the *Yaṁdukkha* and the *Yadanattā*, are the same frame again, shorter. Read them next, aloud, without the grammar open.

[^ind-anatta]: *Anattā* does not change at all, beside *rūpaṁ* or anything else. DPD files *anattā* under two headwords, as an adjective and as a masculine noun; read as the noun, it is the case Chapter 4 describes, a noun keeping its own gender when it is set beside another to characterise it.
