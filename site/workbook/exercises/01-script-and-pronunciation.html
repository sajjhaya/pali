    Exercises: Script and Pronunciation

    Nothing in this chapter can be learned silently. Every item below is meant to be said aloud before it is written down, and several of them cannot be answered any other way.

    The four stages run in the order the chapter argues in. First, three lines given complete, weights and all, to be heard, not worked out. Then two to recite and then write down, because the diacritics are learned by the hand as much as by the eye. Then the letters and the vowels, from memory: how many letters a word has, which is a different question from how many characters are printed, and whether a vowel is long, where the answer is often “it depends on what follows”. Then whole lines, divided and weighed by you. Every Pāli line is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — script and pronunciation

    Nothing is withheld. Each line below is printed twice: as it stands, and divided into syllables with the heavy ones in bold. These are three of the lines a Theravāda Buddhist hears most often in a lifetime, and the point of having them here is that you should hear the weights in them before you can name one.

    1. Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa. (MN 91 — M ii 140)

      na·mo tas·sa bha·ga·va·to a·ra·ha·to sam·mā·sam·bud·dhas·sa

      “Homage to him, the Blessed One, the perfected one, the fully awakened one.”

      Say it three times at chanting pace, giving every heavy syllable about twice the length of a light one. Then, without looking, say where the light syllables run longest without a heavy one among them, and where the heavy ones do the same.

    2. Suṇātu me, bhante, saṅgho. (Kd 1 — Vin i 95)

      su·ṇā·tu me bhan·te saṅ·gho

      suṇātulet it listen3 sg impmeto medat./gen. sgbhantevenerable sirsvocsaṅghothe Sangham nom sg

      “Let the Sangha listen to me, venerable sirs.”

      This is the opening of every formal act of the Sangha. Say it aloud. Only two syllables in it are light; find them. Then say why the e of bhante is heavy although nothing follows it, and why saṅgho is written with ṅ and not n.

    3. Sabbapāpassa akaraṇaṁ, kusalassa upasampadā; (Dhp 183 — Dhp p. 27)

      sab·ba·pā·pas·sa a·ka·ra·ṇaṁ, ku·sa·las·sa u·pa·sam·pa·dā

      sabbapāpassaof all eviln gen sgakaraṇaṁthe not-doingn nom sgkusalassaof the wholesomen gen sgupasampadāthe taking upf nom sg

      “Not doing any evil; taking up what is wholesome.”

      Say it aloud. Two syllables in this line are heavy for a reason that is not a long vowel and is not a doubled consonant. Find them, and say what makes each heavy.

    Vacasā paricitā — script and pronunciation

    Recite until the line is fluent. Then close the book and write it out, and only then check what you wrote against the printed line, letter by letter. Count your own errors instead of correcting them silently: the count is the exercise.

    1. Itipi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā. (AN 6.10 — A iii 285)

      “Such is the Blessed One: perfected, fully awakened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit to be trained, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.”

      Recite it, then write it. Check every long vowel and every doubled consonant. Then say which of your errors you could have heard, had you been reciting properly, and which you could not.

    2. Ayaṁ itthannāmo itthannāmassa āyasmato upasampadāpekkho. (Kd 1 — Vin i 95)

      ayaṁthisitthannāmoso-and-so, one of such-and-such a namem nom sgitthannāmassa āyasmatoof Venerable so-and-som gen sgupasampadāpekkhoseeking full ordinationm nom sg

      “This person, so-and-so, is seeking full ordination under Venerable so-and-so.”

      Recite it, then write it from memory. This line has more traps in it than any other in the set, and they are all traps of the pen, not of the ear. When you have checked it, say how many doubled consonants and how many long vowels it holds.

    Manasānupekkhitā — script and pronunciation

    Cover the chapter. These are answered from memory or not at all.

    How many letters? For each word, say how many Pāli letters it has, and name any digraph.

    1. dukkha
    2. amhi
    3. saṅgha
    4. daḷha
    5. sammāsambuddho

    Long or short? For each form, say whether the marked vowel is long or short, and why.

    1. the e of bhante
    2. the e of mettā
    3. the o of soṇḍo
    4. the a of arahaṁ

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — script and pronunciation

    Split and weigh. Divide each line into syllables, mark every heavy syllable, and say what makes each heavy one heavy. Nothing is printed for you here.

    1. Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; (Dhp 1 — Dhp p. 1)
    2. …poriyā vācāya samannāgato vissaṭṭhāya anelagalāya atthassa viññāpaniyā… (DN 4 — D i 114)
    3. Yassa nakkhamati, so bhāseyya. (Kd 1 — Vin i 95) — Divide and weigh it. Then two questions the line answers only if you say it: what is nakkhamati made of, and what happens to the e of bhāseyya? Say whether that second answer changes the weight of the syllable it is in.

    Where the rules do not settle it.

    1. Yassāyasmato khamati … so tuṇhassa; (Kd 1 — Vin i 95) — Take only the last two words. Divide tuṇhassa, and say how many letters it has. Then say what the chapter’s list of undecidable clusters has to do with your answer, and whether anything here forces you to choose.

    Put the diacritics back. Producing the marks is harder than reading them, and here the sitting-still work of this chapter shows.

    1. Three words, stripped of everything above and below the line. Restore them, and then say which one you could not have restored from the pronunciation alone, and why.

      1. sammasambuddhassa

      2. sangho

      3. anelagalaya

    Coming back — script and pronunciation

    Day 3. Items 1–3 aloud, and 11–14. Vowel length is the first thing to go, and it goes unnoticed.

    Day 10. Items 6–10 from memory, and item 15.

    Day 30. Items 4 and 19. If the recollection still comes out with its long vowels intact, the chapter has done its work.
