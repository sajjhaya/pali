    Exercises: Nouns

    The chapter’s argument was that the declensions are fewer than they look, because masculine and neuter differ only in three cases and everything else is variation on three paradigms. These exercises test that claim from the reader’s side, which is the side that matters: given a form, how much can you actually tell?

    The third stage is the point of the set. A great many Pāli endings are ambiguous, and knowing which ambiguity you are facing is a more useful skill than knowing a paradigm by heart, because it shows you what to go looking for in the rest of the sentence. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — nouns

    Nothing is withheld here. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to see what is in front of you.

    1. Attā hi attano nātho. (Dhp 160 — Dhp p. 24)

      attāself, oneselfm nom sghiindeedattanoof oneselfm gen sgnāthoprotectorm nom sg

      “Oneself is indeed one’s own protector.”

      Read it aloud twice. Say which vowel is long and which consonant is doubled, and hold both when you say it again. Then give the stem, the stem class and the case of attano.

    2. Punappunaṁ vaccho upeti mātaraṁ. (SN 7.12 — S i 174)

      punappunaṁagain and againvacchocalfm nom sgupetigoes to, approachesmātaraṁmotherf acc sg

      “Again and again the calf goes to its mother.”

      Aloud, holding the doubled pp. Then: what stem class is mātaraṁ, and what is the nominative singular of that word?

    3. Te taṁ rattiṁ tikkhattuṁ paccuṭṭhāya pañjalikā aggiṁ namassanti. (AN 10.119 — A v 234)

      tetheytaṁ rattiṁthat nightf acc sg, adverbialtikkhattuṁthree timespaccuṭṭhāyahaving risenabspañjalikāwith palms joinedm nom plaggiṁfirem acc sgnamassantithey worship

      “Rising three times that night, with joined palms, they worship the fire.”

      Aloud. Two i-stems stand in this line, rattiṁ and aggiṁ, and they are both accusative singular. One is feminine and one is masculine. Say what shows you which is which, and be careful, because it is not the ending.

    4. Mātā yathā niyaṁ puttam āyusā ekaputtamanurakkhe. (Snp 1.8 — Sn p. 26)

      mātāmotherf nom sgyathāas, just asniyaṁownputtamsonm acc sgāyusāwith her lifeins sgekaputtamonly sonm acc sganurakkhewould guardopt

      “As a mother would guard with her life her own son, her only son.”

      Aloud, slowly; this is a line worth having by heart. Mātā here and mātaraṁ in item 2 are the same word. Give the case of each. Then say why a search of the printed edition for the word āyusā on its own finds nothing, although the form is here.

    Vacasā paricitā — nouns

    Recite each line until you can say it without looking. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Rebuilding is the work; recognising is not.

    1. Idha pana, upavāṇa, bhikkhu cakkhunā rūpaṁ disvā rūpappaṭisaṁvedī ca hoti. (SN 35.70 — S iv 41)

      idhaherepanamoreoverupavāṇaUpavāṇam voc sgbhikkhua monkm nom sgcakkhunāwith the eyen ins sgrūpaṁform, sightn acc sgdisvāhaving seenabsrūpappaṭisaṁvedīone who experiences the formm nom sgcaandhotiis

    2. Nibbindaṁ virajjati; virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṁ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṁ hoti. (SN 22.59 — S iii 68)

      nibbindaṁbeing disillusionedvirajjatidesire fadesvirāgāthrough the fading of desirem abl sgvimuccatihe is freedvimuttasmiṁwhen he is freedm loc sgvimuttamiti“freed”ñāṇaṁthe knowledgen nom sghoticomes to be

      When you can say it, name the case of virāgā and of vimuttasmiṁ without looking at the gloss, and say what each contributes.

    3. … pubbe ananussutesu dhammesu cakkhuṁ udapādi, ñāṇaṁ udapādi, paññā udapādi, vijjā udapādi, āloko udapādi. (SN 56.11 — S v 422)

      pubbebeforeananussutesu dhammesuregarding teachings not heard beforem loc plcakkhuṁvisionn nom sgudapādiaroseñāṇaṁknowledgen nom sgpaññāwisdomf nom sgvijjārealisationf nom sgālokolightm nom sg

      Five nouns arise in a row, and they are not all of one gender. Rebuild the line from the gloss; then say which are feminine, which neuter and which masculine, and how much of that you could have told from the endings alone.

    Manasānupekkhitā — nouns

    Cover the chapter and the paradigms. These are answered from memory or not at all.

    Identify the form. For each, give the stem and its class, the gender, and the case and number.

    1. bhikkhūnaṁ
    2. raññā
    3. satthāraṁ
    4. cakkhunā
    5. manasi
    6. bhagavato

    Say what the ending does not tell you. Each of these is ambiguous. Give every case and number it could be, and then say what you would look for in a sentence to settle it.

    1. purisā
    2. kaññāya
    3. rūpāni
    4. aggino

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — nouns

    Read the line. Give a word-by-word gloss and an idiomatic rendering, and name the stem class of every noun in it.

    1. Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; (Dhp 1 — Dhp p. 1)
    2. Attanā hi kataṁ pāpaṁ, attanā saṅkilissati. (Dhp 165 — Dhp p. 24)
    3. Ārocemi kho te, mahārāja, paṭivedemi kho te, mahārāja, adhivattati kho taṁ, mahārāja, jarāmaraṇaṁ. (SN 3.25 — S i 101)

    One stem, two cases, one sentence. The Vinaya’s procedure for relinquishing a robe opens by telling the monk what to do, step by step.

    1. Tena bhikkhunā saṅghaṁ upasaṅkamitvā ekaṁsaṁ uttarāsaṅgaṁ karitvā vuḍḍhānaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ pāde vanditvā ukkuṭikaṁ nisīditvā añjaliṁ paggahetvā evamassa vacanīyo. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196) — Gloss it. Bhikkhu appears twice in different cases and numbers: give both, and say what each one’s role is. Then say what declension pāde belongs to and why it cannot be a locative here.

    Put it back into Pāli. Producing a form is harder than recognising one, and it is the only way to find out which of the two you can do. Each phrase below occurs in a line already in this set. Give the Pāli from memory, with the case and number of every noun in it, and only then look back at the line it came from.

    1. Four phrases, one line each:

      1. “one’s own protector” — Dhp 160 — Dhp p. 24

      2. “with the eye” — SN 35.70 — S iv 41

      3. “with her life” — Snp 1.8 — Sn p. 26

      4. “at the feet of the senior monks” — Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196

    Coming back — nouns

    The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

    Day 3. Items 1–4 aloud, and 14–17; the ambiguities are the first thing to go.

    Day 10. Items 8–13 with the paradigms covered, and item 18.

    Day 30. Items 21 and 22. If 22 still comes out, the paradigms have held.
