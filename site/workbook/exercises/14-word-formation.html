    Exercise · Chapter 14

    Exercises: Word Formation

    This chapter’s claim is narrow and testable: knowing how a word was built shows you where to look it up, and very little else. So the set drills recognition, not analysis. Half of it is the same operation performed forwards and backwards (given the derivative, find the base; given the base, predict the derivative), and the two halves are deliberately unequal in difficulty, because in reading you only ever do the first.

    Stage 1 is five passages in which the derivatives are already resolved for you, and four of the five are the Maṅgala Sutta, which many readers of this book will have by heart before they have any grammar. That is the point of choosing them: a line already in the memory has more in it than the thing you came for. Five of the later items contain a trap. They are not marked. Where you find one, say what the trap is as well as what the answer is. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — word formation

    Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear what the suffixes have done to the front of the word.

    1. Sāmaññañca vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi, sāmaññaphalāni ca. Taṁ suṇātha. Katamañca, bhikkhave, sāmaññaṁ? (SN 45.35 — S v 25)

      sāmaññaṁ< samaṇa ascetic + -ya — the ascetic life n acc sgcaandvoto youbhikkhavemonksdesessāmiI will teach1 sg futsāmaññaphalānithe fruits of the ascetic lifen acc plcaandtaṁthatsuṇāthalisten2 pl impkatamaṁwhich, what

      “Monks, I will teach you the ascetic life and the fruits of the ascetic life. Listen to this. And what, monks, is the ascetic life?”

      Say it aloud. The same derivative stands three times in three lines, once inside a compound, and the whole of this chapter’s practical claim is in its first syllable. Name the base, name the suffix, and say which letter of the dictionary the word is filed under and which letter its base is filed under. Then look sāmañña up and read past the first entry. It has two bases, not one, and they are different words. Say what the second is built on, and what in this sentence settles which of the two is meant.

    2. Asevanā ca bālānaṁ, paṇḍitānañca sevanā; Pūjā ca pūjaneyyānaṁ, etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ. (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 46)

      asevanā< a- not + √sev + -anā — non-association f nom sgcaandbālānaṁwith foolsm gen plpaṇḍitānaṁwith the wisem gen plcaandsevanāassociationpūjā< √pūj + -ā — honouring f nom sgcaandpūjaneyyānaṁof those to be honouredm gen pletaṁthismaṅgalamuttamaṁ< maṅgalaṁ blessing + uttamaṁ highest

      “Not associating with fools, and associating with the wise, and honouring those worthy of honour: this is the highest blessing.”

      Say it aloud, and hear that the verse has no verb in it. Three formations in four lines, and one of them is used twice. Name the three, and say what the verse gains by making its opposition out of one noun and a particle instead of two nouns.

    3. Patirūpadesavāso ca, pubbe ca katapuññatā; Attasammāpaṇidhi ca, etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ. (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 46)

      patirūpadesavāso< patirūpa suitable + desa region + vāsa dwelling — living in a suitable region m nom sgcaandpubbein the pastcaandkatapuññatā< kata done + puñña merit + -tā — the fact of having made merit f nom sgattasammāpaṇidhi< atta self + sammā rightly + paṇidhi aiming — right self-direction m nom sgcaandetaṁthismaṅgalamuttamaṁ< maṅgalaṁ blessing + uttamaṁ highest

      “Living in a suitable region, having made merit in the past, aiming oneself rightly: this is the highest blessing.”

      Say it aloud. This is the verse between the two the chapter prints, and it carries the one suffix they do not. Say what -tā has been added to here (something larger than a stem), and name the part of speech of that base. Then put item 1’s question to it: the front of the word has not been strengthened, and -tā is a secondary suffix, so why not? A later item asks the same thing of a different suffix.

    4. Bāhusaccañca sippañca, vinayo ca susikkhito; (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 47)

      bāhusaccaṁ< bahu much + suta heard + -ya — learning n nom sgcaandsippaṁcraftcaandvinayodisciplinecaandsusikkhito< su- well + sikkhita trained

      “Learning and a craft, and discipline well trained in.”

      Say it aloud, slowly, and hold the -cc-. One word here is the chapter entire. Take it apart in four steps (base, suffix, vuddhi, assimilation), and then say how much of the base is still visible in the result. Item 31 will ask you to produce this line from the English, so learn it now.

    5. Mātāpitu upaṭṭhānaṁ, puttadārassa saṅgaho; (Snp 2.4 — Sn p. 47)

      mātāpitumother and father (dvanda)upaṭṭhānaṁ< upa- near + √ṭhā + -ana — attending on n nom sgputtadārassaof children and spousem gen sgsaṅgaho< saṁ- together + √gah + -a — support m nom sg

      “Attending on mother and father, supporting children and spouse.”

      Say it aloud. Two action nouns, two different suffixes, and both prefixes have to be undone before either word can be looked up. Say what each prefix is in its dictionary shape, and which of the two changed on the way in and why.

    Vacasā paricitā — word formation

    Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud.

    1. svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opaneyyiko paccattaṁ veditabbo viññūhī’ti. (AN 6.10 — A iii 285)

      svākkhātowell proclaimedbhagavatāby the Blessed Onem ins sgdhammothe teachingm nom sgsandiṭṭhikovisible here and nowakālikotimelessehipassiko< ehi come! + passa see! + -ikaopaneyyikoleading onwardspaccattaṁindividuallyveditabboto be knownviññūhiby the wiseti(quotation)

      “The teaching is well proclaimed by the Blessed One — visible here and now, timeless, inviting one to come and see, leading onwards, to be known individually by the wise.”

      Set 13 asked what is underneath these four words; this set asks only where to find them. Recite the line until it is fluent, rebuild it from the gloss, and then take the four words in -iko to a dictionary: for each, say which letter you look under, and whether that is the letter its base begins with. Only one of the four moves, and the chapter uses it to demonstrate the rule. Then say, for each of the other three, why the rule left it alone. The reasons are three, and all different.

    2. ’Idaṁ dukkhaṁ ariyasaccan’ti me, bhikkhave, pubbe ananussutesu dhammesu cakkhuṁ udapādi, ñāṇaṁ udapādi, paññā udapādi, vijjā udapādi, āloko udapādi. (SN 56.11 — S v 422)

      idaṁthisdukkhaṁ ariyasaccaṁthe noble truth of sufferingtithus (quotation)meto mebhikkhavemonkspubbeformerlyananussutesu< an- not + anu- along + √su + -ta — unheard m loc pldhammesuamong thingscakkhuṁvisionudapādi< ud- up + augment a- + pādi — arose 3 sg aorñāṇaṁ< √ñā + -ana — knowledgepaññā< pa- + √ñā + -ā — wisdomvijjāclear knowledgeāloko< ā- + √lok + -a — light

      “‘This is the noble truth of suffering’: thus, monks, in regard to things unheard before, vision arose in me, knowledge arose, wisdom arose, clear knowledge arose, light arose.”

      Rebuild it from the gloss, and name each derivative as you rebuild it. Two of the five nouns are built on one root, and they are two of the most doctrinally loaded words in the canon. Say which two, and what that pair demonstrates about deducing a meaning from a derivation. Then say where the augment sits in udapādi, and why it is not at the front of the word.

    3. “Cattārome, bhikkhave, iddhipādā bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā honti mahānisaṁsā”. (SN 51.20 — S v 276)

      cattārofourimethesebhikkhavemonksiddhipādābases of spiritual powerm nom plbhāvitādevelopedpp m nom plbahulīkatā< bahula much + -ī- + kata done — cultivatedmahapphalāof great fruithontiaremahānisaṁsāof great benefit

      “These four bases of spiritual power, monks, when developed and cultivated, are of great fruit, of great benefit.”

      Rebuild it from the gloss. This formula closes the description of every practice the Buddha teaches, so it is one to have by heart. One word in it belongs to the chapter’s section on verbs made from nouns and adverbs and to no other. Name it, name the formation, and say what the -ī- in the middle contributes. Then say why it is neither an ordinary compound nor a denominative verb.

    Manasānupekkhitā — word formation

    Recall. Answer from memory, in a phrase, before opening the chapter.

    1. The tradition divides derivation in two. Name both divisions, and say what each kind of suffix is added to.

    2. In secondary derivation, which syllable of the word gets strengthened? Give the practical consequence for somebody holding a dictionary.

    3. The tradition names three things a prefix can do to the meaning of a root. Give all three in English. Which of the three does a reader most need to be told about?

    4. A grammar writes a suffix as ṇa or ṇvu or ṇika. The ṇ is never pronounced. What is it doing there?

    5. Give two distinct reasons why knowing a word’s derivation will not tell you what the word means.

    Identify the formation. For each, name the suffix, say whether it is primary or secondary, and give the sense the suffix contributes.

    1. vedanā

    2. khanti

    3. kāraka

    4. cakkhumā

    5. sāmaññaṁ

    6. ācariyaka

    7. muṇḍako

    8. nepuññaṁ

    9. pothujjaniko

    10. pāragū

    11. patti

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — word formation

    Build the form. Produce the answer before checking it.

    1. From paṇḍito ‘wise’, build the neuter abstract in -ya. Name the two separate changes that stand between the base and the answer.

    2. From garu ‘heavy, venerable’, build the masculine noun ‘respect’. Which grade is the root in?

    3. From vicikicchā ‘doubt’, build the possessive adjective in -in, nominative singular. Compare your answer with what the canon has before accepting it, and say what the result shows about the rule in item 10.

    4. Warder’s lesson on derivation gives opanayika for ‘leading onwards’. Look the word up in the canon and say what is printed there instead, and what element in the formation accounts for the difference.

    5. From punabbhavo ‘renewed existence’, build the adjective ‘leading to renewed existence’. Then say which of the word’s two vowels changed and why the other did not.

    Read the sentence. Give the three layers: the line as printed, a word-by-word gloss with every derivative resolved, and your own rendering.

    1. “Yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, santiṭṭhāmi tadāssu saṁsīdāmi; yadākhvāhaṁ, āvuso, āyūhāmi tadāssu nibbuyhāmi. (SN 1.1 — S i 1)

    Put it back into Pāli. The answer is a particular line of canon, and getting close to it is the exercise. A well-formed sentence that is not the one in the book is a good result, but it is a different exercise.

    1. ‘Learning and a craft, and discipline well trained in.’ Two of the three nouns are derivatives; one of them is an abstract in -ya built on a compound.

    2. ‘When I stood still I sank down; when I struggled I was swept away.’ Four verbs, each carrying a prefix, and the two pairs are opposites.

    Read at length.

    1. Read SN 1.1, the whole sutta, twice through without stopping to parse anything. It is one of the shortest suttas in the canon, and the first in the Saṁyutta. This is not a drill and should not be treated as one; read it as you would read a paragraph in a language you know. Only then answer:
    • Abhikkantāya rattiyā abhikkantavaṇṇā has one prefix used twice, on two different words. What is the prefix contributing in each?
    • Upasaṅkami carries two prefixes and upasaṅkamitvā carries the same two. Name them, in order, and say what the root is.
    • The Buddha’s explanation turns on two pairs of verbs, and his conclusion restates two of those roots in negated form. Identify the four verbs and the two negated forms, and name the prefix on each.
    • The sutta ends tatthevantaradhāyīti. Resolve the sandhi and identify the verb. Is antara a prefix?

    Reaching back.

    1. Chapter 13. Ehipassiko was taught in the compounds chapter as a syntactic compound and appears in this one as an -ika derivative. Both are right. Say in one sentence why there is no contradiction, and then say which of the two chapters explains the -o on the end.

    2. Chapter 4. -tara and -tama are secondary suffixes and are taught with the adjectives, not here. Uttaritaro and paṇītataro both occur in a single sentence at DN 6. Which of the two is built on an adjective and which on a prefix, and does the difference show?

    3. Chapter 10. Diṭṭhi and diṭṭha are both built on √dis. One is a past participle and one is an action noun in -ti. Given only the two forms and no context, what shows you which is which, and what does that suggest about reading a bare -ti form in a verse?

    4. Chapter 2. Nibbāna, nibbindati and nikkhamati all begin with the same prefix. Say what the prefix is, and why the first two double a b while the third doubles a kh. Chapter 2 gives the general process; this chapter gives the reason the prefix has a consonant to contribute at all.

    Say it aloud.

    1. Read this verse aloud five times, slowly, holding the long vowels and sounding the double consonants. Then find the three derived words in it and name the suffix of each.

      Maggānaṭṭhaṅgiko seṭṭho,
      saccānaṁ caturo padā;
      Virāgo seṭṭho dhammānaṁ,
      dvipadānañca cakkhumā. (Dhp 273 — Dhp p. 39)

    One root, three formations. A single Vinaya rule uses the same root as a verb, as an action noun and as a past participle, and names the whole class of offence after one of them.

    1. Nissajjitabbaṁ saṅghassa vā gaṇassa vā puggalassa vā. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196) — Gloss it. Then account for the formation of nissajjitabbaṁ, and of nissaggiyaṁ and nissaṭṭhaṁ, which the same rule uses a few lines later. All three are built on one root; say what each suffix does and what part of speech each result is.

    Coming back — word formation

    The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

    Day 3. Items 14–24. Recognising a suffix is the only thing in this chapter a reader does at speed, and it is the first thing to go. If the eleven come back without hesitation, the chapter has done its work.

    Day 10. Items 25–29, and item 39. Building a form is the operation run backwards, and it is harder than it looks because nothing in reading ever asks for it.

    Day 30. Items 1, 3 and 4 aloud, and item 33. If sāmaññaṁ, katapuññatā and bāhusaccaṁ still take you to the right letter of the dictionary without thinking, the front vuddhi has stopped being a rule and become a reflex, which is all this chapter wanted.
