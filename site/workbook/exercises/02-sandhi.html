    Exercise · Chapter 2

    Exercises: Sandhi

    This chapter’s skill is taking a written form apart, so most of what follows runs backwards from the printed word to its pieces. That is the direction a reader actually needs, and it is the harder one: forming a junction is a rule applied, undoing one is a hypothesis tested.

    Two working cautions apply throughout. A division is only right if both pieces are words that occur elsewhere, the test the chapter draws from Geiger’s phantom glide. And not every adjacent pair of vowels has been through anything, so “no junction here” is a legitimate answer and one of the items below wants it.

    Every Pāli line is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — sandhi

    Sandhi happens in the mouth before it happens on the page, so this stage is entirely aloud. Each item gives the joined form, its pieces and its meaning, and asks only that you say both and hear what the join is for. Say the pieces slowly, then say them at speaking speed. The junction is what speaking speed does.

    The first three are the canon doing this exercise on itself: the Vinaya’s word-analysis quotes a rule’s word as the rule prints it, joined, and then takes it apart.

    1. Yo panāti yo yādiso … Eso vuccati “yo panā”ti. (Bu Pj 1 — Vin iii 23)

      yo panāti< yo whoever + pana moreover + iti (the quotative)yādisoof whatever kindesothisvuccatiis called

      “‘Whoever’: whoever, of whatever kind … This is what is called ‘whoever’.”

      Say yo pana iti, slowly. Then say it at speed. Where did the long ā come from, and which of the two words does it belong to?

    2. Bhikkhūti bhikkhakoti bhikkhu, (Bu Pj 1 — Vin iii 24)

      bhikkhūti< bhikkhu a monk + itibhikkhakoti< bhikkhako an almsman, a beggar + itibhikkhua monkm nom sg

      “‘A monk’: he is a monk because he lives on alms.”

      The same quotative twice in five words, on two different vowels. Say each pair apart and then joined. One vowel lengthens and one does not. Say which, and then say why not.

    3. “Parimaṇḍalaṁ nivāsessāmīti sikkhā karaṇīyā”ti. (Bu Sk 1 — Vin iv 185)

      parimaṇḍalaṁevenly all roundnivāsessāmīti< nivāsessāmi I shall dress + itisikkhāthe trainingkaraṇīyāto be done

      “‘I shall dress evenly all round’: this is the training to be done.”

      A third vowel before the same quotative. Say nivāsessāmi iti and then the joined form. Then look up nivāsessāmi in your memory of the last three items and say what the three have in common.

    4. Esevanto dukkhassā. (SN 12.51 — S ii 84)

      esevanto< eso this + eva just + anto end m nom sgdukkhassāof sufferingn gen sg

      “Just this is the end of suffering.”

      Three words inside one. Say eso eva anto slowly, then quickly, until you hear it arrive at esevanto on its own. Then say how many vowels were lost and how many survived.

    Vacasā paricitā — sandhi

    Recite until the line is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud, junctions and all. Rebuilding is the work.

    1. …yena mithilā yena brahmāyu brāhmaṇo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā brahmāyuṁ brāhmaṇaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. (MN 91 — M ii 135)

      yenaby whichm ins sgmithilāMithilābrahmāyuBrahmāyubrāhmaṇothe brahminm nom sgtenupasaṅkami< tena by that + upasaṅkami he approachedupasaṅkamitvāhaving approachedbrahmāyuṁBrahmāyuaccbrāhmaṇaṁthe brahminaccabhivādetvāhaving bowedekamantaṁto one sidenisīdihe sat down

      “…he went to Mithilā, to the brahmin Brahmāyu; and having approached, he bowed to the brahmin Brahmāyu and sat down to one side.”

      This is the stock narrative formula for one person going to another, and you will meet it hundreds of times. Recite it until it is automatic, because the reading time it saves later is enormous.

    2. Pātimokkhanti ādimetaṁ mukhametaṁ pamukhametaṁ kusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ. Tena vuccati pātimokkhanti. (Kd 2 — Vin i 103)

      pātimokkhanti< pātimokkhaṁ the Pātimokkha + itiādimetaṁ< ādiṁ beginning acc + etaṁ thismukhametaṁ< mukhaṁ face, foremost + etaṁpamukhametaṁ< pamukhaṁ chief + etaṁkusalānaṁ dhammānaṁof wholesome statesm gen pl

      “‘Pātimokkha’: this is the beginning, this is the face, this is the fore of wholesome states. That is why it is called the Pātimokkha.”

      Five junctions, and one niggahīta in every one of them, so this line is worth saying aloud, not just reading. Rebuild it from the gloss; then say which of the five comes out differently from the other four, and what decided it.

    Manasānupekkhitā — sandhi

    Cover the chapter. These are answered from memory or not at all.

    Name the junction. For each form, say which of the four cases it is: vowel + vowel, vowel + consonant, niggahīta + vowel, or niggahīta + consonant.

    1. natthi
    2. ahampi
    3. tañca
    4. nappajānāti

    Undo the junction. Give the two words or elements that produced each form, and name what happened.

    1. tenupasaṅkami
    2. sveva
    3. etadavoca
    4. appekacce
    5. yāvadeva
    6. evañhi

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — sandhi

    Read the line. Give a word-by-word gloss and an idiomatic rendering, and account for every junction, including any place where two vowels meet and nothing has happened.

    1. Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; (Dhp 1 — Dhp p. 1)

    Undo four junctions in one line. The Vinaya’s rule against theft quotes what a king says to a thief he has caught. It is four sentences and the editions print them as four words.

    1. Corosi bālosi mūḷhosi thenosīti. (Bu Pj 2 — Vin iii 47) — Give the words that produced each of the four, name what happened at each join, and say why the last of the four is longer than the other three.

    Make the junction. Every item so far has run from the page to the pieces. Now run it the other way, which is harder, and which is the only way to find out whether you know the rule or merely recognise its results.

    1. Join each of these into the single form the canon actually prints, and name the change at each seam. Say the answer aloud before you write it.

      1. tena + upasaṅkami

      2. eso + eva + anto

      3. coro + asi

      4. nivāsessāmi + iti

    Where the test does not reach.

    1. The chapter’s test for a proposed division is that both pieces must be words that occur elsewhere. Apply it to the Vinaya’s resolution in item 1, yo panā + ti, and report what it returns for panā. Then say what the result settles, and what it does not, and why the chapter’s footnote calls this a qualification of the test, not an exception to it.

    Coming back — sandhi

    Day 3. Items 1–4 aloud again. The quotative is the junction you will meet most and the one most easily unlearned.

    Day 10. Items 7–16 from memory, and item 19, which is the same material produced instead of parsed.

    Day 30. Items 5 and 19. If the narrative formula in 5 still comes out whole, you have the sentence that opens a third of the canon.
