    Exercises: Numerals

    The pronoun chapter’s exercises began with isolated forms because its thesis was that a pronoun must be identified, not merely recognised. This chapter’s thesis is different: the forms are the easy half, and what has to be worked out is how a numeral attaches to what it counts. So the third stage here matters most, and the first two exist to make it possible.

    The first stage is arranged around the four constructions, not around the numbers, because a reader has to be able to hear them. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — numerals

    Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear what the numeral is attached to.

    1. Ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. (SN 10.9 — S i 212)

      ekaṁone, a certainm acc sgsamayaṁoccasion, timem acc sgbhagavāthe Blessed Onem nom sgrājagaheat Rājagahan loc sgviharatidwellsveḷuvanein the Bamboo Groven loc sgkalandakanivāpeat the squirrels’ feeding groundm loc sg

      “At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha, in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels’ feeding ground.”

      This is the numeral you have heard most often and have probably never counted with. Say it aloud. Eka here agrees with samayaṁ as an adjective does, and it is not really counting anything; it does what English does with “a”. Then say which of the four constructions this is, and whether you would have called it a numeral at all had it not been printed under this heading.

    2. Tena kho pana samayena aññatitthiyā paribbājakā cātuddase pannarase aṭṭhamiyā ca pakkhassa sannipatitvā dhammaṁ bhāsanti. (Kd 2 — Vin i 101)

      tena kho pana samayenanow at that timeaññatitthiyāof other sectsm nom plparibbājakāwanderersm nom plcātuddaseon the fourteenthm loc sgpannaraseon the fifteenthloc sg, used as an adverbaṭṭhamiyāon the eighthf loc sgcaandpakkhassaof the fortnightm gen sgsannipatitvāhaving assembledabsdhammaṁthe teachingm acc sgbhāsantithey speak

      “Now at that time wanderers of other sects would assemble on the fourteenth, the fifteenth and the eighth of the fortnight and speak on the teaching.”

      The Buddha’s fortnightly observance was instituted in answer to this passage, so here the uposatha days enter the canon. Say it aloud. Three ordinals in a row, and they are not all of one gender. Say which one is the odd one out, and hold the answer until item 15 asks you for it again.

    3. Abhivādanasīlissa, niccaṁ vuḍḍhāpacāyino; Cattāro dhammā vaḍḍhanti, āyu vaṇṇo sukhaṁ balaṁ. (Dhp 109 — Dhp p. 16)

      abhivādanasīlissafor one whose habit is to bowm gen sgniccaṁconstantlyvuḍḍhāpacāyinohonouring eldersm gen sgcattārofourm nom pldhammāthingsm nom plvaḍḍhantiincreaseāyulong lifevaṇṇobeautysukhaṁhappinessbalaṁstrength

      “For one in the habit of bowing, always honouring the elders, four things increase: long life, beauty, happiness and strength.”

      Say it aloud, and then say the four things without looking. The numeral and its noun share case, number and gender, which is the construction most readers assume is the only one. Item 4 is what happens when it is not.

    4. Tasmiṁ sahassadhā loke sahassaṁ candānaṁ sahassaṁ sūriyānaṁ … cattāri mahāsamuddasahassāni … sahassaṁ brahmalokānaṁ. (AN 10.29 — A v 59)

      tasmiṁin thatm loc sgsahassadhāthousandfoldlokeworldm loc sgsahassaṁa thousandn nom sgcandānaṁof moonsm gen plsūriyānaṁof sunsm gen plcattārifourn nom plmahāsamuddasahassānithousands of great oceansn nom plbrahmalokānaṁof realms of divinitym gen pl

      “In that thousandfold world there are a thousand moons, a thousand suns … four thousand great oceans … a thousand realms of divinity.”

      The full passage says sahassaṁ fourteen times, and it is a list built to be recited. Say it. Nothing here agrees with anything: sahassaṁ is a neuter noun and what it counts stands in the genitive plural beside it, so the phrase is literally “a thousand of moons”. Then find the place in the line where the construction changes, and say what changed and why it had to.

    Vacasā paricitā — numerals

    Recite each until it is fluent. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing a numeral compound is the work; recognising one is not.

    1. Niṭṭhitacīvarasmiṁ bhikkhunā ubbhatasmiṁ kathine dasāhaparamaṁ atirekacīvaraṁ dhāretabbaṁ. Taṁ atikkāmayato nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan’ti. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196)

      niṭṭhitacīvarasmiṁwhen the robe is finishedn loc sgbhikkhunāby a monkm ins sgubbhatasmiṁwhen it has been withdrawnn loc sg, ppkathinethe kathinan loc sgdasāhaparamaṁfor ten days at mostadv, acc sgatirekacīvaraṁa surplus roben acc sgdhāretabbaṁmay be keptn nom sg, gerundivetaṁthatn acc sgatikkāmayatofor one exceeding itm gen sg, pres partnissaggiyaṁentailing relinquishmentpācittiyaṁan offence entailing confessionti(quotation)

      “When a monk’s robe is finished and the kathina has been withdrawn, a surplus robe may be kept for ten days at most. For one exceeding that: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession.”

      A rule recited fortnightly by every community of monks, and the whole of it turns on one word. Rebuild it from the gloss, and expect dasāhaparamaṁ to be the word that will not come. Then say how many parts that word has, and what case it is in. Having answered that, say why a word in that case is being translated as “for ten days at most” and not as the object of anything.

    2. Māse māse sahassena, yo yajetha sataṁ samaṁ; Ekañca bhāvitattānaṁ, muhuttamapi pūjaye; Sāyeva pūjanā seyyo, yañce vassasataṁ hutaṁ. (Dhp 106 — Dhp p. 16)

      māse māsemonth by monthm loc sg, repeatedsahassenawith a thousandn ins sgyowhoeveryajethashould sacrificeoptsataṁa hundredn acc sgsamaṁyearf acc sgekaṁonem acc sgcaandbhāvitattānaṁone who has developed himselfm acc sgmuhuttaṁfor a momentadv, acc sgapievenpūjayeshould honouroptsāthatevajustpūjanāhonouringf nom sgseyyobetteryaṁthan whichceifvassasataṁa hundred yearsn acc sghutaṁofferingn nom sg, pp

      “Though month after month for a hundred years one should sacrifice with a thousand, and another honour for a single moment one who has developed himself — that honouring is better than a hundred years of offerings.”

      Six numeral expressions in six lines, and no two of them work the same way. Rebuild it from the gloss and then set them out: which is a distributive, which a plain instrumental, which an accusative of duration, which an ordinary agreeing adjective, and which two are compounds. Item 16 takes the first two lines apart in detail; this stage asks only that the whole verse be in your mouth first.

    Manasānupekkhitā — numerals

    Cover the chapter. These are answered from memory or not at all.

    Identify the form. For each, give the numeral it belongs to, what class of numeral it is, and its case and number, or say why it has none.

    1. tiṇṇannaṁ
    2. ubhinnaṁ
    3. catutthī
    4. vīsatiyā
    5. tikkhattuṁ

    Say how the numeral joins its noun. Each of these uses one of the four constructions. Name it, and say what in the phrase shows which one it is.

    1. sahassaṁ candānaṁ (AN 10.29 — A v 59)
    2. saddhiṁ bhikkhusahassena (SN 35.28 — S iv 19)
    3. Cattāro dhammā vaḍḍhanti (Dhp 109 — Dhp p. 16)

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — numerals

    Read the verse. Give each a word-by-word gloss and then an idiomatic rendering, and account for every numeral in it.

    1. Yo sahassaṁ sahassena, saṅgāme mānuse jine; Ekañca jeyyamattānaṁ, sa ve saṅgāmajuttamo. (Dhp 103 — Dhp p. 15)
    2. Māse māse sahassena, yo yajetha sataṁ samaṁ; (Dhp 106 — Dhp p. 16)
    3. Sabbampi taṁ na catubhāgameti, Abhivādanā ujjugatesu seyyo. (Dhp 108 — Dhp p. 16)
    4. Appamādo kho, mahārāja, eko dhammo, yo ubho atthe samadhiggayha tiṭṭhati. (SN 3.17 — S i 86)

    The same three days, twice, in two genders. The chapter quotes the Bodhisatta naming the nights he chose to sit out in the forest.

    1. Cātuddasī pañcadasī aṭṭhamī ca pakkhassa. (MN 4 — M i 20) — Set this beside item 2 and give the gender and case of all six day-names across the two passages. Two of the three days are named twice in two different genders. Say which two, which gender each passage uses, and what that suggests about whether these words are ordinals or names. Then say why the third day behaves differently from the other two.

    Put it back into Pāli. Every item so far has run from a printed numeral to the construction behind it. Now run it the other way. Each phrase below occurs in a line already in this set. Give the Pāli from memory, naming the construction it uses and the case and number of every word in it, and only then look back at the line it came from.

    1. Four phrases, one line each:

      1. “at one time” — SN 10.9 — S i 212

      2. “four things increase” — Dhp 109 — Dhp p. 16

      3. “a thousand moons” — AN 10.29 — A v 59

      4. “for ten days at most” — Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 196

    Coming back — numerals

    The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

    Day 3. Items 1–4 aloud, and 12–14. The four constructions are the whole chapter, and they go before the forms do.

    Day 10. Items 7–11 from memory, and item 18.

    Day 30. Items 5, 6 and 20. If (c) and (d) in 20 still come out, the two constructions that do not agree with anything have stopped being exceptions.
