    Exercises: Gender and Adjectives

    An adjective has no endings of its own. Everything it does it borrows, and the whole skill this chapter asks for is reading that borrowing backwards: given an adjective, what does its ending tell you about the noun it belongs to? That is more useful than it sounds, because it is often the adjective, not the noun, that resolves an ambiguous phrase.

    Agreement is easier to hear than to work out, so the first stage gives whole lines with nothing withheld. The last stage takes up comparison, which the chapter treats as the second defining property of an adjective. Every Pāli line below is canonical and cited; none has been composed. Worked answers are at the back.

    Sotānugata — gender and adjectives

    Nothing is withheld. Each line comes with its gloss and its rendering, and the work is to say it and to hear one stem take different endings from the words around it.

    1. Dīghā jāgarato ratti, dīghaṁ santassa yojanaṁ; Dīgho bālānaṁ saṁsāro, saddhammaṁ avijānataṁ. (Dhp 60 — Dhp p. 9)

      dīghālongf nom sgjāgaratofor one lying awakem gen sg, pres partrattithe nightf nom sgdīghaṁlongn nom sgsantassafor the wearym gen sg, ppyojanaṁa leaguen nom sgdīgholongm nom sgbālānaṁfor foolsm gen plsaṁsārowandering onm nom sgsaddhammaṁthe true teachingm acc sgavijānataṁof those not understandingm gen pl, pres part

      “Long is the night for one lying awake; long is the league for the weary; long is the wandering on for fools who do not understand the true teaching.”

      Say it aloud, one pāda at a time. One adjective, three genders, three lines, and the stem never changes. Say the three forms of it in order without looking, and then say which noun gave each its ending.

    2. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, bālo abyatto akusalo sūdo rājānaṁ vā rājamahāmattaṁ vā nānaccayehi sūpehi paccupaṭṭhito assa. (SN 47.8 — S v 149)

      Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, paṇḍito byatto kusalo sūdo rājānaṁ vā rājamahāmattaṁ vā nānaccayehi sūpehi paccupaṭṭhito assa. (SN 47.8 — S v 151)

      seyyathāpisupposebhikkhavemendicantsm voc plbālofoolishabyattoincompetentakusalounskilfulpaṇḍitowisebyattocompetentkusaloskilfulsūdoa cookm nom sgrājānaṁa kingm acc sgvāorrājamahāmattaṁa royal ministerm acc sgnānaccayehiof various kindsm ins plsūpehiwith saucesm ins plpaccupaṭṭhitowaiting on, attendingm nom sg, ppassawere to be3 sg opt

      “Suppose, mendicants, a foolish, incompetent, unskilful cook were to wait on a king or a royal minister with sauces of various kinds.”

      “Suppose, mendicants, a wise, competent, skilful cook were to wait on a king or a royal minister with sauces of various kinds.”

      The same sentence twice, four sections of one sutta apart, with three adjectives turned round. Say both aloud. Six adjectives and one noun, and every one of the six carries the same three letters at the end. Then say what the second and the fifth have in common that the others do not.

    3. Tena kho pana samayena sukkā bhikkhunī mahatiyā parisāya parivutā dhammaṁ deseti. (SN 10.9 — S i 212)

      tena kho pana samayenanow at that timesukkāSukkāf nom sgbhikkhunīthe nunf nom sgmahatiyālargef ins sgparisāyaby an assemblyf ins sgparivutāsurroundedf nom sg, ppdhammaṁthe Dhammam acc sgdesetishe teaches

      “Now at that time the nun Sukkā was teaching the Dhamma surrounded by a large assembly.”

      Five feminines in a row, and they are not all in one case. Say it aloud, then say which two of the five are not nominative, and how you know; and say why parivutā, which stands at the far end of the sentence from the word it describes, is nominative and not instrumental.

    4. Yo pana bhikkhu atirekacīvaraṁ dhāreyya, nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan’ti. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 195)

      Yā pana bhikkhunī pattasannicayaṁ kareyya, nissaggiyaṁ pācittiyan’ti. (Bi Np 1 — Vin iv 243)

      yowhoeverm nom sgyāwhoeverf nom sgpanamoreoverbhikkhua monkm nom sgbhikkhunīa nunf nom sgatirekacīvaraṁa surplus roben acc sgdhāreyyashould keep3 sg optpattasannicayaṁa stash of bowlsm acc sgkareyyashould make3 sg optnissaggiyaṁentailing relinquishmentn nom sgpācittiyaṁan offence entailing confessionn nom sgti(quotation)

      “Whatever monk should keep a surplus robe: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession.”

      “Whatever nun should make a stash of bowls: an offence entailing relinquishment and confession.”

      The monks’ code and the nuns’ code, built on one frame. Say the two openings one after the other. Two words change and the rest does not. Say which two, and then say why the four words that close both rules did not have to move.

    Vacasā paricitā — gender and adjectives

    Recite each line until you can say it without looking. Then cover the Pāli and rebuild it from the gloss alone, aloud. Producing the endings is the work; recognising them is not.

    1. Tāvadeva chāyā metabbā, utuppamāṇaṁ ācikkhitabbaṁ, divasabhāgo ācikkhitabbo, saṅgīti ācikkhitabbā, cattāro nissayā ācikkhitabbā. (Kd 1 — Vin i 95)

      tāvadevastraightawaychāyāthe shadowf nom sgmetabbāis to be measuredf nom sgutuppamāṇaṁthe daten nom sgācikkhitabbaṁis to be toldn nom sgdivasabhāgothe part of the daym nom sgācikkhitabbom nom sgsaṅgītithe recitationf nom sgācikkhitabbāf nom sgcattāro nissayāthe four supportsm nom plācikkhitabbām nom pl

      “Straightaway the shadow is to be measured, the date told, the part of the day told, the recitation told, and the four supports told.”

      This is what a newly ordained monk is told in the first minutes after his ordination. One word takes four different endings in one sentence, and rebuilding it from the gloss means producing all four. Do that before reading on. Then say which two of the four are spelled alike, and what in the sentence (not in the word) decides between them.

    2. Sahassamapi ce vācā, anatthapadasaṁhitā; Ekaṁ atthapadaṁ seyyo, yaṁ sutvā upasammati. (Dhp 100 — Dhp p. 15)

      sahassaṁa thousandn nom sgapievenceifvācāwordsf nom planatthapadasaṁhitānot connected with a meaningful sayingf nom plekaṁonen nom sgatthapadaṁa meaningful sayingn nom sgseyyobetteryaṁwhichn acc sgsutvāhaving heardabsupasammatione is calmed

      “Though there be a thousand words unconnected with meaning, better is one meaningful saying, hearing which one is calmed.”

      Recite it, then rebuild it from the gloss. This is the chapter’s comparison table in a verse, and seyyo, alone of its forms, cannot be derived from anything: it has to be known. When the line is fluent, say what its positive degree is according to the chapter’s table of irregulars.

    Manasānupekkhitā — gender and adjectives

    Cover the chapter. These are answered from memory or not at all.

    Give the feminine. For each masculine stem, form the feminine and name the rule that produced it.

    1. dāsa ‘servant’
    2. dīgha ‘long’
    3. upāsaka ‘lay follower’
    4. hatthin ‘elephant’
    5. bhikkhu ‘mendicant’
    6. mahant ‘great’

    What does the adjective tell you about its noun? For each adjective, say every gender, case and number it could be carrying, and therefore what you know about the noun it agrees with.

    1. kusalā
    2. dukkhaṁ
    3. sīlavato
    4. mahatiyā

    Diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā — gender and adjectives

    Read the line. Give a word-by-word gloss and an idiomatic rendering, and account for every adjective: its stem, its agreement, and whether it is being compared.

    1. Maggānaṭṭhaṅgiko seṭṭho, saccānaṁ caturo padā. (Dhp 273 — Dhp p. 40)
    2. Atthi kho, bhikkhu, sakko nāma devānamindo amhehi abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro ca. (DN 11 — D i 216)
    3. Cakkhuṁ, bhikkhave, dukkhaṁ. … jivhā dukkhā … kāyo dukkho … mano dukkho. (SN 35.2 — S iv 2)

    Agreement across a long sentence. A formal act of the Sangha names a thing once and then keeps agreeing with it.

    1. Idaṁ cīvaraṁ itthannāmassa bhikkhuno nissaggiyaṁ āyasmantānaṁ nissaṭṭhaṁ. (Bu Np 1 — Vin iii 197) — Gloss it. Four words agree with one another and two do not. Say which are which, and what fixes the gender of the four.

    Put it back into Pāli. Every item so far has run from a printed ending to the noun behind it. Now run it the other way, which is harder, and which is where you find out whether you can form an agreement or only recognise one. Each phrase below occurs in a line already in this set. Give the Pāli from memory, naming the gender, case and number of every word in it, and only then look back at the line it came from.

    1. Four phrases, one line each:

      1. “long is the night” — Dhp 60 — Dhp p. 9

      2. “surrounded by a large assembly” — SN 10.9 — S i 212

      3. “the recitation is to be told” — Kd 1 — Vin i 95

      4. “whatever nun should make a stash of bowls” — Bi Np 1 — Vin iv 243

    Where the ending is not the word you think it is.

    1. Item 1 turns on santassa, and the chapter’s table of irregular comparatives has a sant in it. Look up both. Say whether they are the same word, what each is built from, and what would have gone wrong in the rendering of Dhp 60 had you taken the table’s sant to be the word in the verse. Then find the table’s word somewhere else in the same verse, where it is hiding inside a compound.

    Coming back — gender and adjectives

    The set is not finished on the day it is worked. Re-attempt these, from cold, without reading the answers first.

    Day 3. Items 1 and 5 aloud, and 7–12. The feminine suffixes are a table, and a table goes first.

    Day 10. Items 13–16 from memory, and item 20.

    Day 30. Items 21 and 22. If 21 still comes out with the feminines intact, the table in 7–12 has become something you can use, not just recite.
